Momentum

Momentum is mass × velocity, and in any collision or explosion the total momentum stays the same. The same idea explains why crumple zones, airbags, seat belts and helmets save lives. This is a Year 11 topic (taught in Year 11, for the GCSE in summer 2027), and every spec point in it is Physics-only.

01What actually comes up

Momentum came up in three of the ten real Edexcel papers, always on Paper 2P and always as a short, structured question near the start. The pattern is the same each time: a p = m × v calculation, then either conservation of momentum or force = change in momentum ÷ time, then a safety explanation.

PaperWhat was asked
2023 Edexcel 2P Q4 (7)Two birds, just before bird X catches bird Y: momentum of bird X, total momentum before, momentum after the catch, then the velocity of the two birds together
2022 Edexcel 2P Q2 (8)The unit of momentum (kg m/s); state the principle of conservation of momentum; two objects pushing apart from rest, find the velocity of one (−2.4 m/s); why an egg dropped on a carpet does not break
2022 Edexcel Jan 2P Q1(b) (5)Airbags: state momentum = mass × velocity; momentum of a 72 kg driver at 13 m/s; force = change in momentum ÷ time (3200 N); how an airbag protects the driver

Two patterns are worth noticing. The equation p = m × v is asked for on its own, as a free mark. And every safety question is marked on the same three steps: longer time, smaller rate of change of momentum, smaller force.

On the egg and carpet in 2022, the mark scheme credited the "idea of carpet increases impact time" and then "carpet reduces rate of change of momentum". Both are needed. "The carpet is soft" scores nothing on its own.

On the airbag in January 2022, the scheme rejected one option because "increasing the force does not protect the driver". The airbag does not change how much momentum the driver loses. It changes how long that takes, and so makes the force smaller.

02How it actually works

All of this module is Physics-only. It is small: one definition, one conservation law and one force equation, used in three kinds of question. The marks are lost on signs (momentum has a direction) and on vague wording in the safety explanations. Every heading below says where it was actually asked.

Momentum = mass × velocity

Spec 1.25P · 2022 Edexcel Jan 2P Q1(b) · 2023 Edexcel 2P Q4(a)

Momentum is mass multiplied by velocity: the heavier and faster something is, the more momentum it has.

p = m × v
p = momentum in kg m/s · m = mass in kilograms (kg) · v = velocity in metres/second (m/s)

You must know this from memory. January 2022 gave a mark just for writing momentum = mass × velocity, and then a mark for 72 × 13 = 936 kg m/s. In 2023 the first bird had a mass of 0.41 kg and a speed of 13 m/s: 0.41 × 13 = 5.3 kg m/s.

A slow lorry can have more momentum than a fast bullet, because momentum depends on both mass and velocity. Neither one on its own tells you who has more.

✗ Using grams. A 58 g ball is 0.058 kg. Always convert to kilograms first.
✗ "momentum = mass × acceleration" — that is force.
✓ "momentum = mass × velocity", with the mass in kg and the velocity in m/s.

The unit: kg m/s

Spec 1.2P · 2022 Edexcel 2P Q2(a)

The unit of momentum is the kilogram metre per second, written kg m/s.

It comes straight from the equation: kilograms (mass) multiplied by metres per second (velocity). 2022 asked for it as a multiple-choice mark, and the wrong options were built from dividing by the mass, squaring the mass, or other wrong combinations. If you remember p = m × v, you can rebuild the unit every time.

The unit N s (newton second) is the same size of unit, because force × time = change in momentum. You will see it in some textbooks, but kg m/s is the unit this spec names, so use it.

✗ kg/m/s   ✗ kg m/s² (that is a newton)   ✗ kg² m/s
✓ kg m/s

Momentum is a vector — choose a positive direction

Spec 1.25P (with 1.13, 1.14) · 2022 Edexcel 2P Q2(b)(ii)

Momentum has a direction as well as a size, so before any calculation you choose one direction as positive and give everything moving the other way a minus sign.

Velocity is a vector, and mass is a scalar, so momentum is a vector. A 2 kg trolley moving right at 3 m/s has momentum +6 kg m/s. The same trolley moving left at 3 m/s has momentum −6 kg m/s. Two trolleys with those momenta have a total momentum of zero, not 12.

  1. Draw a quick arrow and write "right = +" (or whichever way you like).
  2. Write each velocity with its sign: things moving the other way are negative.
  3. Work out each momentum with its sign, then add them.
  4. If an answer comes out negative, it means "in the negative direction". Say which way that is.

In 2022, the answer to the push-apart question was −2.4 m/s. The minus sign simply meant it moved the opposite way to the other object. The mark scheme accepted it.

Conservation of momentum

Spec 1.27P · 2022 Edexcel 2P Q2(b)(i)

In a collision or explosion, the total momentum before is equal to the total momentum after, as long as no external forces act.

total momentum before = total momentum after

That sentence is the one-mark "state the principle" answer. The mark scheme's version also mentions "in the absence of external forces", so add it. External forces are things like friction or a push from outside; if they are small, you ignore them.

Why it is true: when two objects collide, by Newton's third law (module 01) they push on each other with equal and opposite forces, for exactly the same time. So one gains exactly the momentum the other loses. The total cannot change.

The method for every conservation question

1. Choose a positive direction.  ·  2. Total momentum before = add up m × v for everything, with signs.  ·  3. Write total momentum after in the same way, with the unknown as a letter.  ·  4. Set before = after and rearrange.

Write the equation out in full before you put numbers in. The mark schemes give a mark for "setting up equation using conservation of momentum", separate from the answer.

Collisions where the objects join together

Spec 1.27P · 2023 Edexcel 2P Q4 — 7 marks

When two objects stick together, they move off as one object with the combined mass, so the velocity after = total momentum ÷ total mass.

2023's question was two birds. Bird X (0.41 kg, 13 m/s) caught bird Y, flying the same way. The question took you through it one step at a time:

  1. Momentum of X = 0.41 × 13 = 5.33 kg m/s.
  2. Total momentum before = momentum of X + momentum of Y = 5.48 kg m/s.
  3. Total momentum after = the same, 5.48 kg m/s. That was a mark for knowing momentum is conserved.
  4. Total mass after = 0.58 kg, so velocity = 5.48 ÷ 0.58 = 9.4 m/s.

Notice the answer is between the two birds' starting speeds. The fast bird slows down; the slow bird speeds up. That is a quick check on any "join together" answer.

✗ Dividing the total momentum by the mass of one bird. After the catch they move together, so use the total mass.
✓ velocity after = total momentum ÷ (m₁ + m₂)

Collisions where the objects bounce apart

Spec 1.27P

If the objects move off separately, the total momentum after is the sum of two separate momenta, and it still equals the total before.

Example: a 0.16 kg snooker ball moving at 2.5 m/s hits a stationary 0.16 kg ball. The first ball carries on at 0.5 m/s. Find the second ball's velocity.

  1. Before: 0.16 × 2.5 + 0.16 × 0 = 0.40 kg m/s.
  2. After: 0.16 × 0.5 + 0.16 × v = 0.08 + 0.16v.
  3. 0.40 = 0.08 + 0.16v, so v = 0.32 ÷ 0.16 = 2.0 m/s.

If an object bounces back, its velocity after is negative. Forgetting that minus sign is the most common error in this whole topic.

Explosions and recoil — starting from rest

Spec 1.27P · 2022 Edexcel 2P Q2(b)(ii)

If everything starts at rest, the total momentum before is zero, so after the explosion the two parts have equal and opposite momentum.

"Explosion" means anything that pushes two objects apart from rest: a spring between two trolleys, two skaters pushing off each other, a gun firing a bullet, a person jumping off a boat.

2022's question had a 1.9 kg object moving off at 3.7 m/s and a 2.9 kg object. The mark scheme's working was:

0 = (1.9 × 3.7) + (2.9 × v)  →  v = −7.03 ÷ 2.9 = −2.4 m/s

The minus sign means the 2.9 kg object moves the opposite way. Note that the heavier object moves off more slowly. The two momenta are the same size (7.03 kg m/s each); the masses are not.

This is why a gun recoils. The bullet gets forward momentum, so the gun must get the same momentum backwards. The gun is much heavier, so its recoil speed is much smaller. (The pair of forces between bullet and gun is a Newton's third law pair, covered in module 01.)

Force = change in momentum ÷ time

Spec 1.28P · 2022 Edexcel Jan 2P Q1(b)(iii)

A resultant force changes an object's momentum; the force equals the change in momentum divided by the time it takes.

F = (mv − mu) ÷ t
F = force (N) · m = mass (kg) · v = final velocity (m/s) · u = initial velocity (m/s) · t = time taken (s) · mv − mu = change in momentum (kg m/s)

This one is on the formula sheet, but you must know how to use it. In words: force = change in momentum ÷ time taken. So force is the rate of change of momentum.

January 2022: the driver's momentum of 936 kg m/s dropped to zero in 0.29 s. Force = 936 ÷ 0.29 = 3200 N (2 s.f.).

It is really F = m × a in disguise. (mv − mu) ÷ t = m × (v − u) ÷ t, and (v − u) ÷ t is the acceleration. Use whichever the question points you towards, but if it mentions momentum, use this one.

✗ Using the final momentum instead of the change. For something that stops, the change is all of its momentum; for something that speeds up from 10 m/s to 25 m/s, it is m × 25 − m × 10, not m × 25.
✓ Always work out mv − mu first, then divide by t.

Change in momentum when something bounces

Spec 1.28P, 1.25P

When an object bounces back, its momentum reverses, so the change in momentum is bigger than when it just stops.

A 0.20 kg ball hits a wall at 8.0 m/s and bounces back at 6.0 m/s. Take "towards the wall" as positive.

  1. mu = 0.20 × (+8.0) = +1.6 kg m/s.
  2. mv = 0.20 × (−6.0) = −1.2 kg m/s.
  3. mv − mu = −1.2 − 1.6 = −2.8 kg m/s. The size of the change is 2.8 kg m/s, away from the wall.

If the ball had simply stopped, the change would have been only 1.6 kg m/s. Bouncing needs a bigger change, so for the same contact time it needs a bigger force.

✗ 1.6 − 1.2 = 0.4 kg m/s. That treats both velocities as the same direction.
✓ −1.2 − (+1.6) = −2.8 kg m/s. The velocities are in opposite directions, so the sizes add.

Momentum and safety: the argument

Spec 1.26P · 2022 Edexcel 2P Q2(c) · 2022 Edexcel Jan 2P Q1(b)(iv)

In a crash your momentum has to fall to zero whatever happens; safety features make that take longer, so the rate of change of momentum, and so the force on you, is smaller.

Look at F = (mv − mu) ÷ t. In a crash, the change in momentum (mv − mu) is fixed: you go from your speed to zero. The only thing a safety feature can change is t. A bigger t on the bottom of the fraction means a smaller F.

The three-step script — one mark each

1. The feature increases the time taken for the person (or object) to stop.

2. The change in momentum is the same, so the rate of change of momentum is smaller.

3. So the force on the person is smaller, and there is less injury.

2022's egg on the carpet used exactly these steps: "idea of carpet increases impact time", then "carpet reduces rate of change of momentum", then a smaller force.

✗ "The airbag reduces the momentum." The driver's momentum still goes from the same value to zero, so the change is the same.
✗ "The airbag absorbs the force" or "cushions the impact". Too vague to score.
✓ "The airbag increases the time taken for the driver to stop, so the rate of change of momentum is smaller, so the force on the driver is smaller."

Crumple zones, airbags, seat belts and helmets

Spec 1.26P · 2022 Edexcel Jan 2P Q1(b)

Each safety feature works the same way: it lets the stop happen over a longer time. Only the part that does the stretching or squashing changes.

FeatureWhat it doesWhy the force is smaller
Crumple zoneThe front and back of the car crumple (deform) in a crashThe car takes longer to stop, so the rate of change of momentum of the car and the people inside is smaller
AirbagInflates and then squashes as the driver moves into itThe driver's head and chest take longer to stop, so the force on them is smaller
Seat beltStretches slightly and holds the passenger in the seatThe passenger takes longer to stop, so the force is smaller; it also stops them hitting the windscreen
Cycle helmetA foam lining compresses when the head hits somethingThe head takes longer to stop, so the force on the skull is smaller

The same idea explains everyday things: catching a ball by pulling your hands back, bending your knees when you land from a jump, and soft playground surfaces. In each one, a longer stopping time means a smaller force.

Momentum = mass × velocity, in kg m/s. It is a vector: choose a positive direction and use minus signs.

Total momentum before = total momentum after, in any collision or explosion, as long as no external forces act.

Joined together: velocity after = total momentum ÷ total mass.

From rest: total momentum is zero, so the two parts move off with equal and opposite momentum.

Force = change in momentum ÷ time taken. Force is the rate of change of momentum.

04Understanding it

These aren't exam questions. They're the questions worth thinking about slowly, because they attack the misconceptions that make exam questions go wrong. Try each one properly before opening the answer.

U1

Why is momentum conserved in a collision? Explain it using forces, not just by stating the rule.

Think about Newton's third law, and how long each force acts for.

Show answer

When A hits B, A pushes on B and B pushes on A. By Newton's third law these forces are equal in size and opposite in direction.

They also act for exactly the same time, because they only exist while the two objects are touching.

Change in momentum = force × time. So B gains exactly as much momentum as A loses, in the same direction A was going. Add the two changes and you get zero.

The catch: this only works if nothing outside the two objects pushes on them. A big friction force from the ground is an external force, and then the total momentum of the two objects can change. That is why the rule says "in the absence of external forces".

U2

Which has more momentum: a 12 000 kg lorry rolling at 0.5 m/s, or a 0.01 kg bullet at 400 m/s?

Show answer

Lorry: 12 000 × 0.5 = 6000 kg m/s. Bullet: 0.01 × 400 = 4 kg m/s.

The slow lorry has 1500 times the momentum of the fast bullet.

The point: momentum depends on mass and velocity together. "Fast" does not mean "lots of momentum", and nor does "heavy". You have to multiply. This is why a slowly rolling lorry is so hard to stop by hand.

U3

A rifle fires a bullet. The bullet and the rifle get momenta of exactly the same size. So why does the bullet fly off at hundreds of metres per second while the rifle only moves back slowly?

Show answer

Before firing, everything is at rest, so the total momentum is zero. After firing, it must still be zero. So the bullet's forward momentum and the rifle's backward momentum are equal and opposite.

Momentum = mass × velocity. The same momentum shared by a small mass gives a large velocity; by a large mass, a small velocity.

A 4 kg rifle and a 0.01 kg bullet: the rifle is 400 times heavier, so it moves 400 times more slowly.

Same idea in 2022's push-apart question: the 2.9 kg object moved off more slowly (2.4 m/s) than the 1.9 kg one (3.7 m/s).

U4

A student says: "An airbag protects you because it reduces your momentum." What is wrong with this, and what should they say instead?

Think about What is your momentum at the end of the crash, with or without the airbag?

Show answer

With or without the airbag, you start at the car's speed and end at zero. So your change in momentum is the same either way. The airbag does not reduce it at all.

What changes is the time. Without an airbag, your head stops in a few milliseconds against the steering wheel. With one, it stops over a longer time as the bag squashes.

Force = change in momentum ÷ time. Same top, bigger bottom, so a smaller force.

The January 2022 mark scheme made the related point that increasing the force does not protect the driver: protection means a smaller force, and that comes from a longer time.

U5

A ball hits a wall and stops dead. An identical ball hits the wall at the same speed and bounces back at the same speed. The contact times are equal. Which ball feels the bigger force from the wall, and by how much?

Show answer

Take towards the wall as positive, and call the momentum before +p.

Stops dead: momentum goes from +p to 0. Change = p.

Bounces back: momentum goes from +p to −p. Change = −p − p = 2p.

Same contact time, so the bouncing ball feels twice the force.

Why this matters: it is the reason signs matter so much. If you forget that the bounce-back velocity is negative, you calculate a change of zero, which is obviously wrong: the ball clearly was pushed.

U6

A cricketer catches a hard ball. Why does she pull her hands back as she catches it?

Show answer

The ball's momentum has to fall to zero either way. The change in momentum is fixed.

By moving her hands back with the ball, she increases the time over which the ball stops.

So the rate of change of momentum is smaller, and the force on her hands is smaller. It stings less.

This is exactly the same argument as a crumple zone, an airbag, a seat belt or a helmet. Learn the argument once and it answers every version.

U7

Trolley A (2 kg) moves right at 3 m/s. Trolley B (2 kg) moves left at 3 m/s. They collide head-on and stick together. A student says the total momentum is 12 kg m/s, so they move off at 3 m/s. What should happen, and where did the student go wrong?

Show answer

Take right as positive. A: 2 × (+3) = +6 kg m/s. B: 2 × (−3) = −6 kg m/s.

Total momentum = +6 + (−6) = 0. After sticking, the total is still 0, so they stop dead.

The student ignored direction and added the sizes. Momentum is a vector, so opposite directions cancel.

The habit that fixes it: write "right = +" at the start of every momentum question, before you write a single number.

U8

In the 2023 two-birds question, the momentum after the catch was the same as before, but the fast bird slowed down from 13 m/s. Where did its momentum go?

Show answer

It was shared with the slower bird. After the catch, both birds move together at 9.4 m/s.

Bird X lost some momentum; bird Y gained exactly the same amount. The total stayed at 5.48 kg m/s.

The same total momentum is now carried by a bigger mass (0.58 kg instead of 0.41 kg), so the velocity must be smaller: 5.48 ÷ 0.58 = 9.4 m/s.

Quick check for any "join together" question: the final velocity must lie between the two starting velocities. If it doesn't, you have made a mistake.

05Exam drill

Now in exam register. Time yourself at roughly one minute per mark. State formulas when asked, show substitutions, give units, and give a direction or a sign for every velocity and momentum.

D1

Airbags are safety devices used in cars. In a crash, a driver of mass 65 kg is moving at 15 m/s.

(a) State the formula linking momentum, mass and velocity. (1)
(b) Calculate the momentum of the driver. (2)
(c) The airbag brings the driver to rest in 0.25 s. Calculate the average force on the driver. (2)
(d) Explain how the airbag reduces the force on the driver. (2)

Show answer

(a) momentum = mass × velocity 1

(b) 65 × 15 1

= 975 kg m/s 1

(c) force = change in momentum ÷ time taken = 975 ÷ 0.25 1

= 3900 N 1

(d) The airbag increases the time taken for the driver to stop 1

So the rate of change of momentum is smaller, and the force is smaller 1

This is the January 2022 question with new numbers. If your (b) was wrong, you would still get (c) for dividing your own answer by 0.25. Always carry on.

D2

(a) Which of these is the correct unit for momentum? (1)

A kg/m/s    B kg m/s²    C kg m/s    D kg² m/s

(b) State the principle of conservation of momentum. (1)

Show answer

(a) C, kg m/s 1

(b) The total momentum before a collision (or explosion) equals the total momentum after it, in the absence of external forces 1

B is the unit of force (a newton). Build the unit from p = m × v: kg × m/s.

D3

A hawk of mass 0.60 kg flies horizontally at 12 m/s. It catches a pigeon of mass 0.30 kg flying in the same direction at 3.0 m/s. After the catch they move together.

(a) Calculate the momentum of the hawk before the catch. (2)
(b) Calculate the total momentum of the two birds before the catch. (1)
(c) State the total momentum of the two birds just after the catch. (1)
(d) Calculate the velocity of the two birds just after the catch. (3)

Show answer

(a) 0.60 × 12 1 = 7.2 kg m/s 1

(b) 7.2 + (0.30 × 3.0) = 7.2 + 0.90 = 8.1 kg m/s 1

(c) 8.1 kg m/s — the same as before, because momentum is conserved 1

(d) Total mass = 0.60 + 0.30 = 0.90 kg 1

velocity = 8.1 ÷ 0.90 1

= 9.0 m/s in the original direction 1

Modelled on 2023 Edexcel 2P Q4. Check: 9.0 m/s lies between 3.0 and 12 m/s, as it must.

D4

Two trolleys, A and B, are at rest with a compressed spring between them. The spring is released. Trolley A (mass 1.5 kg) moves to the left at 2.0 m/s. Trolley B has a mass of 0.75 kg.

(a) Explain why the total momentum of the trolleys after the spring is released is zero. (1)
(b) Calculate the velocity of trolley B. (3)

Show answer

(a) The trolleys start at rest, so the total momentum before is zero, and momentum is conserved 1

(b) Taking right as positive: 0 = (1.5 × −2.0) + (0.75 × v) 1

0.75v = 3.0, so v = 3.0 ÷ 0.75 1

= 4.0 m/s to the right 1

The lighter trolley moves off faster, in the opposite direction. Its momentum (+3.0 kg m/s) is equal and opposite to A's (−3.0 kg m/s).

D5

An egg is dropped from the same height onto a hard floor and onto a thick carpet. It breaks on the floor but not on the carpet.

Explain why, using ideas about momentum. (3)

Show answer

The carpet increases the time taken for the egg to stop (the impact time) 1

The egg's change in momentum is the same, so the rate of change of momentum is smaller 1

So the force on the egg is smaller 1

From 2022 Edexcel 2P Q2(c): the scheme wanted the impact time and the rate of change of momentum. "The carpet is soft" scores nothing without them.

D6

A tennis ball of mass 58 g is at rest. A racket hits it, and it leaves the racket at 50 m/s. The racket is in contact with the ball for 0.0040 s.

(a) Calculate the change in momentum of the ball. (2)
(b) Calculate the average force of the racket on the ball. (2)

Show answer

(a) 58 g = 0.058 kg, so change in momentum = 0.058 × 50 − 0 1

= 2.9 kg m/s 1

(b) F = change in momentum ÷ t = 2.9 ÷ 0.0040 1

= 725 N (about 730 N) 1

Using 58 instead of 0.058 gives an answer a thousand times too big. Convert grams to kilograms first.

D7

A ball of mass 0.20 kg hits a wall at 8.0 m/s and bounces straight back at 6.0 m/s. It is in contact with the wall for 0.050 s.

(a) Calculate the size of the change in momentum of the ball. (3)
(b) Calculate the average force exerted on the ball by the wall. (2)

Show answer

(a) Taking towards the wall as positive, momentum before = 0.20 × 8.0 = +1.6 kg m/s 1

Momentum after = 0.20 × (−6.0) = −1.2 kg m/s 1

Change = −1.2 − 1.6, so the size is 2.8 kg m/s 1

(b) F = 2.8 ÷ 0.050 1

= 56 N, directed away from the wall 1

0.4 kg m/s is the answer you get if you forget the ball has changed direction. The sizes of the two momenta add when the directions are opposite.

D8

Modern cars have crumple zones at the front and back.

Explain how a crumple zone reduces the risk of injury to the people in the car during a collision. (4)

Show answer

The crumple zone deforms (crumples), which increases the time taken for the car and its passengers to stop 1

The change in momentum of the passengers is the same (they still go from their speed to zero) 1

So the rate of change of momentum is smaller 1

So the force on the passengers is smaller, and they are less likely to be injured 1

The script is always the same: time up, rate of change of momentum down, force down. Only the first line changes from feature to feature.

D9

Trolley A of mass 2.0 kg moves to the right at 3.0 m/s. Trolley B of mass 1.0 kg moves to the left at 2.0 m/s. They collide head-on and stick together.

Calculate the velocity of the trolleys after the collision. (4)

Show answer

Taking right as positive, momentum of B = 1.0 × (−2.0) = −2.0 kg m/s 1

Total momentum before = 2.0 × 3.0 + (−2.0) = 4.0 kg m/s 1

velocity after = 4.0 ÷ (2.0 + 1.0) 1

= 1.3 m/s to the right 1

If you got 2.7 m/s, you added 6.0 and 2.0. B is moving the other way, so its momentum is negative.

D10

A rifle of mass 4.0 kg fires a bullet of mass 0.010 kg. The bullet leaves the rifle at 400 m/s.

(a) Calculate the momentum of the bullet. (2)
(b) Explain why the rifle moves backwards when the bullet is fired. (2)
(c) Calculate the recoil velocity of the rifle. (2)

Show answer

(a) 0.010 × 400 1 = 4.0 kg m/s 1

(b) Before firing, the total momentum is zero 1

Momentum is conserved, so the rifle must have momentum equal in size and opposite in direction to the bullet's 1

(c) v = 4.0 ÷ 4.0 1

= 1.0 m/s backwards (opposite to the bullet) 1

The rifle is 400 times heavier than the bullet, so it moves 400 times more slowly.

D11

Two ice skaters stand at rest facing each other, then push apart. Skater A has a mass of 60 kg and moves off at 2.0 m/s. Skater B moves off in the opposite direction at 3.0 m/s.

Calculate the mass of skater B. Ignore friction. (3)

Show answer

Total momentum before = 0, so 0 = (60 × 2.0) + (m × −3.0) 1

3.0m = 120, so m = 120 ÷ 3.0 1

= 40 kg 1

Sense check: B moves faster, so B must be lighter. 40 kg is lighter than 60 kg.

D12

A car of mass 1200 kg speeds up from 10 m/s to 25 m/s in 6.0 s.

(a) State the relationship between force, change in momentum and time taken. (1)
(b) Calculate the resultant force on the car. (3)

Show answer

(a) force = change in momentum ÷ time taken 1

(b) Change in momentum = (1200 × 25) − (1200 × 10) = 18 000 kg m/s 1

F = 18 000 ÷ 6.0 1

= 3000 N 1

Using only the final momentum (30 000 ÷ 6.0 = 5000 N) is the classic error. The car already had momentum before it sped up.

D13

(a) Explain how a seat belt reduces the force on a passenger when a car stops suddenly. (3)
(b) A cycle helmet has a thick foam lining. Explain how the lining protects the rider's head in a fall. (2)

Show answer

(a) The seat belt stretches slightly, increasing the time taken for the passenger to stop 1

The passenger's change in momentum is the same, so the rate of change of momentum is smaller 1

So the force on the passenger is smaller 1

(b) The foam compresses, increasing the time taken for the head to stop 1

So the rate of change of momentum of the head, and the force on it, is smaller 1

"It is soft" or "it cushions the head" is not enough. Name the time and the force.

D14

A snooker ball of mass 0.16 kg moving at 2.5 m/s hits a stationary snooker ball of the same mass. After the collision the first ball moves on in the same direction at 0.50 m/s.

(a) Calculate the velocity of the second ball after the collision. (3)
(b) State one assumption you made in your calculation. (1)

Show answer

(a) Total momentum before = 0.16 × 2.5 = 0.40 kg m/s 1

0.40 = (0.16 × 0.50) + (0.16 × v), so 0.16v = 0.32 1

v = 2.0 m/s in the original direction 1

(b) No external forces act on the balls during the collision (friction from the table is negligible) 1

The stationary ball has zero momentum before, but it still appears in the "after" side of the equation.

D15

A car of mass 1500 kg travelling at 20 m/s crashes into a solid wall and stops.

(a) Calculate the momentum of the car just before the crash. (2)
(b) The car stops in 0.12 s. Calculate the average force on the car. (2)
(c) A car with a crumple zone stops in 0.30 s instead. Calculate the average force on this car. (2)
(d) A passenger of mass 70 kg wearing a seat belt stops in 0.30 s. Calculate the average force on the passenger. (2)

Show answer

(a) 1500 × 20 1 = 30 000 kg m/s 1

(b) F = 30 000 ÷ 0.12 1 = 250 000 N 1

(c) F = 30 000 ÷ 0.30 1 = 100 000 N 1

(d) Change in momentum = 70 × 20 = 1400 kg m/s, so F = 1400 ÷ 0.30 1

= 4700 N (2 s.f.) 1

Compare (b) and (c): the same change in momentum, 2.5 times the time, so 2.5 times less force. That is the whole safety argument in numbers.
