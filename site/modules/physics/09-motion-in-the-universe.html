02How it actually works

Everything in orbit is falling. A satellite moves fast enough sideways that it keeps missing the thing it is falling towards, and gravity keeps bending its path into a circle. That single idea answers most of this topic.

The scale: solar system, galaxy, universe

Topic test Q5(a)

Three words in order of size, and the exam asks you to name them from a description:

  • Solar system — the Sun and everything orbiting it: planets, their moons, asteroids and comets.
  • Galaxy — a large collection of billions of stars (ours is the Milky Way).
  • Universe — space and all the galaxies in it.

Gravity is the force, and only gravity

Topic test Q5(b)(ii)

The force that keeps any object in orbit is gravity (the gravitational force or gravitational pull of the body it orbits). The mark scheme accepts those three, and rejects "gravitational potential" and "gravitational field strength" — those are related quantities, not the force itself.

Orbits are ellipses, not perfect circles. For a comet that means its distance from the Sun changes — and it moves fastest when it is closest to the Sun, slowest when it is furthest away. The topic test's multiple choice turned entirely on that: every wrong option was further from the Sun.

Orbital speed — the one equation to memorise

Topic test Q6, Q7(b), Q8(b)(i)

In one orbit an object travels the circumference of a circle, 2πr, in one time period T — so orbital speed v = 2πr ÷ T. (The equation and its symbol key are on the cheat sheet.)

Three rearrangements, all examined:

  • Find the speed (Callisto): convert the period to seconds first — 400 hours × 60 × 60 = 1 440 000 s — then v = 2π × 1 880 000 ÷ 1 440 000 = 8.20 km/s to 3 s.f.
  • Find the radius (ISS): r = vT ÷ 2π = 7.66 × (92.7 × 60) ÷ 2π = 6780 km.
  • Find the height above the surface: height = orbital radius − radius of the planet. The ISS orbits 409 km up, so the Earth's radius is 6780 − 409 = 6370 km. A geostationary satellite: r = 42 628 km, so height = 42 628 − 6400 = 36 000 km.

Two marks die here every time. Convert the period to seconds before substituting (24 hours = 86 400 s), and remember the orbital radius is measured from the centre of the planet — so the height above the surface is always the radius minus the planet's radius. The topic test asked for the difference explicitly in three separate questions.

Gravitational field strength and weight elsewhere

Topic test Q7(c)

g is the gravitational field strength in N/kg — the pull on each kilogram. On Earth it is about 10 N/kg; on Callisto 1.2; on our Moon 1.6.

Weight follows the same equation everywhere: W = m × g — mass never changes, but g does. (On the cheat sheet.)

To move a weight between two worlds, find the mass first: an object weighing 59 N on the Moon has mass 59 ÷ 1.6 = 37 kg, so on Callisto it weighs 37 × 1.2 = 44 N.

Why does Callisto pull less than our Moon despite being more massive? Because field strength depends on mass and radius — Callisto has a larger radius (it is less dense), so its surface sits further from its centre and the pull there is weaker. Any one of "larger radius", "lower density" or "smaller core" scored the mark.

Satellites: what orbits what

Topic test Q8(a)

The comparison question wants both halves stated: a satellite orbits a planet whereas a planet orbits a star; or the planet's orbital radius and period are greater. For the similarity: both orbit something, and both have the same shape of orbit (circular or elliptical).

A geostationary satellite has a period of exactly 24 hours, so it stays above the same point on the equator — which is why a satellite dish can be bolted in place.

The Doppler effect

Spec 3.8 — this part IS examinable · Topic test Q8(b)(ii)–(iii)

When a source of waves moves relative to an observer, the observed wavelength and frequency change. The three-mark explanation:

The script

1. The speed of the waves is constant 1

2. If the source moves away, the wavefronts spread out — the wavelength increases 1

3. Since speed = frequency × wavelength, a longer wavelength at constant speed means a lower observed frequency 1

And the trick question: why are the radio waves from a geostationary satellite not Doppler-shifted? Because there is no relative motion between the satellite and the point on the Earth's surface — the distance never changes — so there is no change in wavelength or frequency.

This is the one part of the module that is examinable on the resit, because Doppler sits in the Waves topic (spec 3.8) rather than in astrophysics.

04Understanding it

U1

A satellite is in a stable orbit and its engines are off. Nothing is pushing it forwards — so why doesn't it slow down and fall?

Show answer

Nothing needs to push it forwards, because in space there is essentially no air resistance — no friction to slow it down. Newton's first law does the rest: with no force along its direction of travel, its speed stays constant.

Gravity is acting, but it pulls at right angles to the motion — towards the centre of the Earth. That force doesn't speed the satellite up or slow it down; it continuously changes the direction of the velocity, bending the straight-line path into a circle.

So the satellite genuinely is falling towards Earth the whole time — it is just moving sideways fast enough that the Earth's surface curves away beneath it at the same rate.

U2

The ISS orbits at 409 km with speed 7.66 km/s and period 92.7 minutes. Work out the Earth's radius — and explain why the answer is not 409 km short of something.

Show answer

Step 1 — period in seconds: 92.7 × 60 = 5562 s.

Step 2 — rearrange v = 2πr/T: r = vT ÷ 2π = 7.66 × 5562 ÷ 6.283 = 6780 km (3 s.f.).

Step 3: that 6780 km is measured from the centre of the Earth, because that is where the orbit is centred. The station is 409 km above the surface, so the surface sits at 6780 − 409 = 6370 km from the centre — which is the Earth's radius, and matches the real value of about 6371 km.

The reason it isn't "409 km short of something" is that the formula never knew about the surface at all — 2πr is the circumference of the circle the station actually travels, and that circle is centred on the Earth's core.

U3

Callisto is more massive than our Moon, yet its surface gravity is weaker (1.2 N/kg versus 1.6). How can more mass produce less pull?

Show answer

Because surface gravity depends on two things: how much mass is pulling, and how far you are from the centre of it.

Callisto is considerably bigger across, so standing on its surface puts you much further from its centre than standing on the Moon does. That extra distance weakens the pull more than the extra mass strengthens it, and the net result is a lower g.

Any one of these scored the mark: Callisto has a larger radius, a lower density, or a smaller core. What does not score is anything about its distance from the Sun or its orbital radius — those are irrelevant to surface gravity.

U4

A satellite broadcasts radio waves at a fixed frequency. Explain why a geostationary satellite's signal shows no Doppler shift, while a low-orbit satellite passing overhead does.

Show answer

The Doppler effect needs relative motion between source and observer — specifically, the distance between them must be changing.

Geostationary: its period is 24 hours, exactly matching the Earth's rotation, so it hovers above the same point. The distance to your dish never changes, so the wavefronts arrive at exactly the rate they left. No wavelength change, no frequency change.

Low orbit: it sweeps across the sky in minutes. As it approaches, the wavefronts bunch up — shorter wavelength, higher observed frequency. As it recedes, they spread out — longer wavelength, lower frequency. The signal's frequency appears to slide downwards as it passes overhead.

Throughout, the waves themselves travel at the speed of light. It is the wavelength that changes, and frequency follows because v = fλ with v fixed.

U5

Two satellites orbit Earth, one twice as far out as the other. Using v = 2πr/T alone, can you say which travels faster? What does the real answer depend on?

Show answer

From that equation alone, no. It links three quantities: double r, and the speed doubles only if T stays the same. But T does not stay the same — a satellite further out takes longer to go round, and the formula does not tell you by how much.

In reality the further satellite moves more slowly: gravity is weaker further out, so less speed is needed to stay in orbit. The ISS at 409 km does 7.66 km/s and laps the Earth in 93 minutes; a geostationary satellite 36 000 km up manages only 3.1 km/s and takes a full day.

For the exam, the safe version is the comet fact: within one elliptical orbit, an object moves fastest when closest and slowest when furthest away.

05Exam drill

D1

Complete the sentences.

(a) Space and all the galaxies in it is called the ............... (1)
(b) A large collection of billions of stars is called a ............... (1)
(c) The Sun and its collection of planets and moons is called the ............... (1)
(d) Name the force that keeps a comet in orbit around the Sun. (1)

Show answer

(a) Universe 1 · (b) galaxy 1 (allow a named galaxy, e.g. the Milky Way) · (c) solar system 1

(d) Gravity 1 — allow gravitational force or gravitational pull; reject gravitational potential and gravitational field strength.

D2

Callisto orbits Jupiter at an orbital radius of 1 880 000 km with a period of 400 hours. Calculate its orbital speed in km/s, to 3 significant figures. (4)

Show answer

Convert the time: 400 × 60 × 60 = 1 440 000 s 1; substitute into v = 2πr ÷ T 1; evaluate: 2π × 1 880 000 ÷ 1 440 000 = 8.203… 1; give to 3 s.f. = 8.20 km/s 1

The conversion and the 3 s.f. are marked independently — you can lose either without losing the other.

D3

KALPANA-1 has an orbital speed of 3.1 km/s and completes one orbit in 24 hours. Calculate the height of its orbit above the Earth's surface. [radius of Earth = 6400 km] (4)

Show answer

Substitute into v = 2πr ÷ T 1; convert 24 hours to 86 400 s 1; rearrange and evaluate the orbital radius: r = 3.1 × 86 400 ÷ 2π = 42 628 km 1; height = 42 628 − 6400 = 36 000 km 1

Forgetting the last subtraction leaves you with the orbital radius, not the height — one of the most common losses in this topic.

D4

Callisto has a gravitational field strength of 1.2 N/kg; the Earth's Moon has 1.6 N/kg.

(a) Callisto has a larger mass than the Moon. Suggest why its gravitational field strength is lower. (1)
(b) An object weighs 59 N on the Moon. Calculate its weight on Callisto. (3)

Show answer

(a) Any one: Callisto has a larger radius · a lower density · a smaller core 1 — ignore references to orbital radius or distance from the Sun.

(b) Use of W = m × g 1; set up the ratio or find the mass: m = 59 ÷ 1.6 = 37 kg 1; W = 37 × 1.2 = 44 N 1 — an answer of 78–79 N scores 2 (multiplied instead of dividing).

D5

(a) State a difference between an artificial satellite's orbit and a planet's orbit. (1)
(b) State a similarity between an artificial satellite's orbit and a moon's orbit. (1)
(c) Explain how the Doppler effect causes a change in the observed frequency of waves. (4)

Show answer

(a) A satellite orbits a planet whereas a planet orbits a star 1 — both halves of the comparison are required; or: the planet's orbital radius/period is greater.

(b) Both orbit a planet, or both have the same shape of orbit (circular/elliptical) 1

(c) Any three: the speed of the waves is constant 1; if the source moves away the wavefronts spread out and the wavelength increases 1; speed = frequency × wavelength 1; a higher wavelength at constant speed gives a lower frequency 1

D6

The diagram shows a comet's elliptical orbit around the Sun, with points A (closest) through D (furthest).

(a) At which point is the comet moving fastest? (1)
(b) Suggest why the radio signal from a geostationary satellite shows no Doppler shift when received on Earth. (2)

Show answer

(a) A 1 — B, C and D are all further from the Sun, and orbital speed decreases with distance.

(b) There is no relative motion between the satellite and the point on the Earth's surface 1 (the distance does not change); therefore there is no change in wavelength or frequency 1
