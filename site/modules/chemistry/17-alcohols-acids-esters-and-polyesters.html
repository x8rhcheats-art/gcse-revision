02How it actually works

This module is three families of carbon compounds and how they turn into each other. An alcohol can be oxidised to a carboxylic acid. An alcohol plus a carboxylic acid make an ester. Join acids and alcohols with two functional groups each and you get a polyester. Homologous series and alkenes are in module 10, equilibrium in module 15 and addition polymers in module 16. This module links to them rather than repeating them.

Burning alcohols — complete combustion

Spec 4.31C · 2023 Edexcel 1C Q8(d)

In plenty of oxygen, an alcohol burns completely to carbon dioxide and water, just like a hydrocarbon. Burning is one of the three ways ethanol can be oxidised.

Complete combustion of ethanolC₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂Ocount the O in the alcohol too: 1 + 6 = 7 on the left, 4 + 3 = 7 on the right

Balancing any alcohol combustion, in order

  1. Carbon: number of C atoms → that many CO₂.
  2. Hydrogen: count all the H atoms (C₅H₁₁OH has 12) → half that many H₂O.
  3. Oxygen: count the O on the right, subtract the one O already in the alcohol, then halve to get the O₂.
  4. A half is allowed (7½O₂), or double everything.

Pentanol: C₅H₁₁OH → 5CO₂ + 6H₂O. Right side has 10 + 6 = 16 O. Minus 1 in the alcohol = 15 O atoms = 7½O₂.

Complete combustion of pentanolC₅H₁₁OH + 7½O₂ → 5CO₂ + 6H₂O2023 Edexcel 1C Q8(d) — formulae one mark, balancing the second; multiples allowed

✗ C₂H₅OH + 3½O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O — forgot the oxygen already inside the alcohol.
✓ C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O.

Oxidising ethanol to ethanoic acid

Spec 4.31C, 4.37C · 2024 Edexcel 2C Q5(b)

Ethanol is oxidised to ethanoic acid in two ways: slowly by microbes using oxygen in the air, or quickly by heating with acidified potassium dichromate(VI).

Microbial oxidation. Leave wine open to the air and microbes use oxygen to oxidise the ethanol. It turns sour: the ethanoic acid makes vinegar, which is an aqueous solution containing ethanoic acid.

Microbial oxidation of ethanolC₂H₅OH + O₂ → CH₃COOH + H₂Ooxygen from the air; microbes do it · this is why wine left open goes sour

Potassium dichromate(VI). Heat ethanol with potassium dichromate(VI) in dilute sulfuric acid. The dichromate is the oxidising agent.

  1. Add potassium dichromate(VI) and dilute sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) to the alcohol.
  2. Heat.
  3. The solution changes from orange to green.
  4. The ethanol has become ethanoic acid.

The same thing works on the other alcohols: methanol → methanoic acid, propanol → propanoic acid. The carbon count stays the same.

2024 scheme: orange and green "must be in the correct order".
✗ "from green to orange" — no marks.
✗ "the ethanol turns green" — ethanol is colourless; it is the dichromate that changes.
✓ "The potassium dichromate(VI) changes from orange to green."

Making ethanol 1 — hydration of ethene

Spec 4.32C · 2022 Jan Edexcel 2C Q1(c) · 2023 Edexcel 2C Q3(b)

Ethene reacts with steam at about 300 °C and 60–70 atm, with a phosphoric acid catalyst, to make ethanol. "Hydration" means adding water across the C=C double bond.

Hydration of etheneC₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OHsteam · phosphoric acid catalyst · about 300 °C · 60–70 atm
  • Reactants: ethene and steam. The ethene comes from cracking crude-oil fractions (module 10).
  • Catalyst: phosphoric acid (H₃PO₄).
  • Temperature: about 300 °C. Pressure: about 60–70 atm.
  • It is fast, it runs continuously, and the ethanol made is pure.

2022 Jan scheme accepted any pressure "between 60 and 70 atm". It had to be in that window.
✗ "high temperature and high pressure" — the question wants numbers.
✓ "300 °C and 60–70 atmospheres, with a phosphoric acid catalyst."

Making ethanol 2 — fermentation

Spec 4.32C, 4.33C · 2023 Edexcel 2C Q3(a), (c)

Enzymes in yeast turn glucose into ethanol and carbon dioxide, at about 30 °C and with no air.

Fermentation of glucoseC₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂enzymes in yeast · about 30 °C · absence of air · atmospheric pressure

Check it: 6 C, 12 H and 6 O on each side. Write ethanol as C₂H₅OH here. The 2023 scheme gave no formula mark for C₂H₆O in this equation.

Why no air — either of these two-mark chains:

  1. Oxygen in the air would oxidise the ethanol …
  2. … to ethanoic acid (the wine turns to vinegar).

or: fermentation must be anaerobic so ethanol forms; with oxygen the yeast respires aerobically and makes only carbon dioxide and water.

Why about 30 °C — the optimum temperature:

  1. Colder: the enzymes work slowly, so fermentation is too slow.
  2. Hotter: the enzymes in the yeast denature (their active sites change shape), so fermentation stops.
  3. About 30 °C gives a reasonable rate without denaturing the enzymes.

The product is a dilute solution of ethanol in water, so it has to be purified by fractional distillation.

✗ "No air, or the yeast would die." — not the reason given in any mark scheme.
✗ "At high temperature the enzymes are killed." — enzymes are not alive.
✓ "Oxygen would oxidise the ethanol to ethanoic acid." · "Above the optimum the enzymes denature."

Hydration or fermentation — the comparison answer

Spec 4.32C · 2023 Edexcel 2C Q3(b)

Fermentation uses a renewable raw material and gentle conditions, but it is slow, runs in batches and gives impure ethanol. Hydration is the opposite on every point.

The four-mark answer — advantage and disadvantage of fermentation

Advantage: uses a lower temperature and pressure 1, so less energy is needed 1

or: glucose comes from plants, so it is renewable, whereas ethene comes from crude oil, which is finite.

Disadvantage: fermentation is slower 1, and it is a batch process whereas hydration is continuous 1

or: the ethanol is impure, so it has to be purified. Or: growing sugar cane uses land that could grow food crops.

Carboxylic acid reactions — metals and carbonates

Spec 4.36C · 2022 Edexcel 2C Q5(b) · 2022 Jan Edexcel 2C Q7(a)(iii)

Carboxylic acids react like any acid: with metals they give a salt and hydrogen, and with carbonates a salt, water and carbon dioxide.

Ethanoic acid and magnesium2CH₃COOH + Mg → Mg(CH₃COO)₂ + H₂salt = magnesium ethanoate · fizzing · hydrogen gives a squeaky pop
Ethanoic acid and sodium carbonate2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂salt = sodium ethanoate · fizzing · CO₂ turns limewater milky
Methanoic acid and sodium carbonate2HCOOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2HCOONa + H₂O + CO₂2022 Edexcel 2C Q5(b) · salt = sodium methanoate

The salts are named -oate: methanoate, ethanoate, propanoate, butanoate. The metal replaces the H of the –COOH, so sodium ethanoate is CH₃COONa.

Carboxylic acids are weak acids. They do the same reactions as hydrochloric acid, only more gently: slower fizzing at the same concentration.

Test for a carboxylic acid (not using an indicator) — two marks

1. Add a named metal such as magnesium or zinc — or a carbonate such as sodium carbonate 1

2. Effervescence (bubbles, fizzing) 1

An alcohol or an ester gives no fizzing. That is how you tell them apart.

2022 Jan scheme: the metal "must be a named metal" and "REJECT Group 1 metals". 2022 June: "REJECT NaCOOH" and, for the observation, "IGNORE gas given off".
✗ "Add sodium; a gas is given off."
✓ "Add magnesium ribbon; effervescence." · Salt formula HCOONa, not NaCOOH.

Esters — making ethyl ethanoate

Spec 4.38C, 4.39C, 4.40C · 2023 Jan Edexcel 2C Q6(a), (c)(i) · 2023 Edexcel 2C Q6(a) · 2022 Jan Edexcel 2C Q7(a)(ii)

An alcohol and a carboxylic acid react, with concentrated sulfuric acid as a catalyst, to make an ester and water.

Making ethyl ethanoateCH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂Oethanoic acid + ethanol ⇌ ethyl ethanoate + water · catalyst: concentrated sulfuric acid

The –OH of the acid and the H of the alcohol's –OH leave together as water. The rest joins through the new –COO– link.

Ethyl ethanoate: structural formula CH₃COOCH₂CH₃, molecular formula C₄H₈O₂.

The reaction is reversible (⇌), so it reaches equilibrium and never fully converts (module 15). The sulfuric acid is a catalyst. That is why its moles do not change during the reaction (2023 Jan Edexcel 2C Q6(c)(i)).

✓ The C=O drawn as a double bond, the single-bonded O drawn between the two carbons, and every C–H drawn.

Naming and drawing any ester

Spec 4.41C · 2024 Edexcel 2C Q5(c), (d) · 2023 Edexcel 2C Q6(a)(iii) · 2022 Edexcel 2C Q5(c)(i)

Name an ester alcohol-part first, ending in "-yl", then acid-part, ending in "-oate". But write the formula acid-part first.

  1. From the alcohol: methanol → methyl, ethanol → ethyl, propanol → propyl, butanol → butyl.
  2. From the acid: methanoic → methanoate, ethanoic → ethanoate, propanoic → propanoate, butanoic → butanoate.
  3. Name = alcohol part + acid part, e.g. ethyl ethanoate, propyl methanoate.
  4. Formula = the acid part (the side with C=O) written first, then the alcohol chain: ethyl ethanoate is CH₃COOCH₂CH₃.

Why esters matter — smells, uses and the practical

Spec 4.42C, 4.43C · 2023 Edexcel 2C Q6(a)(i), (ii)

Esters are volatile and have distinctive, usually fruity, smells, so they are used as food flavourings and in perfumes. "Volatile" means they evaporate easily, which is how the smell reaches your nose.

Practical 4.43C — preparing ethyl ethanoate:

  1. Put a little ethanol and ethanoic acid in a test tube.
  2. Add a few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid (the catalyst).
  3. Warm in a hot water bath for a few minutes. No naked flame, because ethanol and the ester are flammable.
  4. Pour the mixture into a beaker of sodium carbonate solution (or water). The carbonate neutralises the leftover acids and fizzes.
  5. The ester forms an oily layer on the surface. Waft it carefully: it has a sweet, fruity smell, like pear drops or nail-polish remover.

2023 scheme for "how would you know an ester has formed": distinctive, sweet or fruity smell, and it would also "ACCEPT an oily layer forms".
✗ "It changes colour." / "It fizzes." (the fizzing is the sodium carbonate reacting with leftover acid, not the ester).
✓ "It has a fruity smell" or "an oily layer forms on the surface."

Polyesters and biopolyesters

Spec 4.48C, 4.49C, 4.50C · 2022 Edexcel 2C Q5(d) · 2022 Jan Edexcel 2C Q7(e)

A dicarboxylic acid and a diol join through ester links to make a polyester, and a molecule of water is lost at every link. This is condensation polymerisation.

Each monomer has a functional group at both ends, so the chain can keep growing in both directions. Compare addition polymers (module 16): one monomer with a C=C, and nothing else is made. Here there are two different monomers and water is made too.

      O  O                    H  H
      ║  ║                    │  │
H──O──C──C──O──H        H──O──C──C──O──H
                              │  │
                              H  H
ethanedioic acid        ethanediol

The acid loses –OH from each end. The diol loses H from each –OH. Those join to make water, and the C and O join to make ester links. The repeat unit:

  ┌                    ┐
  │  O  O     H  H     │
  │  ║  ║     │  │     │
 ─┼──C──C──O──C──C──O──┼─
  │           │  │     │
  │           H  H     │
  └                    ┘ n
Making a polyestern HOOC–COOH + n HO–CH₂CH₂–OH → –[OC–COO–CH₂CH₂–O]ₙ– + 2n H₂Oethanedioic acid + ethanediol → polyester + water · two ester links, so two waters, per repeat unit
  1. Draw the acid with no –OH on either end: –OC–(middle)–CO–.
  2. Draw the diol with no H on either O: –O–CH₂CH₂–O–.
  3. Join them: acid part then diol part. Every C=O carbon should now be bonded to an O on one side.
  4. Add brackets, an extension bond through each bracket, and n.

How 2022 Jan Edexcel 2C Q7(e) marked the repeat unit — two marks

1. The right carbons from each monomer (three from that dicarboxylic acid, two from the diol) and the ester linkage 1

2. –OH lost from the dicarboxylic acid and –H lost from the diol 1

The scheme allowed a structure without extension bonds and ignored brackets and n. The marks are on the atoms, so get those right first. Still draw the brackets, extension bonds and n.

2022 June scheme on drawing the monomers: "REJECT OH--C once only". The bond must go from the carbon to the O, not to the H.
✗ HO–CH₂ drawn with the line joining H to C.
✓ H–O–CH₂: the oxygen is bonded to the carbon.

Biopolyesters (spec 4.50C). Some polyesters, called biopolyesters, are biodegradable: microorganisms can break them down. That matters because addition polymers such as poly(ethene) are inert and do not biodegrade. They last for centuries in landfill, and burning them can release toxic gases (module 16). A biodegradable polyester rots away after use, so it causes fewer disposal problems.

✗ "All polyesters are biodegradable." — only some: the biopolyesters.
✓ "Biopolyesters are biodegradable, so they are broken down by microorganisms and do not build up in landfill."

03Cheat sheet

The sentences behind most of the marks

Groups: alcohol –OH · carboxylic acid –COOH · ester –COO–. Draw every bond, including O–H.

Oxidation of ethanol: burning → CO₂ + H₂O; microbes + air → ethanoic acid (vinegar); acidified potassium dichromate(VI), heat → ethanoic acid, orange to green.

Hydration: ethene + steam, phosphoric acid, 300 °C, 60–70 atm. Fermentation: glucose, yeast enzymes, 30 °C, no air: C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂.

No air: oxygen would oxidise ethanol to ethanoic acid. 30 °C: colder is too slow, hotter denatures the enzymes.

Acids + metal → salt + hydrogen. Acids + carbonate → salt + water + CO₂. Salts are -oates.

Esters: acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water, concentrated sulfuric acid catalyst. Name: alcohol-yl, acid-oate. Fruity, volatile, flavourings and perfumes.

Polyester: dicarboxylic acid + diol → polyester + water, condensation polymerisation. Biopolyesters are biodegradable.

Fermentation must happen in the absence of air. Give the two different explanations the mark scheme accepts, and say why both lead to less ethanol.

Show answer

Route 1 — oxidation: oxygen in the air would oxidise the ethanol that has formed into ethanoic acid. That is exactly how wine left open turns into vinegar. The ethanol is made and then destroyed.

Route 2 — respiration: with oxygen, yeast respires aerobically and breaks glucose all the way down to carbon dioxide and water. No ethanol is made at all. Fermentation is the anaerobic route.

Either way, air means less ethanol: one route stops it forming, the other destroys it after it forms.

U2

Why is about 30 °C the best temperature for fermentation, when for most reactions hotter simply means faster?

Show answer

Fermentation is catalysed by enzymes, which are proteins.

Below 30 °C it behaves like any reaction: less kinetic energy, fewer successful collisions, a slow rate.

Well above 30 °C the enzymes' active sites change shape. They denature and stop working, so the rate collapses. That never happens with phosphoric acid in hydration, which is why hydration can run at 300 °C.

About 30 °C is the optimum: as fast as possible without denaturing the enzymes.

U3

One mark scheme rejected C₂H₅OH and wanted C₂H₆O. Another gave no mark for C₂H₆O. How can both be right?

Show answer

They were asking different things.

A molecular formula is just the total number of each atom: 2 C, 6 H, 1 O = C₂H₆O. C₂H₅OH groups the atoms to show structure, so it is not a molecular formula.

In an equation for making ethanol, the examiner wants to see that it is ethanol. C₂H₆O does not show an –OH group. The same atoms could be arranged as a different compound, with the O between the two carbons. C₂H₅OH shows the alcohol group.

Rule: molecular formula → count atoms. Equation or structural formula → show the –OH.

U4

A country has plenty of sugar cane but no crude oil. Another has an oil refinery and little farmland. Which method of making ethanol suits each, and why?

Show answer

Sugar-cane country → fermentation. Its raw material is renewable and local. The gentle conditions (30 °C, 1 atm) need little energy and cheap equipment. It accepts the downsides: slow batches and ethanol that must be distilled.

Oil-refinery country → hydration. Cracking gives ethene. Hydration is fast, continuous and gives pure ethanol. It accepts the downsides: high temperature and pressure cost energy, and crude oil is finite.

The exam version asks for one advantage and one disadvantage. Each must be a feature plus its consequence, not a bare "cheaper".

U5

In the ester name "ethyl ethanoate" the alcohol comes first. In the formula CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ the acid comes first. How do you avoid mixing them up?

Show answer

Tie each part to its ending. -yl always comes from the alcohol, and -oate always from the acid, just as the acid's salts are ethanoates.

In the formula, find the C=O. The carbon chain on the C=O side, including that carbon, is the acid part. The chain on the far side of the single-bonded O is the alcohol part.

CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃: C=O side has 2 C → ethanoate. Other side has 3 C → propyl. Name: propyl ethanoate. Made from propanol and ethanoic acid.

U6

Concentrated sulfuric acid is added when making an ester, and its amount is the same at the end. What is it doing, and why does the reaction still not turn all the acid and alcohol into ester?

Show answer

It is a catalyst. It speeds up the reaction but is not used up, so the number of moles of sulfuric acid does not change.

The reaction is reversible: acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water. As ester and water build up, the backward reaction speeds up until both rates are equal. That is dynamic equilibrium (module 15). A catalyst speeds up the forward and backward reactions equally, so it gets to equilibrium sooner but does not change how much ester is there.

U7

Poly(ethene) forms from ethene with nothing else made. A polyester forms with water as a by-product. Explain the difference.

Show answer

Addition polymerisation (module 16): the C=C double bond opens and the monomers join end to end. Every atom of every monomer ends up in the polymer, so nothing else is made.

Condensation polymerisation: each link forms when the –COOH of the acid reacts with the –OH of the diol, exactly like making an ester. That reaction gives out water, one molecule per ester link. The monomers need a functional group at both ends (a dicarboxylic acid and a diol) so the chain can keep growing.

U8

Ethanol, ethanoic acid and ethyl ethanoate are three colourless liquids. Describe tests to tell them apart, using only what is in this module.

Show answer

Add sodium carbonate solution or magnesium to each. Only ethanoic acid fizzes, because only the carboxylic acid reacts with carbonates and metals.

Warm the other two with acidified potassium dichromate(VI). Ethanol is oxidised, so the colour turns from orange to green. The ester does not change it.

Smell is a clue too: the ester has a strong fruity smell, and ethanoic acid smells of vinegar.
