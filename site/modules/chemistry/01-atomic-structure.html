02How it actually works

An atom is a tiny, heavy, positive nucleus (protons + neutrons) wrapped in shells of almost weightless electrons. Two numbers on the periodic table encode everything the exam asks.

The three particles and the two numbers

Spec 1.14, 1.15, 1.16 · 2021 Q1 · 2022 Q2 · 2023 Q1

ParticleRelative massChargeWhere
Proton1+1nucleus
Neutron10nucleus
Electron1/1836 (≈0)−1shells around the nucleus

Atomic number = number of protons (that alone — 2022's scheme ignored any mention of electrons). Mass number = protons + neutrons. So for ¹¹₅B: 5 protons, 11 − 5 = 6 neutrons, and — because atoms are neutral — 5 electrons. Every "how many X" question is one of those three moves.

An atom is the smallest particle of an element; a molecule is two or more atoms covalently bonded together. Isotopes are atoms of the same element (same protons) with different numbers of neutrons — same atomic number, different mass number. Because they have identical electron arrangements, isotopes have identical chemistry.

Relative atomic mass — the weighted average

Spec 1.16, 1.17 · 2023 Q9a

Ar is the mean mass of the natural mix of isotopes: Ar = Σ(isotope mass × % abundance) ÷ 100. Copper: (63 × 69.5 + 65 × 30.5) ÷ 100 = 63.61 → 63.6 to 3 sf.

Sanity check before moving on: the answer must sit between the isotope masses, closer to the more abundant one. It is why chlorine's Ar is 35.5 without any atom weighing 35.5.

Electronic configurations — the 2.8.8 counting rule

Spec 1.19 · 2023 Q1 · 2025 Q6a

Electrons fill shells from the inside: the first holds 2, the second 8, the third 8 (enough for the first 20 elements). Count the electrons (= protons for an atom) and deal them out:

  • Sodium, 11 electrons → 2.8.1
  • Phosphorus, 15 electrons → 2.8.5 (2025's one-marker)
  • Calcium, 20 electrons → 2.8.8.2

Reading the periodic table: group, period, ion

Spec 1.18, 1.20 – 1.22 · 2022 Q2b · 2023 Q1 · 2024 Q1

The table lists elements in order of atomic number; the layout is the electron structure:

  • Group (column) = number of outer-shell electrons. 2.8.4 → Group 4 — "because it has 4 electrons in its outer shell" is the reason mark.
  • Period (row) = number of occupied shells. 2.8.3 has three shells → Period 3. This is the one "many had absolutely no concept of": period counts the rows down, group counts across the top.
  • Group 8/0 (noble gases): full outer shells — unreactive. An element with a full outer shell has 0 electrons available to bond (2024's Q1c answer: 0, allow Group 8).

Ion charge from group: atoms react to reach a full outer shell. Metals in Groups 1/2/3 lose their outer electrons → 1+/2+/3+; non-metals in Groups 5/6/7 gain → 3−/2−/1−. Sodium 2.8.1 loses one → Na⁺, configuration 2.8. Write the charge with its size: +1, not "positive".

04Understanding it

U1

An atom has the electronic configuration 2.8.3. Without being told the element, state its group, its period, the charge on its ion, and — using a periodic table — its identity.

Show answer

Group 3 — three outer electrons. Period 3 — three occupied shells. Ion: loses the three outer electrons → 3+ (configuration 2.8).

Identity: 2 + 8 + 3 = 13 electrons = 13 protons → atomic number 13 → aluminium.

Same-numbers-different-meanings is the trap: "3" appears as both group and period here by coincidence. Group counts the outer electrons; period counts the shells. The 2023 cohort mixing them up is why this question keeps returning.

U2

Chlorine has two isotopes, ³⁵Cl (75%) and ³⁷Cl (25%). Explain why the two isotopes react identically, and calculate Ar.

Show answer

Identical chemistry: both have 17 protons and therefore 17 electrons in the same arrangement (2.8.7). Chemical behaviour is decided entirely by the electrons — the extra two neutrons in ³⁷Cl change only the mass.

Ar = (35 × 75 + 37 × 25) ÷ 100 = (2625 + 925) ÷ 100 = 35.5.

Check: between 35 and 37, closer to 35 (the abundant one). ✓

U3

Why does an atom of element X with 14 protons, 14 electrons and 15 neutrons have a mass number of 29 — and why are answers of 14, 15 and 28 each wrong in a different way?

Show answer

Mass number counts what has mass in the nucleus: protons + neutrons = 14 + 15 = 29.

The wrong answers (this was 2022's actual MCQ, distractors and all): 14 is the proton count — the atomic number, not the mass number. 15 is just the neutrons. 28 is protons + electrons — but electrons weigh ≈1/1836 of a proton, effectively nothing, and never contribute to the mass number.

U4

Sodium's ion is Na⁺ but its configuration is 2.8.1. Talk through what the atom loses, what the ion's configuration becomes, and why the exam refuses the answer "positive" for the charge.

Show answer

The single outer electron is lost (it is the easiest route to a full outer shell). The ion's configuration is 2.8 — eleven protons now hold ten electrons, so the net charge is +1.

"Positive" was ignored (scored nothing) in 2023 because it answers a different question — direction without magnitude. Mg loses two electrons and is also "positive", but its ion is 2+. The charge is a number with a sign: +1, 2+, 3−, written either way round, or as the symbol Na⁺.

U5

Element Y is in Group 8 (the noble gases). How many electrons does it have available for bonding, and what does that predict about its chemistry?

Show answer

Zero. A Group 8 element has a full outer shell — no tendency to lose, gain or share anything. (2024's answer line literally accepted "0".)

Prediction: unreactive — noble gases form almost no compounds, exist as single atoms, and are the exam's example of why full shells are the stable end-state that every other atom is scrambling towards by ionic or covalent bonding.

05Exam drill

D1

An atom of boron is represented as ¹¹₅B.

(a) State the atomic number and the mass number. (2)
(b) State the number of protons, neutrons and electrons in this atom. (3)

Show answer

(a) Atomic number 5 1; mass number 11 1

(b) 5 protons 1; 11 − 5 = 6 neutrons 1; 5 electrons (neutral atom) 1

D2

The diagram shows an atom with the electronic configuration 2.8.1.

(a) Name the part of the atom that contains the protons and neutrons. (1)
(b) Give the number of protons in this atom. (1)
(c) Give the group and the period that contain this element. (2)
(d) Give the charge on the ion formed from this atom. (1)

Show answer

(a) The nucleus 1

(b) 2 + 8 + 1 = 11 1

(c) Group 1 (one outer electron) 1; Period 3 (three occupied shells) 1

(d) +1 / 1+ / Na⁺ 1 — "positive" alone scores nothing

D3

An atom of element X contains 14 protons, 14 electrons and 15 neutrons.

(a) State the mass number of this atom. (1)
(b) Explain which group of the periodic table element X belongs to. (2)

Show answer

(a) 14 + 15 = 29 1

(b) Group 4 1; because the configuration is 2.8.4 — four electrons in the outer shell 1

D4

A sample of copper contains two isotopes: ⁶³Cu with relative abundance 69.5% and ⁶⁵Cu with relative abundance 30.5%.

(a) State what is meant by the term isotopes. (2)
(b) Calculate the relative atomic mass of this sample of copper. Give your answer to three significant figures. (3)

Show answer

(a) Atoms of the same element / with the same number of protons 1 with different numbers of neutrons 1

(b) (63 × 69.5) + (65 × 30.5) = 6361 1; ÷ 100 = 63.61 1; = 63.6 to 3 sf 1

D5

Write the electronic configurations of: (a) phosphorus (atomic number 15); (b) calcium (atomic number 20); (c) the chloride ion, Cl⁻ (chlorine's atomic number is 17). (3)

Show answer

(a) 2.8.5 1

(b) 2.8.8.2 1
