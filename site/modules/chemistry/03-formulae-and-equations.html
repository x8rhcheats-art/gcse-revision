The grammar of the subject. Few questions test it alone — instead it leaks marks in every other topic: a wrong formula, a missed balancing number, HBR instead of HBr, an equation with no state symbols. Fix it once, earn everywhere.

02How it actually works

Chemical formulae are not memorised one by one — they are built from ion charges. Equations are then balanced by counting atoms, never by editing formulae. Two rules, endlessly reused.

The ion charges you must know

Spec 1.38 · 2024 Q8a

The spec's list, arranged by how you remember it:

  • From the group number: Groups 1, 2, 3 → +1, +2, +3 (Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺). Groups 5, 6, 7 → −3, −2, −1 (N³⁻, O²⁻, Cl⁻).
  • Named metals the table can't tell you: Ag⁺, Zn²⁺, Cu²⁺, Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺, Pb²⁺ — silver is +1, zinc always +2, iron comes in two flavours (the Roman numeral in the name says which: iron(III) = Fe³⁺).
  • The molecular ions: H⁺, OH⁻ (hydroxide), NH₄⁺ (ammonium), NO₃⁻ (nitrate), CO₃²⁻ (carbonate), SO₄²⁻ (sulfate).

Building a formula: charges must cancel

Spec 1.39 · 2023 Q4b · 2024 Q8a · 2025 Q6b

Total positive charge = total negative charge. The crossing trick: each ion's charge number becomes the other ion's subscript.

  • Na⁺ + Cl⁻ → NaCl (1:1, nothing to write).
  • Mg²⁺ + Cl⁻ → MgCl₂; Mg²⁺ + N³⁻ → Mg₃N₂ (2024's table answers).
  • NH₄⁺ + CO₃²⁻ → (NH₄)₂CO₃ — brackets around a molecular ion whenever it is multiplied; (NH₄)₂SO₄ likewise.

Naming runs backwards: MgO = magnesium oxide (spelling counts); NH₄Cl = ammonium chloride. -ide = just the element; -ate = element + oxygen (sulfate, nitrate, carbonate).

Elements that go around in pairs: the diatomic molecules H₂, N₂, O₂, F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂. In any equation, elemental hydrogen is H₂, chlorine Cl₂ — writing a lone Cl was 2023's "fascinating variations" complaint at the electrodes, and it is wrong everywhere else too.

Balancing: change the big numbers, never the formulae

Spec 1.25 · 2024 Q5a · 2025 Q4b, Q6d · 2023 Q9b

A chemical equation is an atom ledger — same count of every element on both sides. The method:

  1. Write correct formulae first. These are now frozen — balancing never edits a subscript, only the coefficients in front.
  2. Count each element, fix imbalances one element at a time, recount after each change. Save lone elements (O₂, C) for last — their coefficient can be set freely.
  3. Check the whole thing once more. Half the "did not include balancing numbers" losses are equations abandoned one step early.

The school's set pieces: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO · 2Al + Fe₂O₃ → Al₂O₃ + 2Fe · 2CuO + C → 2Cu + CO₂ · Ca₃(PO₄)₂ + 8C → Ca₃P₂ + 8CO (count the oxygens: 8 on each side) · Ca₃P₂ + 6H₂O → 3Ca(OH)₂ + 2PH₃ · ethane's combustion 2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O. Schemes usually give M1 for formulae, M2 for balancing — and "ALLOW multiples", so doubling everything is fine; fractions usually accepted too.

State symbols — and which one is the actual test

Spec 1.25 · 2021 Q8a · 2022 Q3b · 2024 Q10a · 2025 Q4b

(s) solid · (l) pure liquid · (g) gas · (aq) dissolved in water. The papers' favourite: CaCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g).

Three decisions carry the marks:

  • Water is (l) — it is the liquid itself, not dissolved in anything.
  • Acids and dissolved salts are (aq) — 2024's commentary: many wrote zinc nitrate as (s) or (l); it is a soluble salt in solution. Check the solubility rules when unsure.
  • Precipitates are (s) — insoluble products are solid even though they formed in solution: Al(OH)₃(s), PbBr₂(s).

04Understanding it

U1

Why is magnesium chloride MgCl₂ but sodium chloride just NaCl? Explain from the charges, and state what the "crossing trick" is actually doing.

Show answer

Compounds are electrically neutral. Mg²⁺ carries two positives, and each Cl⁻ only one negative — so it takes two chloride ions to cancel one magnesium: MgCl₂. Na⁺ and Cl⁻ cancel one-for-one: NaCl.

The crossing trick (charge of one ion becomes subscript of the other) is just solving that neutrality equation quickly: it finds the smallest counts that make total + equal total −. That is why the result must always be simplified (Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ "cross" to Mg₂O₂ → reduce to MgO).

U2

A student balances Mg + O₂ → MgO by rewriting it as Mg + O₂ → MgO₂. Chemically, what has the student done wrong — and what is the legal fix?

Show answer

They have invented a different substance. MgO₂ is not magnesium oxide — the formula of a compound is fixed by its ion charges (Mg²⁺, O²⁻ → MgO) and is not negotiable during balancing. Editing subscripts changes what reacts, not how much.

The legal fix uses coefficients only: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO — four atoms of magnesium-and-oxygen on each side, formulae untouched. (With state symbols, 2025's version: 2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s).)

U3

In Ca₃(PO₄)₂ + 8C → Ca₃P₂ + 8CO, show the oxygen audit that makes 8 the right coefficient for both carbon and CO.

Show answer

Left side oxygen: (PO₄)₂ = 2 × 4 = 8 oxygens. Calcium (3) and phosphorus (2) already match Ca₃P₂ — so all 8 oxygens must leave inside CO, one each: 8CO. Eight CO needs eight carbons: 8C.

Recount: Ca 3=3, P 2=2, O 8=8, C 8=8. ✓ The general skill: brackets multiply everything inside, and a systematic per-element count beats staring. This equation appeared two years running (2024, 2025) — the school likes it because the bracket-counting is the whole test.

U4

The same reaction — zinc + nitric acid → zinc nitrate + hydrogen — gets four state symbols. Give them, and explain the two that students got wrong in 2024.

Show answer

Zn(s) + 2HNO₃(aq) → Zn(NO₃)₂(aq) + H₂(g).

The two casualties: zinc nitrate, widely written (s) or (l). It is a soluble salt (all nitrates are soluble) sitting dissolved in the solution it formed in — (aq). It only becomes (s) after you deliberately crystallise it. And the acid: dilute acid is a solution, always (aq), never (l).

The habit: for every substance ask where is it, physically, in the flask? — not what it could be in a bottle somewhere.

U5

Ammonia reacts with sulfuric acid to give ammonium sulfate. Build the product's formula from its ions, then write and balance the full equation.

Show answer

Formula: NH₄⁺ and SO₄²⁻ — two ammoniums cancel one sulfate → (NH₄)₂SO₄, brackets because the molecular ion is doubled.

Equation: the product contains two NH₄ groups, so two ammonia molecules are needed: 2NH₃ + H₂SO₄ → (NH₄)₂SO₄. Count: N 2=2, H (6+2)=8=8, S 1=1, O 4=4. ✓

(2023 asked exactly this; state symbols were ignored even if wrong there — but the balancing 2 was required.)

05Exam drill

D1

Complete the table by giving the formula of each compound: sodium chloride; magnesium chloride; magnesium nitride; ammonium carbonate; iron(III) hydroxide; aluminium sulfate. (6)

Show answer

NaCl 1; MgCl₂ 1; Mg₃N₂ 1; (NH₄)₂CO₃ 1; Fe(OH)₃ 1; Al₂(SO₄)₃ 1

Wrong case or missing brackets is penalised — capitals and subscripts are part of the answer.

D2

Add the state symbols to complete the equation for calcium carbonate reacting with dilute hydrochloric acid:

CaCO₃(......) + 2HCl(......) → CaCl₂(......) + H₂O(......) + CO₂(......) (2)

Show answer

CaCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) 1 → CaCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g) 1

Reactants one mark, products the other. Water is (l); the two dissolved substances are (aq). This exact equation has appeared twice.

D3

Write balanced chemical equations for:

(a) aluminium + iron(III) oxide → iron + aluminium oxide (2)
(b) copper(II) oxide heated with carbon → copper + carbon dioxide (2)
(c) ethane (C₂H₆) burning completely in air (2)

Show answer

(a) Correct formulae: Al, Fe₂O₃, Fe, Al₂O₃ 1; balanced: 2Al + Fe₂O₃ → 2Fe + Al₂O₃ 1

(b) Formulae: CuO, C, Cu, CO₂ 1; balanced: 2CuO + C → 2Cu + CO₂ 1

(c) Products CO₂ + H₂O 1; balanced: 2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O 1 — multiples and halves (C₂H₆ + 3½O₂…) are accepted

D4

(a) Complete the equation: Ca₃(PO₄)₂ + .......C → Ca₃P₂ + .......CO (1)
(b) Calcium phosphide reacts with water to form calcium hydroxide and phosphine (PH₃). Write the balanced equation. (2)

Show answer

(a) Ca₃(PO₄)₂ + 8C → Ca₃P₂ + 8CO 1 — audit the 8 oxygens inside the bracket

(b) Formulae: Ca₃P₂, H₂O, Ca(OH)₂, PH₃ 1; balanced: Ca₃P₂ + 6H₂O → 3Ca(OH)₂ + 2PH₃ 1 — check H: 12 = 6 + 6 ✓

D5

(a) Name the compounds NH₄Cl and MgO. (2)
(b) Give the formula of ammonium sulfate, and the balanced equation for its formation from ammonia and sulfuric acid. (2)

Show answer

(a) Ammonium chloride 1; magnesium oxide 1 — spelling must be exact

(b) (NH₄)₂SO₄ 1; 2NH₃ + H₂SO₄ → (NH₄)₂SO₄ 1
