There are no school-paper questions on alkenes or polymers, so all the intel here is from real Edexcel papers. The same five jobs come up again and again: define unsaturated, do the bromine water test, draw C₄H₈ isomers, draw a repeat unit, and discuss disposal.

02How it actually works

Alkenes are hydrocarbons with one carbon–carbon double bond. That double bond is the whole story. It lets alkenes add bromine, which is the test for them. It also lets thousands of alkene molecules join into a polymer. Alkanes, cracking and alkane isomers are in module 10. Ethene + steam → ethanol and condensation polymers are in module 17. This module links to them rather than repeating them.

The C=C functional group and the general formula

Spec 4.3, 4.23, 4.24 · 2023 Edexcel 1C Q4(a)(i), (b)(i)

Alkenes are a homologous series of hydrocarbons that all contain the functional group C=C, and all fit the general formula CₙH₂ₙ.

General formula of the alkenesCₙH₂ₙn = number of carbon atoms (n ≥ 2) · twice as many H as C · alkanes are CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (module 10)

A functional group is the atom or group of atoms that decides how a compound reacts. For alkenes it is the >C=C< double bond.

A homologous series is a family of compounds. 2023 Edexcel 1C Q4(a)(i) gave one mark each for any two of these:

  1. the same general formula
  2. the same functional group
  3. each member differs from the next by CH₂
  4. similar chemical properties (similar reactions)
  5. a trend in physical properties, such as boiling point

Every alkene has the empirical formula CH₂. CₙH₂ₙ always has twice as many hydrogen atoms as carbon atoms, so the simplest ratio is always 1 : 2. Ethene C₂H₄, propene C₃H₆ and butene C₄H₈ all simplify to CH₂.

The 2023 scheme rejected "same / similar physical properties".
✗ "They have similar physical properties."
✓ "They show a trend in physical properties, such as boiling point increasing as the chain gets longer."

Unsaturated hydrocarbon — three separate marks

Spec 4.1, 4.25 · 2023 Jan Edexcel 2C Q4(b)(ii) · 2023 Edexcel 1C Q4(c)(ii) · 2022 Jan Edexcel 2C Q1(a)

An alkene is a hydrocarbon because it contains hydrogen and carbon atoms only, and it is unsaturated because it contains a C=C double bond.

"Explain why ethene is an unsaturated hydrocarbon" — three marks

1. It contains hydrogen and carbon atoms 1

2. only 1

3. It contains a carbon–carbon double bond, C=C 1

Saturated means single C–C bonds only. Alkanes are saturated (module 10). An unsaturated molecule could take in more atoms, because the double bond can open up. That is exactly what happens with bromine.

The scheme rejected "carbon and hydrogen molecules". The mark for "only" is lost if the first mark is lost.
✗ "It is made of carbon and hydrogen molecules and has a double bond."
✗ "It contains carbon and hydrogen and a double bond." — no "only", so 2 of 3.
✓ "It contains hydrogen and carbon atoms only, and it has a C=C double bond."

Ethene's bonding — the dot-and-cross diagram

Spec 1.46, 4.23 · 2023 Edexcel 1C Q4(a)(ii)

In ethene the two carbon atoms share two pairs of electrons — that is the double bond — and each carbon shares one pair with each of its two hydrogen atoms.

Start from the displayed formula and swap every line for one shared pair of electrons. The single C–H lines become one pair each. The double line between the carbons becomes two pairs. What to draw in the exam:

  1. Two C atoms side by side, with four electrons (two pairs) in the overlap between them.
  2. Each C overlapping with two H atoms, one shared pair in each overlap.
  3. No other electrons on the carbons. Each C now has 8 outer electrons, and each H has 2.

The scheme said "REJECT if non-bonding electrons shown on carbon".
✗ An extra lone pair drawn on each carbon.
✓ Two shared pairs C to C, one shared pair per C–H, and nothing else. Dots and crosses can be in any combination.

Isomers of C₄H₈

Spec 4.3, 4.5, 4.26 · 2024 Edexcel 1C Q8(a) · 2023 Jan Edexcel 1C Q7(e) · 2022 Jan Edexcel 1C Q8(a)(iv)

Isomers are compounds with the same molecular formula but different structural (displayed) formulae. The definition is two marks, and both halves are needed.

Isomers — two marks

Look for the double bond, not just the formula.

Alkenes + bromine — an addition reaction

Spec 4.6, 4.27 · 2024 Edexcel 1C Q8(b) · 2022 Edexcel 1C Q5(b)

Bromine adds across the C=C double bond: the double bond opens, one bromine atom joins each carbon, and a single product — a dibromoalkane — forms.

Ethene + bromineC₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ → CH₂BrCH₂Br · product: 1,2-dibromoethane · room temperature, no UV needed
   H  H                    H  H
   │  │                    │  │
H──C══C──H  +  Br──Br → H──C──C──H
                           │  │
                           Br Br
ethene        bromine   1,2-dibromoethane
  1. The C=C double bond opens to a single C–C bond.
  2. Each carbon now has a spare bond, so one Br atom adds to each carbon.
  3. Two reactants → one product. Nothing is lost. That is what "addition" means.
  4. The product is saturated, so it cannot add any more bromine.
Propene + bromineC₃H₆ + Br₂ → C₃H₆Br₂CH₂=CHCH₃ + Br₂ → CH₂BrCHBrCH₃ · product: 1,2-dibromopropane

The "1,2" says the two Br atoms sit on carbons 1 and 2, the two that had the double bond. But-1-ene gives 1,2-dibromobutane, C₄H₈Br₂. The 2024 question gave C₄H₈Br₂ and asked for the reaction type: addition.

✗ "Ethene + bromine → bromoethane + hydrogen bromide." — that is the alkane reaction.
✗ "Substitution" or "displacement" for an alkene with bromine.
✓ "Addition: C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂, 1,2-dibromoethane, the only product."

The bromine water test — alkane or alkene?

Spec 4.28 · 2023 Edexcel 1C Q4(c)(iii) · 2023 Jan Edexcel 2C Q4(b)(iii) · 2022 Jan Edexcel 2C Q1(b)

Shake the hydrocarbon with bromine water: an alkene decolourises it (orange to colourless), an alkane leaves it orange.

Add bromine water to…ObservationWhy
An alkene (e.g. ethene)Orange → colourlessBromine adds across the C=C, so the bromine is used up
An alkane (e.g. ethane)Stays orange — no changeSaturated: no C=C to add to. Alkanes only react with bromine in UV light, by substitution (module 10)

"Describe a test to show it is unsaturated" — two marks

1. Add bromine water (and shake) 1

2. It turns from orange to colourless — it is decolourised 1

The second mark depends on the first: no bromine, no colour mark.

2023 Jan 2C scheme: "IGNORE clear", "NOT just `changes colour'" and "If reaction takes place in presence of uv, then score M1 only".
✗ "Add bromine and it goes clear."
✗ "Bromine water changes colour."
✗ "Add bromine water and put it in UV light."
✓ "Add bromine water. It turns from orange to colourless."

"Clear" means see-through. Bromine water is already clear — it is clear orange. The word you need is colourless.

Classifying organic reactions

Spec 4.6 · 2024 Edexcel 1C Q8(b)

Every organic reaction on this course is one of three types: substitution, addition or combustion — and the reactant's bonds tell you which.

TypeWhat happensExample
SubstitutionOne atom is swapped for another. Two products.CH₄ + Br₂ → CH₃Br + HBr (UV)
AdditionTwo molecules join into one. The C=C opens.C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂ · alkene + steam → alcohol (module 17) · polymerisation
CombustionBurning in oxygenC₂H₄ + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 2H₂O

A quick rule: saturated molecules (alkanes) do substitution; unsaturated ones (alkenes) do addition. Everything with C and H can burn. The 2024 multiple choice offered addition, combustion, decomposition and substitution for but-1-ene + bromine. The C=C and the single product point to addition.

Addition polymers — monomer, polymer, repeat unit

Spec 4.44 · 2024 Edexcel 1C Q1(a) · 2023 Edexcel 1C Q4(b)(ii)

An addition polymer is made by joining up many small molecules called monomers; each monomer's C=C opens and the monomers add to each other in a long chain.

  1. The monomer is a small molecule with a C=C — an alkene, such as ethene.
  2. The double bond in each monomer opens.
  3. Each monomer joins to the next through those spare bonds, thousands of times over.
  4. The result is one very long saturated chain — the polymer. Nothing else is made.

Part of a poly(ethene) chain, made from three ethene molecules:

  H  H  H  H  H  H
  │  │  │  │  │  │
──C──C──C──C──C──C──
  │  │  │  │  │  │
  H  H  H  H  H  H

The chain is just the same two-carbon piece over and over. That piece is the repeat unit. It is drawn in brackets, with an extension bond sticking out each side and n after the bracket. n is a large number — thousands.

Making poly(ethene)n CH₂=CH₂ → –[CH₂–CH₂]ₙ–n ethene monomers → poly(ethene) · the repeat unit has the same atoms as one monomer

So in the 2024 choose-from-the-box question, the substance that can form a polymer was ethene — it is the only one with a C=C. Methane cannot: it has no double bond.

✗ "Poly(ethene) contains double bonds." — the double bonds were used up making the chain.
✓ "Poly(ethene) is saturated. It does not decolourise bromine water."

Working backwards — monomer from repeat unit, and polymer sums

Spec 4.46 · 2023 Jan Edexcel 1C Q6(a), (c), (e) · 2022 Jan Edexcel 1C Q8(d)(i)

To find the monomer, take the two main-chain carbons of the repeat unit, put a double bond between them, and remove the brackets, n and extension bonds.

  1. Find the two carbons in the main chain inside the brackets.
  2. Join them with a C=C double bond.
  3. Keep every atom or group that was on them.
  4. Delete the extension bonds, brackets and n. Name it.

In 2023 Jan Edexcel 1C Q6(a) the monomer had one Cl, three H and a C=C: chloroethene. Chloroethane has no double bond. Chloropropene has three carbons.

2022 Jan 1C Q8(d)(i) asked for the chain made from two propene molecules. The scheme wanted two carbons each with 2 H, two carbons each with 1 H and 1 CH₃, and "nothing attached to the joining bonds" — the extension bonds at the ends stay empty.

Number of monomers in a polymernumber of monomers = Mᵣ of polymer ÷ Mᵣ of monomerthe repeat unit has the same atoms as the monomer, so its Mᵣ is the same

Worked example — how many chloroethene units?

A poly(chloroethene) molecule has Mᵣ = 2 490 000. [Aᵣ: C = 12, H = 1, Cl = 35.5]

  1. Chloroethene is C₂H₃Cl.
  2. Mᵣ = (2 × 12) + (3 × 1) + 35.5 = 62.5
  3. Number of monomers = 2 490 000 ÷ 62.5 = 39 840, about 40 000.

That was 2023 Jan Edexcel 1C Q6(c): Mᵣ of chloroethene 1; 2 490 000 ÷ 62.5 = 39 840 1.

An addition polymer has the same empirical formula as its monomer, because every monomer atom ends up in the chain. Poly(ethene) is CH₂, PTFE is CF₂, poly(chloroethene) is C₂H₃Cl. In 2023 Jan 1C Q6(e), a polymer with 10 600 C, 10 600 H and 31 800 Cl atoms gave a ratio of 1 : 1 : 3, so the empirical formula was CHCl₃.

Disposing of addition polymers

Spec 4.47 · 2024 Edexcel 1C Q8(c)(ii) · 2023 Jan Edexcel 1C Q6(d) · 2022 Jan Edexcel 1C Q8(d)(ii)

Addition polymers are inert and do not biodegrade, so they last in landfill for hundreds of years; burning them gets rid of them but releases toxic gases and carbon dioxide.

Landfill, as a chain:

  1. Addition polymers are inert — unreactive. Their long chains are saturated, like alkanes.
  2. Microorganisms cannot break them down, so they are non-biodegradable.
  3. So they stay in landfill for a very long time, and landfill sites fill up.

Burning, as a chain:

  1. Burning releases heat energy, which can be used — the upside.
  2. But it produces toxic gases: carbon monoxide from incomplete combustion, and hydrogen chloride from burning poly(chloroethene).
  3. It also produces carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas, which adds to global warming / climate change.

"Discuss the problems of landfill and burning" — four marks

From 2023 Jan Edexcel 1C Q6(d), any four of:

Polymers remain in landfill indefinitely / landfill sites run out 1

because they are inert / do not biodegrade 1

✗ "Burning plastics damages the ozone layer." — capped at 3 of 4.
✗ "Landfill causes global warming." — ignored.
✓ Landfill: "does not biodegrade, stays for hundreds of years, sites fill up". Burning: "toxic gases such as carbon monoxide, and CO₂, a greenhouse gas".

04Understanding it

U1

Ethene decolourises bromine water in seconds at room temperature. Ethane leaves it orange. Why the difference, when both are hydrocarbons with two carbons?

Show answer

Ethene has a C=C double bond. The double bond can open, so bromine simply adds on — one Br to each carbon — with no other bond needing to break. The bromine is used up, so the orange colour goes.

Ethane is saturated. It has no double bond to open. To react, a strong C–H bond must be broken and an H swapped for a Br. That needs the energy of UV light. In the dark at room temperature nothing happens, so the bromine water stays orange.

That is why the test works: it is really a test for the C=C.

U2

Why do all alkenes fit CₙH₂ₙ — and why can a compound fit CₙH₂ₙ and still not be an alkene?

Show answer

An alkane chain CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ is "full". Making one C=C bond means each of two carbons uses an extra bond on its neighbour instead of on a hydrogen. So two H atoms are lost: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ − 2H = CₙH₂ₙ.

Joining the two ends of a chain into a ring also uses up two bonds, so it also loses two H atoms. A four-carbon ring is C₄H₈ — the same as butene — but it has single bonds only. It is saturated, and it does not decolourise bromine water.

That is exactly the trick in 2023 Jan 1C Q7(b) and 2022 Jan 1C Q8(a)(ii). The formula alone is not enough. Look for the double bond.

U3

Propene has three carbons. Why does the poly(propene) repeat unit have only two carbons in the main chain?

Show answer

Only the carbons in the C=C take part in polymerisation. When the double bond opens, those two carbons get a spare bond each, and those are the bonds that join to the next monomer.

The third carbon, in the CH₃ group, was only joined by a single bond. Nothing happens to it. So it stays as a side branch, hanging off every other carbon of the chain.

Rule: the main chain of any addition polymer is built only from the old C=C carbons. Everything else becomes a side group.

U4

Poly(ethene) is made from ethene. Would poly(ethene) decolourise bromine water? Explain.

Show answer

No. Every C=C in the ethene was used up making the chain — each one opened to link to its neighbours. The polymer has single C–C bonds only, so it is saturated, just like a very long alkane.

The name "poly(ethene)" only tells you which monomer it was made from. It does not mean the polymer still contains ethene or double bonds.

U5

Why does addition polymerisation make only one product, and why does the polymer have the same empirical formula as the monomer?

Show answer

In an addition reaction, molecules join together and nothing is removed. Each monomer's double bond opens and the monomer adds onto the chain whole. No small molecule like water is released. So the polymer is the only product.

Because every atom of every monomer ends up in the chain, the polymer is just n copies of the monomer's atoms. The ratio of atoms is the same, so the empirical formula is the same: poly(ethene) is CH₂, like ethene.

Condensation polymers (module 17) are different: two monomers join and water is released too.

U6

Why are addition polymers such a disposal problem in landfill, when paper and food waste rot away?

Show answer

Addition polymers are long saturated chains — chemically they are like enormous alkanes. They are inert: they do not react with water, air or most chemicals.

Microorganisms in the soil have no way to break them down, so they are non-biodegradable. Paper and food are broken down by microorganisms; a plastic bag is not.

So the polymer stays in the landfill for hundreds of years, and the sites fill up. The same inertness that makes plastics useful — they do not rot or corrode — is what makes them hard to get rid of.

U7

Burning poly(ethene) and burning poly(chloroethene) both release toxic gases, but not quite the same ones. Explain.

Show answer

Poly(ethene) contains only C and H. With plenty of oxygen it gives CO₂ and water. Burnt in limited air — as in most fires — incomplete combustion gives carbon monoxide, which is toxic, and soot.

Poly(chloroethene) also contains chlorine. When it burns, the chlorine ends up as hydrogen chloride, a toxic, acidic gas, as well as CO and CO₂.

Both release carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas. The 2023 Jan scheme allowed CO or HCl as the named toxic gas.

U8

But-1-ene and but-2-ene are isomers. What is actually different about them, and why is there no "but-3-ene"?

Show answer

Both are C₄H₈ — same molecular formula. The difference is the position of the C=C. In but-1-ene it is between carbons 1 and 2 (at the end of the chain). In but-2-ene it is between carbons 2 and 3 (in the middle). Different arrangement, so different structural formulae — that makes them isomers.

You always number from the end nearer the double bond. A double bond "between carbons 3 and 4" is the same as one between carbons 1 and 2 counted from the other end. So "but-3-ene" is just but-1-ene written backwards.

Both still add bromine: but-1-ene gives 1,2-dibromobutane, but-2-ene gives 2,3-dibromobutane. Both are C₄H₈Br₂.
