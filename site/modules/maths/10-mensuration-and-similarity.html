02How it actually works

Mensuration means measuring: lengths, areas and volumes. Area is length × length, so it is measured in square units. Volume is length × length × length, in cubic units. Similarity builds on exactly that idea: if every length is multiplied by k, areas are multiplied by k2 and volumes by k3. Compound measures (speed, density, pressure) are one quantity divided by another.

Units of length, area and volume

Spec 4.9A, 4.10F

An area conversion squares the length conversion, and a volume conversion cubes it.

1 m = 100 cm, so 1 m2 = 100 × 100 = 10 000 cm2 and 1 m3 = 100 × 100 × 100 = 1 000 000 cm3.

Areas of flat shapes

Spec 4.9B–D · 2016 Edexcel SAM 1H Q10

Every area formula comes from the rectangle: base × perpendicular height.

Triangle and parallelogram — memorisetriangle = ½ × base × height  ·  parallelogram = base × heightthe height is perpendicular to the base, never the slanted side
Area of a trapezium — on the formula sheetA = ½(a + b)ha and b are the parallel sides · h is the perpendicular distance between them
  1. Compound shape: split it into rectangles, triangles, trapezia and parts of circles, or take a small shape away from a large one.
  2. Find any missing length first, often with Pythagoras.
  3. Add or subtract the areas. Keep full calculator values until the end.

Worked example: trapezium with parallel sides 7 cm and 11 cm, 5 cm apart

A = ½ × (7 + 11) × 5 = 45 cm2

Circles and semicircles

Spec 4.9E · 2018 Edexcel May 1H Q6

The circumference is π × diameter and the area is π × radius squared.

Circle — memoriseC = πd = 2πr  ·  A = πr2not on the formula sheet · halve the diameter before squaring

A semicircle's perimeter is half the circumference plus the diameter, because the straight edge is part of the boundary.

Worked example (2018 1H Q6, numbers changed): MON is a diameter of a circle, P is on the circle, MP = 4.5 cm, PN = 8.8 cm and angle MPN = 90°. Work out the circumference.

MN = √97.69 = 9.8838…   (M1)

= 31.050… = 31.1 cm (3 s.f.)   (A1)

Worked example: perimeter of a semicircle of diameter 12 cm

½ × π × 12 + 12 = 6π + 12 = 30.8 cm (3 s.f.)

Arcs and sectors

Spec 4.9A (Higher) · 2016 Edexcel Jan 4H Q24 · 2017 Edexcel Jan 3HR Q21 (old spec)

A sector with angle θ is the fraction θ/360 of the whole circle, so its arc and area are that fraction of the circumference and the area.

Arc length — memorisearc = (θ/360) × 2πrθ in degrees · radians are not in the spec · not on the formula sheet
Sector area — memorisesector area = (θ/360) × πr2perimeter of a sector = arc + 2r
  1. Write down θ/360 as a fraction.
  2. Arc: fraction × 2πr. Area: fraction × πr2.
  3. Perimeter: add the two radii to the arc.
  4. Working backwards from an area: set the formula equal to the area and solve for r2, then r.

Worked example 1: sector with radius 9 cm and angle 80°

Arc = (80/360) × 2π × 9 = 4π = 12.57 cm

Area = (80/360) × π × 81 = 18π = 56.5 cm2

Perimeter = 4π + 9 + 9 = 30.6 cm

Worked example 2: a sector has angle 72° and area 45π cm2. Work out its perimeter.

(72/360) × πr2 = 45π, so r2 = 45 × 360 ÷ 72 = 225   (M1)

r = 15   (A1)

Arc = (72/360) × 2π × 15 = 6π   (M1)

Perimeter = 6π + 15 + 15   (M1) = 48.849… = 48.8 cm   (A1)

✗ Giving the arc length when the question asks for the perimeter. The two radii are part of the boundary.
✗ Using 2πr for the area, or πr2 for the arc. Area has r2 because it is measured in square units.

Prisms and cylinders

Spec 4.10C–E · 2016 Edexcel SAM 2H Q3 · 2018 Edexcel May 2H Q6(a)

A prism has the same cross-section all the way through, so its volume is the area of that cross-section times its length.

Volume of a prism — on the formula sheetV = area of cross-section × lengthcuboid, triangular prism, trapezium prism, cylinder
Cylinder — on the formula sheetV = πr2h  ·  curved surface area = 2πrhtotal surface area = 2πrh + 2πr2, adding the two circular ends
  1. Find the shape that is the same all the way through: the cross-section.
  2. Work out its area.
  3. Multiply by the length (the distance between the two end faces).
  4. Surface area: add the area of every face. For a cylinder, the curved surface unrolls into a rectangle 2πr long and h high.

Worked example: cylinder of radius 4 cm and height 10 cm

V = π × 42 × 10 = 160π = 503 cm3 (3 s.f.)

Curved surface = 2π × 4 × 10 = 80π · two ends = 2 × π × 16 = 32π

Total surface area = 112π = 352 cm2 (3 s.f.)

Pyramids and cones

Spec 4.10A (Higher), 4.10E · 2017 Edexcel Jan 4HR Q19 (old spec) · 2016 Edexcel SAM 1H Q19

A pyramid or cone holds exactly one third of the prism with the same base and height.

Volume of a pyramid — memoriseV = ⅓ × base area × perpendicular heightNOT on the formula sheet · the height is from the apex straight down to the base
Cone — on the formula sheetV = ⅓πr2h  ·  curved surface area = πrll = slant height · h = vertical height · l2 = r2 + h2
  1. Sketch the right-angled triangle inside the cone: radius r, height h, slant height l.
  2. Use Pythagoras to get whichever of r, h and l is missing.
  3. Volume uses the vertical height h. Curved surface uses the slant height l.
  4. Total surface area of a solid cone = πrl + πr2 (curved surface plus the base).

Worked example: cone with base radius 5 cm and height 12 cm

l = √(52 + 122) = √169 = 13 cm

V = ⅓ × π × 25 × 12 = 100π = 314 cm3

Curved surface = π × 5 × 13 = 65π · total = 65π + 25π = 90π cm2

A square-based pyramid with base 6 cm and height 10 cm: V = ⅓ × 36 × 10 = 120 cm3.

✗ Using the slant height in ⅓πr2h. The volume needs the vertical height.
✗ Forgetting the ⅓ for a pyramid: that gives the volume of a prism.
✗ Total surface area = πrl only. That is the curved part. A solid cone has a circular base too.

Spheres and hemispheres

Spec 4.10A (Higher) · 2018 Edexcel May 2H Q11 · 2015 Edexcel Jan 3HR Q18 (old spec)

A sphere's volume depends on r3 and its surface area on r2; a hemisphere is half of each, plus a flat circle if it is solid.

Sphere — on the formula sheetV = (4/3)πr3  ·  surface area = 4πr2hemisphere: V = (2/3)πr3 · curved surface 2πr2 · solid hemisphere total 3πr2

Worked examples

Sphere, radius 6 cm: V = (4/3) × π × 216 = 288π = 905 cm3 · SA = 4π × 36 = 144π = 452 cm2

Solid hemisphere, radius 5 cm: V = (2/3) × π × 125 = 262 cm3 · total SA = 2π × 25 + π × 25 = 75π = 236 cm2

✗ Putting the diameter into the formula. 2015 3HR Q18 gave a diameter of 18.6 cm: r = 9.3.
✗ Hemisphere surface = 2πr2 for a solid hemisphere. Add the flat circle: 3πr2.

Frustums and composite solids

Spec 4.10 · 2018 Edexcel May 1H Q16

A frustum is a large cone with a similar small cone cut off the top, so its volume is the large cone minus the small cone.

  1. Find the small cone's dimensions from the similar triangles (or the scale factor).
  2. Volume of frustum = volume of large cone − volume of small cone.
  3. Composite solids (a cone on a hemisphere, a cylinder with a hemisphere on top): add the volumes. For surface area, only count faces on the outside.

Worked example: a cone of radius 6 cm and height 16 cm has the top 8 cm cut off

The small cone is similar with scale factor 8/16 = ½, so its radius is 3 cm.

V = ⅓π(62 × 16) − ⅓π(32 × 8) = ⅓π(576 − 72) = 168π = 528 cm3 (3 s.f.)

Check with the volume scale factor: the small cone is (½)3 = ⅛ of the large one, so the frustum is ⅞ of 192π = 168π.

Exact answers and algebraic volumes

Spec 4.10 · 2017 Edexcel Jan 4HR Q19 · 2016 Edexcel Jan 3H Q19 (old spec)

"Give your answer as a multiple of π" or "in terms of π" means leave π as a symbol and do not use the π button.

  1. Write every formula with π in it.
  2. If both sides of an equation contain π, divide it out.
  3. Solve for the unknown, then write the answer as a number × π.

Worked example: a cylinder has height equal to twice its radius and volume 54π cm3. Find the radius.

πr2(2r) = 54π, so 2r3 = 54 and r3 = 27

r = 3 cm

Similar shapes: lengths

Spec 4.11A (Foundation) · 2018 Edexcel May 2H Q6(b) · 2017 Edexcel Jan 4HR Q14 (old spec)

Similar shapes have equal angles and every length multiplied by the same scale factor k.

Length scale factor — memorisek = new length ÷ corresponding old lengthuse one pair of matching sides to find k, then multiply or divide every other length by it
  1. Match the corresponding sides. Redraw the two shapes the same way round if they are nested or flipped.
  2. Find k from a pair of lengths you know in both shapes.
  3. Big from small: × k. Small from big: ÷ k.
  4. For nested triangles (a line parallel to one side), use the whole side of the big triangle, not the extra piece.

Worked example: in triangle ABC, D is on AB and E is on AC with DE parallel to BC. AD = 4 cm, AB = 10 cm, DE = 6 cm. Find BC.

Triangles ADE and ABC are similar (corresponding angles are equal). k = 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5

BC = 6 × 2.5 = 15 cm

✗ Using DB = 6 as the big side. The big triangle's side is AB, the whole length.
✗ Adding instead of multiplying: "AB is 6 more than AD, so BC is 6 more than DE". Similar means multiply.

Similar shapes: area and volume

Spec 4.11A–C (Higher) · 2016 Edexcel SAM 2H Q18 · 2018 Edexcel May 1H Q16

If lengths are multiplied by k, areas are multiplied by k2 and volumes by k3.

Area and volume scale factors — memoriselength × k  ·  area × k2  ·  volume × k3surface area and cross-section are areas · mass and capacity scale like volume
  1. Find the scale factor you are given (length, area or volume).
  2. Turn it into the length scale factor: square root an area ratio, cube root a volume ratio.
  3. Turn the length factor into the one you need: square it for area, cube it for volume.

Worked example 1: two similar bottles are 10 cm and 15 cm tall. The small one holds 400 cm3.

k = 15 ÷ 10 = 1.5 · volume factor = 1.53 = 3.375

Large bottle = 400 × 3.375 = 1350 cm3

Worked example 2: solids A and B are similar with surface areas 50 cm2 and 72 cm2. A has volume 250 cm3. Find the volume of B.

Area factor = 72/50 = 36/25, so length factor k = √(36/25) = 6/5   (M1)

Volume of B = 250 × (6/5)3 = 250 × 216/125   (M1) = 432 cm3   (A1)

Worked example 3 (2018 1H Q16, numbers changed): volume of frustum ÷ volume of large cone = 26/27. The large cone has height h. Find the height of the frustum.

Small cone ÷ large cone = 1 − 26/27 = 1/27   (M1)

Length scale factor = ∛(1/27) = 1/3   (M1)

Height of frustum = h − ⅓h   (M1) = ⅔h   (A1)

✗ Multiplying the volume by the area ratio 72/50. Areas and volumes scale differently: go through the length factor.
✗ Taking the cube root of 98/125 in the 2018 question. That ratio compared the frustum with the large cone. The frustum is not similar to anything: first find the small cone, 1 − 98/125 = 27/125.

Compound measures: speed, density and pressure

Spec 4.4F–G · 2016 Edexcel SAM 1H Q1 · 2018 Edexcel May 2H Q11 · 2016 Edexcel SAM 2H Q3

A compound measure is one quantity per unit of another, so it is always a division, and its units say which.

Speed — memorisespeed = distance ÷ timekm/h: kilometres per hour · time in hours as a decimal: 45 minutes = 0.75 h
Density — memorisedensity = mass ÷ volumeg/cm3 · mass = density × volume
Pressurepressure = force ÷ areaN/m2 · the spec says the pressure formula will be given
  1. Write the formula in words first.
  2. Convert the units so they match the answer's units: minutes to hours, cm3 or m3, and so on.
  3. Substitute and calculate. Rearrange if you need mass, time or force.

Worked examples

Speed: 420 km in 5 hours 36 minutes. 36 minutes = 36/60 = 0.6 h. Speed = 420 ÷ 5.6 = 75 km/h.

Converting: 72 km/h = 72 000 m ÷ 3600 s = 20 m/s.

Density: gold has density 19.3 g/cm3. A 12 cm3 bar has mass 19.3 × 12 = 231.6 g.

Pressure: a force of 800 N on 0.5 m2 gives 800 ÷ 0.5 = 1600 N/m2.

SAM 1H Q1 (7800 km in 9 h 45 min) gave M1 for "7800 ÷ 9.45", but only as a lesser method mark: the answer 800 needed 9.75 hours.

✗ 9 hours 45 minutes = 9.45 hours.
✓ 45 minutes = 45/60 = 0.75 hours, so 9.75 hours.

04Understanding it

U1

A shape is enlarged by scale factor 3. Why does its area become 9 times as big, not 3 times?

Show answer

Area is a length times a length. Both lengths are multiplied by 3, so the area is multiplied by 3 × 3 = 9.

You can see it with squares: a 1 cm square enlarged by 3 becomes a 3 cm square, which holds nine of the original squares. Volume has three lengths, so it goes up by 33 = 27.

U2

Why is 1 m3 equal to 1 000 000 cm3 and not 100 cm3?

Show answer

A 1 m cube is 100 cm long, 100 cm wide and 100 cm high. Its volume in cm3 is 100 × 100 × 100 = 1 000 000.

The conversion factor is applied once for each dimension: once for length, twice for area, three times for volume. It is the same idea as the scale factors in U1.

U3

Why does the arc length formula have 2πr, while the sector area formula has πr2?

Show answer

The arc is a fraction of the circumference, which is a length: 2πr. The sector is a fraction of the whole circle's area: πr2.

In both, the fraction is θ/360, because a sector of angle θ is θ/360 of a full turn. A quick check: r2 only appears in things measured in square units.

U4

What goes wrong if you use the slant height in the cone volume formula, and why?

Show answer

The volume is ⅓ × base area × perpendicular height, the distance straight up from the base to the apex. The slant height is longer (it is the hypotenuse of the triangle r, h, l), so the answer comes out too big.

The curved surface formula πrl uses l because the surface unrolls into a sector whose radius is the slant height.

U5

In the 2018 frustum question, why can you not take the cube root of 98/125 to get a scale factor?

Show answer

Scale factors only exist between similar shapes. The frustum is not similar to the large cone: it has a flat top, the cone has a point.

The similar pair is the small cone and the large cone. The small cone is 1 − 98/125 = 27/125 of the large one, so k = ∛(27/125) = 3/5. The small cone's height is (3/5)h, and the frustum's is h − (3/5)h = (2/5)h.

U6

A solid hemisphere has total surface area 3πr2. Explain where the 3 comes from.

Show answer

The curved part is half of a sphere's surface: ½ × 4πr2 = 2πr2.

The flat face is a circle of radius r: πr2. Together: 2πr2 + πr2 = 3πr2. A hollow bowl has only the 2πr2.

U7

A car travels 60 km at 30 km/h, then 60 km at 60 km/h. Why is its average speed not 45 km/h?

Show answer

Average speed = total distance ÷ total time. The first 60 km takes 2 hours and the second takes 1 hour, so it is 120 km in 3 hours: 40 km/h.

The car spends longer at the slower speed, so the slow speed counts for more. Averaging the two speeds ignores that.

U8

Two similar jugs have heights in the ratio 2 : 3. A student says the larger jug holds 1.5 times as much. What is the correct factor, and why?

Show answer

The length scale factor is 3/2 = 1.5. Capacity is a volume, so it scales by k3 = 1.53 = 3.375.

The jug is 1.5 times as tall, 1.5 times as wide and 1.5 times as deep, and all three multiply.
