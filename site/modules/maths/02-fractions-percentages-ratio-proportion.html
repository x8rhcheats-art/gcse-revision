Fractions, Percentages, Ratio and Proportion

The money-and-sharing questions that open every paper, plus the proportion question that closes the first half. The whole topic runs on one idea: multipliers. A 4% fall is × 0.96, three years of it is × 0.96³, and undoing a 5% rise is ÷ 1.05. Get that habit and reverse percentages, compound interest and "y is proportional to x²" all become the same calculation.

From the 2018 May 1H mark scheme, on the salary that became HK$252 000 after a 5% rise:

"NB: An answer of 239 400 scores M0 M0 A0"

239 400 is 252 000 minus 5% of 252 000. That takes 5% of the new amount, but the rise was 5% of the old one. The method is wrong, so it scores zero, not "nearly right". Divide by the multiplier: 252 000 ÷ 1.05 = 240 000.

02How it actually works

Almost every question here is one of four routines: fraction arithmetic, percentage multipliers, ratio "parts", and proportion "find k". The calculator is allowed on both papers, but fraction "show that" questions still need every line written out, because the answer is printed on the paper.

Fractions and mixed numbers — "show that"

Spec 1.2F, 1.2I · 2018 Edexcel May 2H Q4 · 2016 Edexcel SAM 2H Q10

Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions, then add or subtract over a common denominator, or multiply (or flip and multiply to divide).

  1. Change every mixed number to an improper fraction: 3 2/5 = 17/5.
  2. Add/subtract: rewrite both over a common denominator, then combine the numerators.
  3. Divide: multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction.
  4. Simplify and convert back to a mixed number, matching the form printed in the question.

Worked example — show that 3 2/5 − 1 3/4 = 1 13/20

17/5 − 7/4 (M1 for correct improper fractions)

= 68/20 − 35/20 (M1 for a correct common denominator with correct numerators)

= 33/20 = 1 13/20 (A1 shown, with no calculator decimals)

Division: 2 1/3 ÷ 1 5/9 = 7/3 ÷ 14/9 = 7/3 × 9/14 = 63/42 = 3/2 = 1 1/2.

✗ Writing 3.4 − 1.75 = 1.65 = 1 13/20. Decimal working does not show the fraction method, and in a "show that" it scores nothing.
✓ Improper fractions → common denominator → simplify, with every stage written.

Percentage change and multipliers

Spec 1.6B, 1.6D, 1.6E · 2018 Edexcel May 2H Q3 · 2016 Edexcel SAM 2H Q5

Increasing by r% multiplies by (1 + r/100); decreasing by r% multiplies by (1 − r/100).

Percentage change — memorisepercentage change = (change ÷ original) × 100always divide by the ORIGINAL amount · +8% → × 1.08 · −7.5% → × 0.925
  1. Turn the percentage into a multiplier.
  2. New amount = original × multiplier.
  3. For a percentage change: find the change, then divide by the original and × 100.

Worked example — a price rises from $480 to $552. Find the percentage increase.

Change = 552 − 480 = 72 (M1)

72 ÷ 480 × 100 = 15% (A1). Or: 552 ÷ 480 = 1.15, so 15% up.

✗ 72 ÷ 552 × 100 = 13.0%. Dividing by the new amount.
✓ Always divide by the value before the change.

Reverse percentages

Spec 1.6F · 2018 Edexcel May 1H Q7(b) · 2016 Edexcel SAM 1H Q8

If you know the amount after a percentage change, divide by the multiplier to get back to the original.

  1. Write the multiplier for the change (a 12% rise is 1.12, a 35% sale is 0.65).
  2. original × multiplier = new amount, so original = new ÷ multiplier.
  3. If you are told the amount of the change instead (SAM 1H Q8: the 15% reduction was $22.50), then 15% = 22.50, 1% = 1.50 and 100% = 150.

Worked example — after a 12% increase a laptop costs $1344. Find the price before the increase.

112% = 1344, or x × 1.12 = 1344 (M1)

1344 ÷ 1.12 (M1) = $1200 (A1)

Check: 1200 × 1.12 = 1344 ✓

✗ 1344 × 0.88 = 1182.72. Taking 12% off the new price.
✓ Divide the new price by 1.12. The 12% was 12% of the unknown original, not of 1344.

Compound interest and depreciation

Spec 1.6G, 1.6B (Higher) · 2016 Edexcel SAM 2H Q8 · 2018 Edexcel May 1H Q7(a)

Compound change applies the same multiplier once per year, so n years is the multiplier to the power n.

Compound growth and decayvalue = P × (1 ± r/100)nP = starting amount · r = percentage rate per year · n = number of years · + for interest, − for depreciation
  1. Write the yearly multiplier: 3.5% interest → 1.035; 18% depreciation → 0.82.
  2. Raise it to the number of years and multiply by the starting amount.
  3. Round only at the end, to the accuracy asked (money: 2 dp or the nearest whole unit).
  4. If asked for the interest, subtract the original amount at the end.

Worked example — $25 000 is invested at 3.5% per year compound interest for 4 years

25 000 × 1.0354 (M2; M1 for 25 000 × 1.035 or a correct year-by-year method started)

= 28 688.075… = $28 688.08 (A1)

Interest earned = 28 688.08 − 25 000 = $3688.08

Depreciation: a $15 000 car losing 18% a year is worth 15 000 × 0.825 = $5561.10 after 5 years.

✗ 25 000 × 0.035 × 4 + 25 000. That is simple interest. The 2018 scheme gave only a special case B1 for 160 000 × 0.12 (4% × 3).
✗ 160 000 × 0.96 × 3. Multiplying by 3 instead of cubing.
✓ 160 000 × 0.963 = 141 557.76 → HK$141 558.

Repeated percentage change and "how many years?"

Spec 1.6A (Higher)

Successive percentage changes multiply; they do not add.

  1. Multiply the multipliers: +30% then −20% → 1.3 × 0.8 = 1.04.
  2. Read off the overall change: 1.04 means a 4% increase, not 10%.
  3. "After how many years does it first fall below…?" Keep multiplying year by year (or try powers) and state the first whole year that passes the target.

Worked example — a car worth $24 000 loses 12% of its value each year. After how many years is it first worth less than half?

0.885 × 24 000 = $12 665.57 (still above $12 000) (M1 for trying a power of 0.88)

0.886 × 24 000 = $11 145.70 (below $12 000) (M1 for the values either side)

6 years (A1)

✗ "+30% then −20% is +10%". The 20% is taken off a bigger amount.
✓ 1.3 × 0.8 = 1.04 → a 4% increase overall.

Ratio — simplifying and sharing

Spec 1.7A, 1.7B · 2018 Edexcel May 2H Q2 · 2016 Edexcel SAM 1H Q2

A ratio splits an amount into equal parts: find the value of one part, then multiply.

  1. Add the ratio numbers to get the total number of parts.
  2. One part = amount ÷ total parts.
  3. Multiply each ratio number by one part.
  4. If you are told a difference (e.g. "A gets $36 more than B"), that difference equals the difference in parts, and you find one part from that instead.
  5. For the form 1 : n, divide both sides by the first number.

Worked example — $2880 is shared in the ratio 3 : 5 : 7. Find the difference between the largest and smallest shares.

3 + 5 + 7 = 15 parts; 2880 ÷ 15 = 192 per part (M1)

(7 − 3) × 192 (M1) = $768 (A1)

Difference version: Amir and Beth share in the ratio 5 : 3, and Amir gets $36 more. 2 parts = 36, so 1 part = 18; Amir gets 90, Beth 54.

✗ 2880 ÷ 3 = 960 for the first share. Dividing by the ratio number, not the total of the parts.
✓ Total parts first, then one part, then multiply.

Ratio with fractions and combined ratios

Spec 1.7E · 2018 Edexcel May 1H Q2

A fraction of a whole and a ratio within that fraction chain together: work from the number you know back to the whole.

  1. Turn a ratio into fractions: girls : boys = 2 : 7 means girls are 2/9 of the children.
  2. Use the known number to find the group it belongs to (girls → children).
  3. Use the fraction of the whole to find the whole (children → everyone), then answer the question asked.
  4. To combine a : b and b : c, scale them so the b values match.

Worked example — a : b = 2 : 3 and b : c = 4 : 5. Find a : b : c.

Make b the same: 2 : 3 = 8 : 12 and 4 : 5 = 12 : 15 (M1)

a : b : c = 8 : 12 : 15 (A1)

Direct proportion

Spec 2.5A (Higher) · 2018 Edexcel May 2H Q16 · 2014 Edexcel Jan 3H Q14

"y is directly proportional to xn" means y = kxn for some constant k, which you find from one pair of values.

Direct proportiony ∝ xn  ⇒  y = kxnthe spec only uses x, x2, x3 and √x
  1. Write the equation with k: y = kx2 (use the power in the question).
  2. Substitute the given pair and solve for k.
  3. Write the formula with the number for k. This is where the A1 is.
  4. Use the formula for the new value. When you square-root at the end, take the positive root if the context needs one.

Worked example — y is directly proportional to x2. y = 45 when x = 3. Find y when x = 4.

y = kx2 (M1)

45 = k × 32 → k = 5 (M1)

y = 5x2 (A1)

x = 4: y = 5 × 16 = 80 (B1 ft)

✗ y = x2 + k. Proportion means multiply by k.
✗ Finding k = 5 but then writing the formula as y = kx2. The scheme wants y = 5x2 written out.

Inverse proportion

Spec 2.5A (Higher) · 2016 Edexcel SAM 2H Q16 · 2012 Edexcel Jan 3H Q15

"y is inversely proportional to xn" means y = k/xn: as x goes up, y goes down.

Inverse proportiony ∝ 1/xn  ⇒  y = k/xn"inversely proportional to the square of d" is F = k/d2
  1. Write y = k/xn.
  2. Substitute the pair and rearrange: k = y × xn.
  3. Write the formula with the number for k.
  4. To find x, rearrange: xn = k/y, then take the root.

Worked example — F is inversely proportional to d2. F = 20 when d = 3. Find d when F = 5.

F = k/d2 (M1); 20 = k/9 → k = 180 (M1); F = 180/d2 (A1)

5 = 180/d2 → d2 = 36 (M1) → d = 6 (A1)

Scaling in proportion and the graphs

Spec 2.5A (Higher) · 2018 Edexcel May 2H Q16(b)

Without finding k, you can see what happens: if y ∝ xn and x is multiplied by s, then y is multiplied by sn (or divided by sn for inverse).

  1. Write the scale factor for x (a 25% increase is × 1.25).
  2. Raise it to the power: y ∝ x2 → y is × 1.252 = × 1.5625.
  3. For inverse proportion, divide instead: y ∝ 1/x2 → y is × 1/1.5625 = × 0.64, which is a 36% decrease.
  4. Graph shapes: y ∝ x is a straight line through the origin; y ∝ x2 is a curve through the origin getting steeper; y ∝ 1/x is a curve that never meets the axes.

Worked example — R = 3t2 and R = 12/x. Show that t is inversely proportional to √x.

3t2 = 12/x (M1 for equating)

t2 = 4/x → t = 2/√x, which is of the form k/√x, so t ∝ 1/√x (A1)

Unitary method and exchange rates

Spec 1.7C, 1.7D, 1.10C

Find the value of one unit, then multiply to the number you want. Exchange rates are direct proportion with k = the rate.

  1. Write what "1 unit" is worth (1 pen, 1 gram, £1).
  2. Multiply or divide to reach the amount asked.
  3. For comparisons ("which is better value?") convert both to the same currency or the same unit amount, then compare and state which with the figures.

Worked example — £1 = €1.16. A watch costs €348 in Paris and £310 in London. Where is it cheaper, and by how much in pounds?

348 ÷ 1.16 = £300 (M1)

£310 − £300 = £10 (M1)

Paris is cheaper by £10 (A1)

04Understanding it

U1

A shop raises a price by 20%, then cuts the new price by 20%. Why is it not back where it started?

Show answer

The 20% cut is taken from a bigger number than the 20% rise was. As multipliers: 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96.

So the final price is 96% of the original, a 4% fall overall. Percentage changes multiply; they never simply add.

U2

Why do you divide by 1.05, not multiply by 0.95, to undo a 5% increase?

Show answer

The rise was original × 1.05 = new. Undoing "× 1.05" means "÷ 1.05".

× 0.95 takes 5% of the new amount, which is bigger than 5% of the original. 252 000 × 0.95 = 239 400, but 240 000 × 1.05 = 252 000. They differ by 600, and the scheme gives zero for the first.

U3

Why is compound interest P × 1.023, not P × 1.06?

Show answer

Each year the 2% is added to the current balance, which already includes last year's interest. So the balance is multiplied by 1.02 three times: 1.02 × 1.02 × 1.02 = 1.061208.

× 1.06 is simple interest, 2% of the original three times. The difference, 0.1208%, is the "interest on the interest".

U4

Money is shared in the ratio 3 : 7 : 4, and the second share is $30 more than the third. Why is one part worth $10?

Show answer

The second share is 7 parts and the third is 4 parts, so the difference between them is 7 − 4 = 3 parts.

3 parts = $30, so 1 part = $10. Then the first share = 3 × 10 = $30. You never need the total.

U5

y is directly proportional to x2. Without finding k, what happens to y when x is tripled?

Show answer

y = kx2. Replace x by 3x: k(3x)2 = 9kx2 = 9y.

So y is multiplied by 9. In general, multiplying x by s multiplies y by sn when y ∝ xn.

U6

F is inversely proportional to d2. Explain why doubling d gives a quarter of F.

Show answer

F = k/d2. With 2d: k/(2d)2 = k/(4d2) = F/4.

The square makes the effect bigger than the change in d. Doubling divides by 22 = 4, and tripling divides by 9. This is the inverse square law.

U7

What does a graph of y against x look like if y is directly proportional to x, and why does it have to go through the origin?

Show answer

A straight line through (0, 0) with gradient k.

y = kx, so when x = 0, y = k × 0 = 0. A straight line that does not pass through the origin, like y = 2x + 3, is linear but not proportional: doubling x does not double y.

U8

To divide by 14/9 you multiply by 9/14. Why does that work?

Show answer

Dividing asks "how many 14/9s fit into this?" Multiplying by the reciprocal gives the same answer because (14/9) × (9/14) = 1.

So a ÷ (14/9) = a × (9/14) × [(14/9) ÷ (14/9)] = a × 9/14. Dividing by a number is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal.
