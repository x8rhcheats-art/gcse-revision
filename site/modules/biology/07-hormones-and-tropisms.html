01How it actually works

Animals coordinate responses two ways: nervously, using electrical impulses, and hormonally, using chemicals carried in the blood. Plants use chemicals only.

Hormones — the definition that earns marks

Spec 2.79

A hormone is a chemical messenger made in an endocrine gland, carried in the blood, that acts on target organs.

All four elements matter. Students routinely lose a mark by saying hormones travel "around the body" rather than in the blood, or by omitting that they act on specific target organs rather than everything.

Adrenaline, explained rather than listed

Adrenaline is released when you are frightened or stressed. Every effect serves the same purpose: preparing for "fight or flight".

Increased heart rate and increased breathing rate deliver more oxygen and glucose to the muscles, so more respiration can occur and more energy is released for movement. Blood glucose rises to supply that respiration, and pupils dilate to let in more light so you can see more.

Answers that just list the effects get fewer marks than answers that link them to respiration in the muscles.

Plant tropisms

Spec 2.93

A tropism is a growth response towards or away from a stimulus. Two to know:

TropismStimulusShootsRoots
PhototropismLightPositive — grow towardsNegative
GravitropismGravityNegative — grow awayPositive — grow down

The advantages: shoots growing towards light get more light for photosynthesis; roots growing down reach water and mineral ions, and anchor the plant.

Auxin — the mechanism

Auxin is a plant hormone made at the tip of a shoot. It causes cell elongation — cells get longer.

When light comes from one side, auxin moves to the shaded side. That gives an unequal distribution: more auxin on the shaded side than the lit side. The cells on the shaded side therefore elongate more, so that side grows longer and the shoot bends towards the light.

Three things make this a full-mark answer: auxin made at the tip, it accumulates on the shaded side, and the unequal distribution causes unequal growth. Answers saying the plant "grows towards the light because it wants light" score nothing — plants have no intent, and the mark is for the mechanism.

In roots, auxin has the opposite effect: it inhibits elongation. So the side with more auxin grows less, which is why roots bend downwards rather than up.

03Understanding it

U1

A shoot on a windowsill bends towards the window. Explain fully, in terms of auxin.

Show answer

Auxin is produced at the tip of the shoot. Because light is coming from one side, the auxin moves away from the light and accumulates on the shaded side.

This gives an unequal distribution of auxin — a higher concentration on the shaded side than the lit side.

Auxin causes cell elongation, so the cells on the shaded side grow longer than those on the lit side. The shaded side of the shoot therefore becomes longer, and the shoot bends towards the light.

The advantage is that the leaves receive more light, so the plant can photosynthesise more.

U2

Why does a root grow downwards when auxin also collects on its lower side?

Show answer

Gravity causes auxin to accumulate on the lower side of the root, just as it collects on the shaded side of a shoot.

The difference is the effect: in roots, auxin inhibits cell elongation rather than promoting it. So the lower side, with more auxin, elongates less than the upper side.

The upper side grows more, so the root bends downwards — positive gravitropism. This takes it towards water and mineral ions, and anchors the plant.

The same hormone, the same distribution, opposite effect. That reversal is the whole point of the question.

U3

A student is startled by a loud bang. Their heart races for several minutes. Explain why the response involves both control systems.

Show answer

Nervous: the sound is detected and an impulse travels along neurones to produce the immediate jump or flinch. This is very fast but short-lived.

Hormonal: adrenaline is released from the adrenal glands into the blood. It travels to target organs including the heart, increasing heart rate and breathing rate.

Because the hormone must be carried in the blood, this response is slower to start but lasts much longer — which is why the heart is still racing minutes after the bang, long after the nervous response has finished.

U4

If the tip is cut off a shoot, it stops bending towards light. What does this show?

Show answer

It shows that the tip is where auxin is produced. Removing it removes the source of the hormone, so no unequal distribution can build up and no unequal growth occurs — the shoot grows straight.

Covering the tip with an opaque cap gives the same result, showing that the tip is also where the light is detected. Covering the shoot below the tip while leaving the tip exposed still produces bending, which confirms it.

04Exam drill

D1

State what is meant by a hormone. (3)

Show answer

A chemical messenger 1; produced by an endocrine gland and carried in the blood 1; that acts on a target organ 1.

D2

Give two differences between nervous and hormonal coordination. (2)

Show answer

Nervous responses are faster than hormonal ones 1; hormonal responses last longer than nervous ones 1. Also accepted: electrical impulse along neurones versus chemical in the blood.

D3

Explain how auxin causes a shoot to grow towards light. (4)

Show answer

Auxin is produced at the tip of the shoot 1; it accumulates on the shaded side, giving an unequal distribution 1; auxin causes cell elongation 1; so the shaded side grows longer and the shoot bends towards the light 1.

D4

Name the tropism shown by a root growing downwards, and state whether it is positive or negative. (2)

Show answer

Gravitropism / geotropism 1; positive 1.

D5

Describe three effects of adrenaline and explain how they help the body respond to danger. (4)

Show answer

Any three effects: increased heart rate 1; increased breathing rate 1; dilated pupils or raised blood glucose 1; plus the link — more oxygen and glucose reach the muscles so more respiration occurs, releasing more energy for movement 1.

D6

State one advantage to a plant of each tropism: shoots growing towards light, and roots growing downwards. (2)

Show answer

Shoots towards light: leaves receive more light for photosynthesis 1; roots downwards: reach water and mineral ions in the soil, and anchor the plant 1.

D7

Name the gland that produces insulin and the gland that produces adrenaline. (2)

Show answer

Insulin — pancreas 1; adrenaline — adrenal gland 1.
