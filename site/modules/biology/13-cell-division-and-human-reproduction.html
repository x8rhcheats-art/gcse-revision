01How it actually works

Mitosis and meiosis

Spec 3.28 – 3.30

Human body cells are diploid: 46 chromosomes in 23 pairs. Gametes are haploid: 23, one from each pair. The two types of division exist to produce each.

MitosisMeiosis
PurposeGrowth, repair, asexual reproductionMaking gametes
WhereAll body cellsTestes and ovaries only
DivisionsOneTwo
Cells madeTwoFour
Chromosome numberDiploid (46) — same as parentHaploid (23) — half
GeneticallyIdentical to parent and each otherDifferent from each other

Before either division, every chromosome is copied, which is why chromosomes look like an X. In mitosis the copies line up at the middle and are pulled apart, one to each end.

Why meiosis gives variation

The chromosomes are shuffled randomly as they separate, so each gamete gets a different mix. Then random fertilisation means any sperm can fuse with any egg. Together these explain why siblings differ.

The menstrual cycle — four hormones

Spec 3.10, 3.11

HormoneMade byDoes
FSHPituitaryCauses an egg to mature in the ovary; stimulates oestrogen
OestrogenOvaryBuilds up the uterus lining
LHPituitaryTriggers ovulation — egg release, about day 14
ProgesteroneOvaryMaintains the lining; inhibits FSH and LH

If no egg is fertilised, progesterone falls and the lining breaks down — menstruation. If pregnancy happens, progesterone stays high so the lining is kept. Build (oestrogen) versus maintain (progesterone) is the distinction most often swapped.

The developing baby

Spec 3.12

The placenta lets substances pass by diffusion between mother and fetus without the bloods mixing: glucose, oxygen and amino acids into the fetus, carbon dioxide and urea out. The amniotic fluid surrounds the fetus and protects it from bumps.

Secondary sexual characteristics

Spec 3.13

Testosterone in boys: deeper voice, facial and body hair, muscle growth, sperm production. Oestrogen in girls: breasts develop, hips widen, periods begin, body hair.

03Understanding it

U1

Why must gametes be made by meiosis and not mitosis?

Show answer

At fertilisation two gametes fuse. If each had 46 chromosomes the zygote would have 92, doubling every generation.

Meiosis halves the number to 23, so fertilisation restores the diploid number of 46. It also makes every gamete genetically different, producing variation.

U2

A karyotype shows 45 chromosomes with only one sex chromosome, an X. What is the person's sex, and suggest how this happened.

Show answer

Female — there is no Y chromosome. This is Turner syndrome.

The likely cause is that the sex chromosomes failed to separate properly during meiosis, so one gamete had no sex chromosome at all. When it fused with a normal gamete carrying an X, the zygote had 45.

From the mock scheme: the ovaries do not develop normally, so there is less oestrogen, puberty and secondary sexual characteristics are delayed, and she is usually infertile.

U3

Explain what happens to progesterone levels if an egg is fertilised, and why that matters.

Show answer

Progesterone levels stay high (later the placenta makes it).

Progesterone maintains the uterus lining, so the embryo can stay implanted and develop. It also inhibits FSH and LH, so no more eggs mature or are released during pregnancy.

04Exam drill

D1

Give three differences between mitosis and meiosis. (3)

Show answer

Mitosis makes two cells, meiosis four 1; mitosis cells are diploid, meiosis cells haploid 1; mitosis cells are identical, meiosis cells genetically different 1.

D2

State the roles of FSH and LH in the menstrual cycle. (2)

Show answer

FSH causes an egg to mature in the ovary 1; LH triggers ovulation / release of the egg 1.

D3

Describe how the placenta is involved in exchanging substances between mother and fetus. (3)

Show answer

Substances diffuse across the placenta 1; glucose, oxygen and amino acids pass to the fetus 1; carbon dioxide and urea pass to the mother 1.

D4

Explain why a red blood cell cannot be used to show a karyotype. (2)

Show answer

Red blood cells have no nucleus 1; so they contain no chromosomes 1.

D5

Give one advantage of sexual reproduction and one advantage of asexual reproduction. (2)

Show answer

Sexual: variation, so the species can survive environmental change or disease 1; asexual: faster / only one parent needed 1.

D6

Name the type of cell division that occurs in white blood cells, and state where meiosis occurs in humans. (2)

Show answer

Mitosis 1; in the testes and ovaries 1.
