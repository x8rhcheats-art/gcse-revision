02How it actually works

Every human starts as one cell. Two kinds of cell division get us there: meiosis makes the sex cells, and mitosis turns the fertilised egg into a body. The rest of the module is the organs and hormones that bring the sex cells together and look after the embryo.

Sexual and asexual reproduction

Spec 3.1 · 2024 Edexcel 1B Q2(d)

Sexual reproduction joins gametes from two parents, so the offspring vary; asexual reproduction uses one parent and mitosis, so the offspring are clones.

SexualAsexual
ParentsTwoOne
GametesYes — made by meiosisNo gametes, no meiosis
FertilisationYes — gametes fuseNo fusion
OffspringGenetically variedGenetically identical (clones)
SpeedSlowerFaster
AdvantageVariation — a species can survive environmental change or diseaseFast; no mate needed; good characteristics kept

For "give three differences", the 2024 scheme accepted any three rows — as long as each compares both types.

Fertilisation and the zygote

Spec 3.2

Fertilisation is the fusion of a male and a female gamete nucleus to make a zygote, which divides by mitosis to become an embryo.

  1. A haploid sperm (23 chromosomes) meets a haploid egg (23) in the oviduct.
  2. The nuclei fuse, making a diploid zygote (46).
  3. The zygote divides by mitosis again and again, making a ball of cells — the embryo.
  4. The embryo moves down to the uterus and implants in the thick lining.

Chromosomes — diploid and haploid

Spec 3.32

Human body cells are diploid, with 46 chromosomes in 23 pairs; gametes are haploid, with 23 — one from each pair.

One chromosome of each pair came from the mother, one from the father. Each chromosome is a long molecule of DNA carrying many genes (module 15). Pair 23 is the sex chromosomes: XX in a female, XY in a male.

Chromosome numbers in humansgamete (23) + gamete (23) → zygote (46)haploid + haploid → diploid · this is why gametes must have half the number

Mitosis — two identical cells

Spec 3.28, 3.29 · 2024 Y11 mock Q6(a)(ii)

Mitosis makes two diploid daughter cells that are genetically identical to the parent cell and to each other.

  1. Each chromosome is copied — which is why chromosomes look like an X just before division.
  2. The copied chromosomes line up across the middle of the cell.
  3. The two copies of each chromosome are pulled apart, one to each end.
  4. Two new nuclei form, and the cell divides into two identical cells.

Mitosis is used for growth, repair, cloning and asexual reproduction. White blood cells, skin cells and a growing embryo are all made by mitosis.

Meiosis — four different gametes

Spec 3.30

Meiosis makes four haploid cells, each genetically different — the gametes.

Why meiosis and fertilisation give variation

Spec 3.31

Meiosis shuffles the chromosomes into every gamete differently, and random fertilisation means any sperm can meet any egg.

  1. In meiosis, the pairs of chromosomes separate randomly, so each gamete gets a different mix of maternal and paternal chromosomes.
  2. So every sperm and every egg is genetically different.
  3. Random fertilisation: which sperm fuses with which egg is down to chance.
  4. So every zygote has a new combination of alleles — which is why siblings differ.

Karyotypes and the sex chromosomes

Spec 3.25, 3.32 · 2024 Y11 mock Q6 · 2023 Jan Edexcel 1B Q5

A karyotype is a photograph of a cell's chromosomes arranged in pairs — so it needs a cell with a nucleus.

  • Red blood cells cannot be used: they have no nucleus, so no chromosomes.
  • White blood cells are used: they have a nucleus, and they are made by mitosis, so they show the person's full diploid set.
  • A male karyotype has a Y chromosome — pair 23 is one large (X) and one small (Y).

The 45-chromosome karyotype — the mark points (2023 Jan 1B Q5(b), 2024 mock Q6(b))

1. 45 chromosomes instead of 46 — only one in pair 23 1

2. Only one X, no Y — so the person is female 1

3. Ovaries do not develop — less oestrogen, no normal puberty or secondary sexual characteristics 1

4. Cannot produce eggs — infertile 1

Cause: the sex chromosomes failed to separate in meiosis, so one gamete had no sex chromosome 1

This is Turner syndrome. The name is not needed — the reasoning is.

The menstrual cycle — four hormones

Spec 3.10B · 2023 Edexcel 2B Q5(a) · 2024 Edexcel 2B Q4(a)

Two hormones from the pituitary (FSH, LH) control the ovary; two from the ovary (oestrogen, progesterone) control the uterus lining.

HormoneMade byDoes
FSHPituitary glandCauses an egg (follicle) to mature in the ovary; stimulates oestrogen release
OestrogenOvaryBuilds up (repairs) the uterus lining; stimulates LH release
LHPituitary glandTriggers ovulation — egg release, about day 14; stimulates progesterone release
ProgesteroneOvaryMaintains the lining; inhibits FSH and LH
  1. Days 1–5: the lining breaks down and is lost — menstruation.
  2. FSH makes an egg mature and the ovary release oestrogen.
  3. Oestrogen builds up the lining and triggers a surge of LH.
  4. LH causes ovulation at about day 14. The empty follicle (corpus luteum) makes progesterone.
  5. Progesterone maintains the lining and stops FSH and LH, so no more eggs mature.
  6. No fertilisation → progesterone falls → the lining breaks down → the cycle restarts.

"Describe the roles of FSH and LH" — four marks (2023 2B Q5(a)(iii))

1. FSH makes eggs / follicles mature 1

2. FSH stimulates oestrogen release 1

3. LH causes ovulation / egg release 1

4. LH stimulates progesterone release 1

Using the hormones — contraception data

2023 Edexcel 2B Q5(b)

Hormonal contraceptives work because progesterone (often with oestrogen) inhibits FSH and LH, so no egg matures and none is released.

The mechanism links straight back to the cycle. What Edexcel actually asked was to evaluate methods from a data table — so here is the shape of the answer:

The placenta and amniotic fluid

Spec 3.11, 3.12 · 2024 Edexcel 2B Q4(b)

The placenta lets food and oxygen diffuse from mother to fetus and waste diffuse back, without the two bloods mixing; the amniotic fluid cushions the fetus.

  • Into the fetus: glucose, amino acids, oxygen, also minerals, vitamins and antibodies.
  • Out of the fetus: carbon dioxide and urea.
  • Adapted by: a large surface area (villi), a thin barrier, and a good blood supply on both sides to keep the concentration gradients steep.
  • The umbilical cord joins the fetus to the placenta.
  • The amniotic fluid, inside the amnion, protects the fetus from bumps and physical damage.

Harmful substances cross too — carbon monoxide and nicotine from smoking, alcohol, some viruses. That is why the 2024 data showed smokers' babies gaining less mass (module 12).

04Understanding it

U1

Why must gametes be made by meiosis and not mitosis?

Show answer

At fertilisation two gametes fuse. If each had 46 chromosomes the zygote would have 92, doubling every generation.

Meiosis halves the number to 23, so fertilisation restores the diploid number of 46. It also makes every gamete genetically different, producing variation.

U2

A karyotype shows 45 chromosomes with only one sex chromosome, an X. What is the person's sex, and suggest how this happened.

Show answer

Female — there is no Y chromosome. This is Turner syndrome.

The likely cause is that the sex chromosomes failed to separate properly during meiosis, so one gamete had no sex chromosome at all. When it fused with a normal gamete carrying an X, the zygote had 45.

From the mock scheme: the ovaries do not develop normally, so there is less oestrogen, puberty and secondary sexual characteristics are delayed, and she is usually infertile.

U3

Explain what happens to progesterone levels if an egg is fertilised, and why that matters.

Show answer

Progesterone levels stay high (later the placenta makes it).

Progesterone maintains the uterus lining, so the embryo can stay implanted and develop. It also inhibits FSH and LH, so no more eggs mature or are released during pregnancy.

U4

Every cell in your body came from one zygote, yet they all have the same chromosomes. Which type of division made them, and why does that matter?

Show answer

Mitosis. Each division copies every chromosome, then separates the copies so each new cell gets a full, identical set of 46.

That means every body cell carries the same genes as the zygote. Cells become different (muscle, nerve, skin) by using different genes — not by having different ones. It is also why a white blood cell can show your whole karyotype.

U5

Sperm carry either an X or a Y chromosome; eggs always carry an X. Use meiosis to explain why the chance of a boy is 50%.

Show answer

A man's cells are XY. In meiosis the pair separates, so half his sperm get the X and half get the Y.

A woman's cells are XX, so every egg gets an X. Fertilisation is random: an X sperm gives XX (girl), a Y sperm gives XY (boy). With equal numbers of each sperm, the chance is 1 in 2 every time — the father's sperm decides.

U6

On a hormone graph, the oestrogen peak comes just before the LH peak, and ovulation follows. Explain the link.

Show answer

FSH makes an egg mature, and the ovary releases rising amounts of oestrogen, which builds up the uterus lining.

High oestrogen stimulates the pituitary to release a surge of LH. The LH surge triggers ovulation. So the lining is ready just as the egg is released — timed so an embryo would have somewhere to implant.

U7

A contraceptive tablet contains progesterone. Use the menstrual cycle to explain why it prevents pregnancy.

Show answer

Progesterone inhibits FSH and LH from the pituitary.

Without FSH, no egg matures. Without an LH surge, no ovulation happens. No egg is released, so there is nothing for a sperm to fertilise. It is the same reason no new eggs are released during pregnancy, when progesterone stays high.

U8

Why does the placenta need a large surface area and a good blood supply, when the two bloods never mix?

Show answer

Everything crosses the placenta by diffusion (and some active transport). The rate of diffusion is faster with a large surface area, a thin barrier and a steep concentration gradient.

The mother's blood keeps bringing glucose and oxygen, and the fetal blood keeps carrying them away, so the gradients stay steep. Because the bloods do not mix, the fetus is protected from the mother's blood pressure and from many pathogens.
