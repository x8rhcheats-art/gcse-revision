Pollination then fertilisation

Spec 3.3, 3.4

Pollination is the transfer of pollen from an anther to a stigma. Fertilisation comes later:

  1. Pollen lands on the stigma.
  2. A pollen tube grows down the style to the ovary.
  3. The male nucleus travels down the tube into the ovule.
  4. It fuses with the female nucleus — that fusion is fertilisation.
  5. The ovule becomes the seed; the ovary becomes the fruit.

Self-pollination is anther to stigma on the same plant; cross-pollination is between two plants and gives more variation.

Germination

Spec 3.5, 3.6

Seeds need three conditions:

  • Water — activates enzymes that break down the food store
  • Oxygen — for aerobic respiration, releasing energy for growth
  • Warmth — a suitable temperature for the enzymes

Light is not needed — seeds live on their stored starch until the first leaves appear. The Year 11 mock explicitly refused "light for photosynthesis" as a germination condition.

The practical sets up four tubes of cress seeds: dry cotton wool (no water), moist at 20 °C (all conditions), boiled cooled water under oil (no oxygen), moist in a fridge (too cold). Only the second germinates.

Asexual reproduction in plants

Spec 3.7

Runners (strawberries) grow along the ground and root to form new plants. Cuttings are pieces of stem planted to grow roots. Both produce clones — genetically identical to the parent.

03Understanding it

U1

Explain why wind-pollinated flowers produce far more pollen than insect-pollinated ones.

Show answer

Wind carries pollen randomly, so almost all of it lands nowhere useful. Producing huge amounts makes it likely that some reaches a stigma.

Insects carry pollen directly from flower to flower, so much less is wasted and less needs to be made.

U2

In the germination practical, why is the water in one tube boiled, cooled and covered with oil?

Show answer

Boiling removes dissolved oxygen from the water. Cooling avoids killing the seeds with heat. The oil layer stops oxygen from the air dissolving back in.

So that tube tests whether oxygen is needed, with water and warmth still present. The seeds do not germinate because they cannot respire aerobically to release energy for growth.

U3

Some fruits are brightly coloured and sweet. Suggest how this helps the plant.

Show answer

The colour attracts animals, which eat the fruit for its sugars. The seeds pass through the gut undigested and are egested far from the parent.

That disperses the seeds, so the new plants do not compete with the parent for light, water and minerals.

04Exam drill

D1

Describe the events from pollination to fertilisation. (4)

Show answer

Pollen lands on the stigma 1; a pollen tube grows down the style 1; the male nucleus travels down the tube to the ovule 1; it fuses with the female nucleus 1.

D2

Give three differences between an insect-pollinated and a wind-pollinated flower. (3)

Show answer

Large coloured petals versus small or none 1; stigma inside and sticky versus outside and feathery 1; anthers inside versus hanging outside, or nectary present versus absent 1.

D3

State the three conditions needed for germination and explain why each is needed. (3)

Show answer

Water to activate enzymes 1; oxygen for respiration 1; warmth for enzyme action 1.

D4

Name the structures that develop into the seed and the fruit. (2)

Show answer

Ovule becomes the seed 1; ovary becomes the fruit 1.

D5

Explain why plants produced by runners are genetically identical to the parent. (2)

Show answer

Runners are asexual reproduction — no gametes fuse 1; the new cells are produced by mitosis 1.
