01How it actually works

Photosynthesis makes glucose out of carbon dioxide and water, using light trapped by chlorophyll inside chloroplasts. It is an endothermic reaction: light energy goes in and is stored as chemical energy.

The two equations

Spec 2.18, 2.19

Word equation:

Photosynthesis — word equationcarbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygenlight energy, absorbed by chlorophyll · write "light" and "chlorophyll" above the arrow, not as reactants
Photosynthesis — balanced symbol equation6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂the balancing numbers are a mark of their own · C₆H₁₂O₆ is glucose

Light and chlorophyll are not reactants. Putting them on the left of the arrow is a standard way to lose the mark — they belong above it.

Limiting factors, and why the graph flattens

Spec 2.20

Three things can limit the rate: light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature. A limiting factor is the one in shortest supply — the one holding the rate back.

The classic graph rises then levels off. The answer has two halves:

  • On the slope: the factor on the x-axis is limiting. Increase it and the rate increases.
  • On the plateau: that factor is no longer limiting. Something else now is — and the exam wants you to name a plausible candidate, such as carbon dioxide concentration or temperature.

Temperature behaves differently from the other two, because photosynthesis is controlled by enzymes. Raise it and the rate climbs to an optimum, then falls as the enzymes denature. Light and CO₂ have no such collapse — they simply stop helping.

Light intensity and the inverse square law

Light intensity is inversely proportional to the square of the distance from the lamp. Move the lamp from 10 cm to 20 cm and the intensity does not halve — it drops to a quarter.

Light intensity and distancelight intensity ∝ 1 ÷ distance²double the distance → quarter the intensity · treble it → a ninth

Gas exchange in plants — the bit students get backwards

Spec 2.40B, 2.41B, 2.43B, 2.44B

Plants respire all the time, day and night. They photosynthesise only in light. What changes through the day is the net movement of gas, and that word is where the marks are.

ConditionsWhat is happeningNet gas exchange
DarknessRespiration onlyNet O₂ in, net CO₂ out
Dim light (compensation point)Both, at equal ratesNo net exchange
Bright lightPhotosynthesis faster than respirationNet CO₂ in, net O₂ out

Saying "plants respire at night and photosynthesise in the day" is the error the spec point is written to catch. Respiration never stops.

The practicals

Spec 2.23, 2.45B

Oxygen production: pondweed such as Elodea in water with sodium hydrogencarbonate (the CO₂ supply), a lamp at measured distances, and you count bubbles per minute. Use an LED lamp so the water is not heated — otherwise temperature is not controlled.

Testing a leaf for starch: boil in water to kill it, boil in ethanol to remove the chlorophyll, rinse in hot water to soften, then add iodine. Blue-black means starch, so photosynthesis happened. The ethanol must be heated in a water bath, never over a flame — it is flammable.

The three controls each use the same test with one thing removed: cover half a leaf with foil (no light), use a variegated leaf (no chlorophyll in the white parts), or seal the plant with soda lime (no CO₂). In every case the deprived region stays orange-brown.

Hydrogencarbonate indicator measures net gas exchange from a leaf: it is orange at normal CO₂ levels, turns yellow when CO₂ rises (respiration winning) and purple/magenta when CO₂ falls (photosynthesis winning).

03Understanding it

U1

A graph of photosynthesis rate against light intensity rises steeply, then flattens completely. Explain both parts.

Show answer

Rising part: light intensity is the limiting factor. As it increases, more light energy is absorbed by chlorophyll, so the rate of photosynthesis increases.

Flat part: light is no longer limiting — increasing it further has no effect. Another factor is now limiting, such as carbon dioxide concentration or temperature.

The mark is for naming the other factor. "Something else is limiting" does not score.

U2

A lamp is moved from 10 cm to 30 cm from a pondweed. Bubbles fall from 45 per minute to 5. Is that consistent with the inverse square law?

Show answer

Treble the distance → intensity falls to 1 ÷ 3² = 1/9 of its value. One ninth of 45 is 5. So yes — the fall is exactly what the inverse square law predicts, which also suggests light was the limiting factor across that whole range.

Had the count fallen only to 15 (a third), light would not have been the sole limiting factor.

U3

A plant is left in bright light. Hydrogencarbonate indicator around it turns purple. Later, in the dark, it turns yellow. Explain both colours, using the word "net".

Show answer

Purple in light: photosynthesis is faster than respiration, so there is a net uptake of carbon dioxide. CO₂ in the tube falls and the indicator goes purple.

Yellow in dark: photosynthesis has stopped but respiration continues, so there is a net release of carbon dioxide. CO₂ rises and the indicator goes yellow.

Note what is not true: the plant did not "start respiring" in the dark. It was respiring the whole time — in the light, photosynthesis was simply using CO₂ faster than respiration produced it.

U4

Why is a variegated leaf used to show that chlorophyll is needed for photosynthesis, and what result would you expect?

Show answer

A variegated leaf has green parts (with chlorophyll) and white parts (without), on the same leaf — so light, CO₂, temperature and water are all identical for both. The only variable is chlorophyll, which makes it a controlled comparison.

After destarching, light and the iodine test: the green parts turn blue-black (starch made) and the white parts stay orange-brown (no starch). Chlorophyll is therefore required.

U5

A student boils a leaf in ethanol over a naked Bunsen flame. Give one safety problem and one reason the plant should have been destarched first.

Show answer

Safety: ethanol is highly flammable and would catch fire over a naked flame. It must be heated in a water bath with the Bunsen turned off.

Destarching: leaving the plant in the dark for 24–48 hours first removes any starch already stored. Without that step, a blue-black result could come from starch made before the experiment, so it would not prove anything about the conditions being tested.

U6

A farmer's greenhouse tomatoes grow poorly with yellow leaves. Suggest which mineral ion is short, and why the symptom follows.

Show answer

Magnesium ions. Magnesium is needed to make chlorophyll; without enough of it the plant cannot make the green pigment, so leaves turn yellow.

Less chlorophyll also means less light absorbed, so less photosynthesis, less glucose and poorer growth — which links the symptom to the poor yield.

If the leaves were green but the plant simply small, nitrate would be the better answer: nitrate is needed for amino acids and therefore proteins for growth.

04Exam drill

D1

Write the balanced symbol equation for photosynthesis. (2)

Show answer

Correct formulae: CO₂, H₂O, C₆H₁₂O₆, O₂ 1; correctly balanced 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ 1.

D2

State what is meant by a limiting factor, and name three for photosynthesis. (4)

Show answer

The factor in shortest supply that is holding back / limiting the rate 1; light intensity 1; carbon dioxide concentration 1; temperature 1.

D3

Explain two ways the palisade mesophyll layer is adapted for photosynthesis. (2)

Show answer

Contains many chloroplasts, so more chlorophyll to absorb light 1; positioned near the top of the leaf, where light intensity is greatest 1.

D4

Explain why a plant placed in darkness releases carbon dioxide. (3)

Show answer

Respiration continues in the dark 1; photosynthesis stops without light, so no carbon dioxide is being used 1; so there is a net release of carbon dioxide 1.

D5

Describe how you would test a leaf for starch. (4)

Show answer

Boil the leaf in water to kill it 1; boil in ethanol in a water bath to remove the chlorophyll 1; rinse in hot water to soften it 1; add iodine solution — blue-black shows starch is present 1.

D6

A lamp is moved from 20 cm to 40 cm from a pondweed. State what happens to the light intensity reaching the plant, and explain your answer. (2)

Show answer

It falls to a quarter of its previous value 1; because light intensity is inversely proportional to the square of the distance, and 2² = 4 1.

D7

Explain why an LED lamp is used rather than a filament lamp in the pondweed experiment. (2)

Show answer

A filament lamp releases heat, which would raise the temperature of the water 1; temperature is a control variable that also affects the rate, so the results would not show the effect of light intensity alone 1.

D8

Name the mineral ion needed to make amino acids, and state the effect of a deficiency. (2)

Show answer

Nitrate ions 1; deficiency causes stunted / poor growth 1.
