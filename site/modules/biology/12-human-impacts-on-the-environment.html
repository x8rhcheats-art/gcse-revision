Human Impacts on the Environment

"Reject carbon monoxide" · "Ignore nutrients"

Carbon monoxide is a pollutant, not a greenhouse gas. And "nutrients" run off the fields scores nothing — write nitrate or phosphate ions, from fertiliser, leaching into the water.

02How it actually works

Humans add gases to the air, minerals and sewage to the water, and cut down forests. Every question here wants the same shape of answer: what the pollutant is, what it does, and the biological consequence — which organisms are harmed, and why.

Sulfur dioxide and acid rain

Spec 4.12 · 2024 Edexcel 2B Q1(c)(i) · 2023 Y11 mock Q4

Sulfur dioxide from burning fossil fuels dissolves in rain to make acid rain, which kills organisms in lakes and damages trees.

  1. Coal and oil contain sulfur. Burning them releases sulfur dioxide (volcanoes release it naturally).
  2. It dissolves in water in clouds, forming acid rain.
  3. Acid rain falls into lakes and rivers and lowers the pH. Fish, their eggs and invertebrates die — whole food chains collapse.
  4. It falls on forests: it damages leaves and leaches minerals out of the soil, so trees grow poorly and die.

It also corrodes limestone buildings — but that is chemistry. In biology, give the biological consequence.

Carbon monoxide

Spec 4.12 · 2023 Y11 mock Q4 · 2024 Edexcel 2B Q4(c)

Carbon monoxide binds to haemoglobin in red blood cells, so the blood carries less oxygen.

  • Source: incomplete combustion of fuels — not enough oxygen. Car exhausts, faulty gas boilers, and cigarette smoke.
  • Effect: it binds to haemoglobin more strongly than oxygen does, and does not let go.
  • Consequence: less oxygen reaches the cells, so less aerobic respiration and less energy released. Drowsiness, then unconsciousness and death.

The 2024 paper linked it to pregnancy: smoking mothers had smaller babies, because carbon monoxide in the smoke means less oxygen reaches the fetus through the placenta (module 13).

✗ "Carbon monoxide poisons you."
✗ "Carbon monoxide is a greenhouse gas."
✓ "Carbon monoxide binds to haemoglobin, reducing the oxygen-carrying capacity of the blood."

The greenhouse effect

Spec 4.15 · 2024 Edexcel 1B Q5(a)(ii)

Greenhouse gases absorb the infrared radiation given off by the warm Earth and re-radiate it, trapping heat in the atmosphere.

  1. Radiation from the Sun passes through the atmosphere and warms the Earth's surface.
  2. The warm Earth gives off infrared (long-wave) radiation.
  3. Greenhouse gases absorb some of this infrared and re-radiate it, some back towards Earth.
  4. So heat is trapped and the Earth stays warmer than it would otherwise be.

That is natural and necessary — without it Earth would be too cold for most life. The problem is the enhanced greenhouse effect: extra gases from human activity trap more heat, causing global warming.

"Explain the greenhouse effect" — two marks (2024 1B Q5(a)(ii))

1. Infrared radiation is absorbed by greenhouse gases / heat is trapped 1

2. So the temperature of the Earth increases 1

"It makes a hole in the ozone layer" is a different problem, and scores nothing.

Greenhouse gases and where they come from

Spec 4.13, 4.14 · 2024 Edexcel 1B Q5(a)(i) · 2022 Edexcel 2B Q1(a)(i)

The five greenhouse gases on the spec are carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, CFCs and water vapour. Oxygen and carbon monoxide are not greenhouse gases.

The 2024 multiple choice listed CFCs, methane, oxygen and water vapour. The answer was all but oxygen — students who forgot water vapour chose wrongly.

Global warming and its consequences

Spec 4.15 · 2023 Jan Edexcel 2B Q5(a)(ii) · 2022 Edexcel 2B Q1(a)(iii)

More greenhouse gas traps more heat, raising the Earth's average temperature — and every mark is for a biological consequence of that.

  • Ice caps and glaciers melt → sea levels rise → flooding of low-lying land and habitat loss
  • Climate change — more extreme weather, droughts, storms, desertification
  • Species migrate or change distribution; some become extinct; food chains are disrupted
  • Pests and diseases spread into new areas
  • Crop yields change; some regions can no longer grow the same crops
  • Oceans absorb more CO₂ and become more acidic → coral bleaching

✗ "Global warming makes the Earth hotter." — that is the definition, not a consequence.
✓ "Ice caps melt, so sea levels rise, flooding habitats." · "Species migrate or become extinct."

Water pollution by sewage

Spec 4.16

Untreated sewage is food for bacteria, and the bacteria's respiration uses up the oxygen in the water.

  1. Raw sewage enters a river. It contains organic matter and minerals such as nitrate and phosphate.
  2. Bacteria feed on the organic matter and multiply rapidly.
  3. They respire aerobically, using up the dissolved oxygen.
  4. Fish and other animals cannot respire and die — or move away.
  5. The minerals also cause eutrophication downstream. Sewage may also carry pathogens that spread disease.

So sewage can strip the oxygen even without an algal bloom: the bacteria have food from day one.

Eutrophication — the chain

Spec 4.17 · 2023 Jan Edexcel 2B Q5(b)(iii) · 2022 Jan Edexcel 2B Q4(c)

Eutrophication is excess minerals in water causing an algal bloom, whose decay by bacteria uses up the oxygen. Fertiliser or sewage gets into a river or lake. Then:

  1. Excess nitrates and phosphates are leached from the fields into the water.
  2. Algae grow rapidly — an algal bloom covers the surface.
  3. The bloom blocks light, so plants below cannot photosynthesise and die.
  4. The algae also die.
  5. Decomposers (bacteria) feed on the dead plants and algae and multiply.
  6. The bacteria respire aerobically, using up the oxygen in the water — so fish and other animals die.

The four-mark biodiversity answer (2023 Jan 2B Q5(b)(iii))

1. Nitrate leached / run-off into the river 1

2. Algae grow and block light, so plants die 1

3. Dead matter decomposed; bacterial respiration means less oxygen 1

4. Fish and other species die — fewer species, lower biodiversity 1

Why it is the bacteria, not the algae

Spec 4.17

The oxygen is used up by the huge population of decomposing bacteria, not by the algae.

While they are alive, algae photosynthesise and give off oxygen at the surface. The crash comes later: dead algae and dead plants are food for bacteria, the bacteria multiply enormously, and their respiration strips the dissolved oxygen.

✗ "The algae use up all the oxygen."
✗ "The fish die because there is no light."
✓ "Bacteria decompose the dead plants and algae; their aerobic respiration uses up the oxygen, so fish cannot respire."

The 2022 fish-farm question turned this round. Crabs and lobsters eat waste food and faeces, so less decomposition, fewer bacteria and less bacterial respiration — more oxygen stays in the water. Seaweed absorbs the nitrate, so there is no algal bloom.

Deforestation — soil, water and minerals

Spec 4.18B · 2023 Edexcel 2B Q1(b)(i)

Without trees, nothing holds the soil or takes up its minerals and water.

EffectWhy

LeachingMinerals wash out of the soil into rivers instead of being taken up by trees — soil becomes infertile, rivers become eutrophic

The 2023 dinoflagellate passage wanted this chain: trees removed → soil erosion → minerals such as nitrates and magnesium washed into the sea → the algae get nitrate for amino acids and magnesium for chlorophyll → population explodes. Match each mineral to its use.

Deforestation — the carbon cycle and the air

Spec 4.18B, 4.10 · 2023 Jan Edexcel 2B Q5(a)

Deforestation raises carbon dioxide in two ways: less photosynthesis, and the carbon in the trees is released.

  1. Fewer trees → less photosynthesis → less CO₂ removed from the air.
  2. The trees are burned (combustion) or left to decompose (decomposers respire) → their stored carbon is released as CO₂.
  3. More CO₂ → enhanced greenhouse effect → global warming.

It also upsets the balance of atmospheric gases: less oxygen is released by photosynthesis. But the mark schemes want CO₂ up first — "less oxygen to breathe" is not the point.

Eutrophication: nitrates → algal bloom → blocks light → plants die → bacteria decompose them → bacteria respire → oxygen used up → fish die.

04Understanding it

U1

After heavy rain, fish in a lake next to a farm die. Explain the full sequence of events.

Show answer

Rain washes fertiliser from the fields into the lake, adding nitrates and phosphates. Algae grow rapidly and form a bloom on the surface.

The bloom blocks light from plants lower down, which cannot photosynthesise and die. Bacteria decompose the dead plants and algae and multiply rapidly.

The bacteria respire aerobically, using up the dissolved oxygen. Without oxygen, fish cannot respire and they die.

U2

A town switches from coal power to an anaerobic digester that burns biogas. Give one environmental benefit and one drawback.

Show answer

Benefit: less carbon dioxide and methane released overall, so less contribution to the greenhouse effect and global warming — and less carbon monoxide, so less effect on oxygen transport in the blood.

Drawback: the Year 11 mock accepted that digesters may release more sulfur dioxide, causing acid rain that kills fish and damages trees.

The mark scheme wanted a discussion on both sides with a biological consequence for each — a gas named with no effect scores little.

U3

Why is the greenhouse effect necessary for life, and why is it now a problem?

Show answer

Without greenhouse gases, the infrared radiated by Earth would escape to space and the planet would be far too cold for most life.

Human activity has increased the concentration of those gases, so more infrared is trapped. This enhanced greenhouse effect raises the average temperature, causing climate change, rising sea levels and habitat loss.

U4

Oxygen levels in a eutrophic lake are often high in the afternoon while the algae are alive, then crash a few weeks later. Why?

Show answer

While alive, the algae photosynthesise and release oxygen, so the surface water is well oxygenated in daylight.

When the algae and the shaded plants die, they become food for bacteria. The bacterial population grows enormously, and their aerobic respiration uses up the dissolved oxygen faster than it can be replaced. That is when the fish die — proof that the bacteria, not the living algae, remove the oxygen.

U5

A pregnant woman smokes. Explain why her baby is likely to have a lower birth mass.

Show answer

Cigarette smoke contains carbon monoxide. It binds to haemoglobin in the mother's red blood cells, so her blood carries less oxygen.

Less oxygen diffuses across the placenta to the fetus. The fetus's cells carry out less aerobic respiration, releasing less energy for growth, so it grows more slowly. This was the data in 2024 Edexcel 2B Q4.

U6

Cutting down a rainforest makes the soil infertile within a few years. Explain why.

Show answer

In a forest, mineral ions released by decomposers are taken up quickly by tree roots. The canopy also breaks the force of the rain.

Without trees, heavy rain hits bare ground. Soil erosion removes the fertile topsoil, and leaching washes the minerals down and out into rivers. No roots are there to absorb them, and no leaf litter replaces them. The remaining soil has too few minerals for crops.

U7

Both burning fossil fuels and cutting down forests raise atmospheric CO₂. Explain why deforestation counts twice.

Show answer

Burning fossil fuels only adds carbon dioxide.

Deforestation adds CO₂ — the trees are burned or decomposed, releasing their stored carbon — and removes a sink: fewer trees means less photosynthesis, so less CO₂ is taken out of the air every year afterwards.

U8

Untreated sewage kills fish in a river even when no algal bloom forms. How?

Show answer

Sewage is itself organic matter — food for bacteria. The bacteria multiply rapidly and respire aerobically, using up the dissolved oxygen directly.

So the oxygen falls without the algae-and-light stage. Fish cannot get enough oxygen for respiration and die. The minerals in the sewage may still cause eutrophication further downstream.
