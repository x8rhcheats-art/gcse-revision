The greenhouse effect

Spec 4.13 – 4.15

The Sun's radiation reaches Earth and warms it. Earth re-emits heat as infrared. Greenhouse gases absorb some of that infrared and re-radiate it back, keeping the planet warm. That is natural and necessary. The problem is the enhanced greenhouse effect: extra gases trap more heat, causing global warming.

GasMain human source
Carbon dioxideBurning fossil fuels; deforestation
MethaneCattle; rice paddy fields; landfill

Consequences of global warming the mark schemes accept: climate change, ice caps melting and sea levels rising, flooding, habitat loss, species migrating or becoming extinct, more extreme weather, and changes in where crops can grow.

Eutrophication — the chain

Spec 4.16, 4.17

Fertiliser or untreated sewage gets into a river or lake. Then:

  1. Excess nitrates and phosphates enter the water.
  2. Algae grow rapidly — an algal bloom covers the surface.
  3. The bloom blocks light, so plants below cannot photosynthesise and die.
  4. The algae also die.
  5. Decomposers (bacteria) feed on the dead plants and algae and multiply.
  6. The bacteria respire aerobically, using up the oxygen in the water — so fish and other animals die.

Step 6 is the one that goes wrong. The oxygen is not used up by the algae, and not "because the plants stopped photosynthesising" alone — it is the respiration of the huge bacterial population. Sewage adds a second route: it is itself food for bacteria, so their respiration strips oxygen even without an algal bloom.

03Understanding it

U1

After heavy rain, fish in a lake next to a farm die. Explain the full sequence of events.

Show answer

Rain washes fertiliser from the fields into the lake, adding nitrates and phosphates. Algae grow rapidly and form a bloom on the surface.

The bloom blocks light from plants lower down, which cannot photosynthesise and die. Bacteria decompose the dead plants and algae and multiply rapidly.

The bacteria respire aerobically, using up the dissolved oxygen. Without oxygen, fish cannot respire and they die.

U2

A town switches from coal power to an anaerobic digester that burns biogas. Give one environmental benefit and one drawback.

Show answer

Benefit: less carbon dioxide and methane released overall, so less contribution to the greenhouse effect and global warming — and less carbon monoxide, so less effect on oxygen transport in the blood.

Drawback: the Year 11 mock accepted that digesters may release more sulfur dioxide, causing acid rain that kills fish and damages trees.

The mark scheme wanted a discussion on both sides with a biological consequence for each — a gas named with no effect scores little.

U3

Why is the greenhouse effect necessary for life, and why is it now a problem?

Show answer

Without greenhouse gases, the infrared radiated by Earth would escape to space and the planet would be far too cold for most life.

Human activity has increased the concentration of those gases, so more infrared is trapped. This enhanced greenhouse effect raises the average temperature, causing climate change, rising sea levels and habitat loss.

04Exam drill

D1

Explain how sulfur dioxide causes damage to lakes. (2)

Show answer

Sulfur dioxide dissolves in rain to form acid rain 1; which acidifies the lake, killing fish and other aquatic organisms 1.

D2

Name three greenhouse gases and give a source of each. (3)

Show answer

Carbon dioxide from burning fossil fuels 1; methane from cattle or rice fields 1; nitrous oxide from fertilisers, or CFCs from old fridges 1.

D3

Explain how untreated sewage entering a river can kill fish. (5)

Show answer

Sewage adds nutrients such as nitrates 1; algae grow rapidly and block light 1; plants die 1; bacteria decompose the dead matter and the sewage, multiplying 1; bacterial respiration uses up oxygen so fish die 1.

D4

Explain why carbon monoxide is dangerous. (2)

Show answer

It binds to haemoglobin in red blood cells 1; reducing the capacity of the blood to carry oxygen 1.

D5

Give three effects of deforestation. (3)

Show answer

Increased carbon dioxide in the atmosphere 1; soil erosion 1; leaching of minerals, disrupted water cycle or loss of biodiversity 1.

D6

Give three biological consequences of global warming. (3)

Show answer

Loss of habitats 1; species migrate or become extinct 1; flooding from rising sea levels, or changes to where crops grow 1.
