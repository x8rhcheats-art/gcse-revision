01How it actually works

Atoms are never used up. Carbon and nitrogen move between the air, living things and the soil, and the same atoms go round again and again. Microorganisms do most of the returning.

The carbon cycle — four processes

Spec 4.10

ProcessWhat happens to carbon
PhotosynthesisPlants remove CO₂ from the air and make glucose
RespirationPlants, animals and decomposers return CO₂ to the air
FeedingCarbon compounds pass along the food chain as animals eat
DecompositionBacteria and fungi break down dead organisms and waste — and respire, releasing CO₂
CombustionBurning wood and fossil fuels returns CO₂ to the air
FossilisationDead matter that does not decay, under pressure over millions of years, becomes coal, oil and gas

Photosynthesis is the only process that takes carbon dioxide out of the air. Every other arrow puts it back. That one fact answers most "why does CO₂ rise" questions: less photosynthesis (deforestation) or more combustion.

The nitrogen cycle — why plants need help

Spec 4.11B

Air is 78% nitrogen, but N₂ is very unreactive, so plants cannot use it. They need nitrogen as nitrate ions to make amino acids and proteins, and they take nitrates in through their roots by active transport.

Organism or processConvertsNotes
Nitrogen-fixing bacteriaN₂ gas → nitrogen compoundsIn root nodules of legumes (peas, beans, clover) and in soil
LightningN₂ → nitratesThe energy splits the N≡N bond
DecomposersProteins in dead matter and urea → ammonia / ammoniumBacteria and fungi
Nitrifying bacteriaAmmonium → nitrites → nitratesNeed oxygen — aerobic
Denitrifying bacteriaNitrates → N₂ gasAnaerobic — thrive in waterlogged soil

The pairing that decides the waterlogging question: nitrifying bacteria need oxygen, denitrifying bacteria work without it. Waterlogged soil has no air, so nitrifying bacteria stop and denitrifying bacteria take over — nitrates are lost as nitrogen gas and the soil becomes less fertile. Ploughing and draining add air and reverse it.

Why farmers grow clover and beans

Legumes carry nitrogen-fixing bacteria in their root nodules. Growing them, then ploughing them back into the soil, adds nitrogen compounds — a natural alternative to buying nitrate fertiliser. This is crop rotation.

The four bacteria, in order round the cycle

Fixing: N₂ in. Decomposers: protein → ammonium. Nitrifying: ammonium → nitrate, needs oxygen. Denitrifying: nitrate → N₂ out, no oxygen.

03Understanding it

U1

A leaf falls to the forest floor. Trace one carbon atom in it back into the atmosphere.

Show answer

The carbon is in organic compounds such as cellulose. Decomposers — bacteria and fungi — secrete enzymes that break these down, and absorb the products.

The decomposers use the glucose in respiration, which releases the carbon as carbon dioxide into the air. Decomposition itself does not release CO₂ — the decomposers' respiration does.

U2

A field floods for several weeks. Afterwards the crop grows poorly. Explain, using the nitrogen cycle.

Show answer

Waterlogged soil has no air spaces, so it lacks oxygen.

Nitrifying bacteria need oxygen, so they stop turning ammonium into nitrates. Denitrifying bacteria are anaerobic and thrive, converting the nitrates that remain into nitrogen gas.

Both changes mean less nitrate in the soil. The crop cannot make enough amino acids and proteins, so its growth is stunted.

U3

Why might a farmer plant clover in a field one year and wheat the next?

Show answer

Clover is a legume with nitrogen-fixing bacteria in its root nodules. They convert nitrogen gas into nitrogen compounds, enriching the soil.

When the clover is ploughed in, decomposers and nitrifying bacteria turn its proteins into nitrates. The wheat then has plenty of nitrate for amino acids and proteins, so less fertiliser is needed.

U4

Name the only process in the carbon cycle that removes CO₂ from the air, and explain why cutting down rainforest raises CO₂ in two ways.

Show answer

Photosynthesis is the only process that removes CO₂.

Deforestation raises CO₂ because fewer trees means less photosynthesis, so less is removed; and the trees are usually burned or left to decompose, which releases the carbon stored in them.

Decomposers break down proteins in the dead animal 1; producing ammonium compounds 1; nitrifying bacteria convert ammonium into nitrates 1; the plant absorbs nitrates through its roots by active transport 1.

D4

Explain why plants cannot use nitrogen gas directly. (2)

Show answer

Nitrogen gas is very unreactive 1; plants can only absorb nitrogen as nitrate ions from the soil 1.

D5

Explain why waterlogged soil contains fewer nitrates. (3)

Show answer

Waterlogged soil lacks oxygen 1; so nitrifying bacteria, which need oxygen, cannot make nitrates 1; and denitrifying bacteria convert nitrates into nitrogen gas 1.

D6

State where nitrogen-fixing bacteria are found, and what they do. (2)

Show answer

In root nodules of legumes such as peas and clover, or in the soil 1; they convert nitrogen gas into nitrogen compounds 1.
