01How it actually works

Genetic modification — making insulin

Spec 5.12 – 5.15

A gene from one species is put into another, which then makes the protein. The organism is transgenic.

  1. Restriction enzymes cut the human insulin gene out of human DNA, leaving sticky ends.
  2. The same restriction enzyme cuts open a bacterial plasmid — a small ring of DNA — giving matching sticky ends.
  3. Ligase joins the gene into the plasmid.
  4. The plasmid, now a vector, is put back into a bacterium.
  5. The bacteria are grown in a fermenter; they multiply and make human insulin, which is extracted for people with diabetes.

Using the same restriction enzyme on both matters: the sticky ends only match if they were cut at the same base sequence.

GM crops

Spec 5.16

Crops given genes for pest resistance (they make an insecticide) or herbicide resistance, so more survive and yield rises. Concerns include genes spreading to wild plants, effects on insects that are not pests, and a smaller range of varieties.

Cloning plants — micropropagation

Spec 5.17B

Small pieces of a plant (explants) are placed on sterile agar containing nutrients and plant hormones. They grow into many identical plants. Used to produce large numbers of plants with desired characteristics quickly, and to save rare species.

Cloning animals — Dolly the sheep

Spec 5.18B, 5.19B

  1. Remove the nucleus from an egg cell (enucleate it).
  2. Take the nucleus from an adult body cell of the animal to be cloned.
  3. Insert the body-cell nucleus into the empty egg.
  4. An electric shock makes the egg divide into an embryo.
  5. Implant the embryo into the uterus of a surrogate mother.

The lamb is a clone of the animal that gave the body cell — not the egg donor and not the surrogate. Transgenic cloned animals can produce human proteins in their milk.

Weighing cloning

02Cheat sheet

Two enzymes, two jobs

Restriction enzymes cut (sticky ends). Ligase joins. The plasmid is the vector.

03Understanding it

U1

Why must the same restriction enzyme cut both the human DNA and the plasmid?

Show answer

Each restriction enzyme cuts at one specific base sequence, leaving particular sticky ends. Using the same enzyme gives the gene and the plasmid complementary sticky ends.

The bases pair up, holding the gene in place so ligase can join it into the plasmid. Different enzymes would leave ends that do not match.

U2

A body cell is taken from a black sheep, an egg from a white sheep, and the embryo implanted into a brown sheep. What colour is the lamb, and why?

Show answer

Black. The lamb's DNA all comes from the nucleus inserted into the egg — from the black sheep's body cell.

The white sheep's egg had its nucleus removed, so contributes no genes. The brown surrogate only provides the uterus.

04Exam drill

D1

Describe how bacteria can be genetically modified to produce human insulin. (5)

Show answer

The insulin gene is cut out with a restriction enzyme 1; a plasmid is cut with the same restriction enzyme 1; the gene is joined into the plasmid by ligase 1; the plasmid is inserted into a bacterium 1; the bacteria multiply in a fermenter and make insulin 1.

D2

Describe how an adult animal can be cloned. (4)

Show answer

Remove the nucleus from an egg cell 1; insert the nucleus from an adult body cell 1; an electric shock makes it divide into an embryo 1; implant the embryo into a surrogate mother 1.

D3

Give one advantage and one disadvantage of cloning crop plants. (2)

Show answer

Advantage: many plants with the desired characteristic quickly 1; disadvantage: no variation, so all could be killed by one disease 1.

D4

State the functions of restriction enzymes and ligase. (2)

Show answer

Restriction enzymes cut DNA at specific sequences 1; ligase joins pieces of DNA together 1.
