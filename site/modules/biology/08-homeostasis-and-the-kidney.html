Homeostasis, Excretion and the Kidney

Nine spec points, and the hardest block on the Year 10 checklist. Filtration then selective reabsorption, negative feedback in both directions, and the ADH loop that students most often get backwards.

01How it actually works

Homeostasis is the maintenance of a constant internal environment. Three things are controlled: body temperature, blood glucose and water content. All three work by negative feedback — a change is detected and a response is triggered that reverses it.

Excretion is not egestion

Spec 2.71

Excretion is the removal of the waste products of metabolism — substances the body made. Egestion is the removal of undigested food as faeces, which was never absorbed and never part of a reaction.

OrganExcretes
LungsCarbon dioxide (and water vapour)
KidneysUrea, excess water and excess salts
SkinWater, salts and urea in sweat

The liver produces urea but does not excrete it, so it is not the excretory organ for urea — a distinction examiners like.

Where urea comes from

Excess amino acids cannot be stored. In the liver they are deaminated: the amino group is removed, forming ammonia, which is highly toxic. The ammonia is immediately converted to the less toxic urea, which travels in the blood to the kidneys for excretion.

The kidney: filtration then selective reabsorption

Spec 2.72B – 2.75B

This is a two-stage process, and the whole topic depends on keeping them separate.

Stage 1 — ultrafiltration. Blood arrives at the glomerulus under high pressure. Small molecules are forced out into the Bowman's capsule: water, glucose, salts and urea. Large molecules stay in the blood: proteins and blood cells are too big to pass through.

Stage 2 — selective reabsorption. As the filtrate travels along the tubule, useful substances are taken back into the blood:

SubstanceWhat happensHow
GlucoseAll reabsorbedActive transport (against the gradient)
WaterAmount varies with needOsmosis
SaltsAs much as neededActive transport
UreaNot reabsorbedStays in the filtrate and leaves as urine

The word selective is the point: the kidney filters almost everything out, then chooses what to take back. Glucose is reabsorbed by active transport, which requires energy from respiration — that is why kidney tubule cells contain many mitochondria.

The path of urine: kidney → ureter → bladder → urethra. Ureter and urethra are easy to swap, and the mark schemes do not accept the wrong one.

Water balance and ADH — the loop to get right

Spec 2.76B – 2.79B

When you are dehydrated — too little water, so blood is too concentrated:

  1. The change is detected (by the brain).
  2. The pituitary gland releases more ADH into the blood.
  3. ADH makes the kidney tubules more permeable to water.
  4. More water is reabsorbed into the blood.
  5. A small volume of concentrated urine is produced, and blood water content rises back to normal.

When you have drunk a lot — blood too dilute — every step reverses: less ADH, tubules less permeable, less water reabsorbed, and a large volume of dilute urine.

The error to avoid: ADH does not "remove water" or "make you urinate more". More ADH means less urine. Reading the name as "anti-diuretic — anti-weeing" fixes the direction permanently.

Negative feedback, stated generally

Every homeostatic mechanism has the same shape: a change is detected, a response is triggered that opposes the change, and the level returns towards normal. Because the response opposes the change, the system is self-correcting.

Blood glucose is the other example worth knowing. Insulin lowers blood glucose (causing the liver to convert glucose to glycogen for storage); glucagon raises it (converting glycogen back to glucose). Two hormones, opposite directions, same gland — the pancreas.

03Understanding it

U1

A person runs a marathon on a hot day and drinks very little. Describe what happens to their urine and explain the hormonal control.

Show answer

They lose a lot of water in sweat, so the water content of the blood falls and the blood becomes more concentrated.

This is detected, and the pituitary gland releases more ADH into the blood. ADH makes the kidney tubules more permeable to water, so more water is reabsorbed back into the blood by osmosis.

The result is a small volume of concentrated urine, darker in colour. Blood water content returns towards normal — negative feedback.

U2

Protein is found in a patient's urine. Explain what this suggests about their kidneys.

Show answer

Normally protein molecules are too large to be filtered at the glomerulus, so they stay in the blood and never enter the filtrate at all.

Protein in the urine therefore suggests the filtering membrane is damaged — the pores have become too large, letting protein through.

Note the reasoning: this cannot be a reabsorption problem, because protein is never in the filtrate to be reabsorbed. That distinction is what the question is testing.

U3

Why does an untreated diabetic have glucose in their urine, when a healthy person has none?

Show answer

In a healthy person, glucose is filtered out at the glomerulus but all of it is reabsorbed by active transport, so none reaches the urine.

In an untreated diabetic, blood glucose is very high because there is too little insulin to lower it. So much glucose is filtered that the active transport system becomes saturated — it cannot reabsorb it all.

The excess glucose stays in the filtrate and appears in the urine.

U4

Explain why kidney tubule cells contain a large number of mitochondria.

Show answer

Glucose and salts are reabsorbed by active transport, which moves substances against the concentration gradient.

Active transport requires energy, which is released by aerobic respiration. That happens in the mitochondria, so cells doing a lot of active transport need many of them.

The same reasoning explains why root hair cells in plants are also packed with mitochondria.

U5

Explain why urea must be removed from the body, and where it is made.

Show answer

The body cannot store excess amino acids. In the liver they are deaminated — the amino group is removed — producing ammonia, which is highly toxic.

The liver immediately converts ammonia into the less toxic urea. Even so, urea is still a waste product and would be harmful if it built up, so it is carried in the blood to the kidneys and excreted in the urine.

A point worth keeping straight: urea is made in the liver but excreted by the kidneys.

U6

Someone drinks two litres of water quickly. Describe the changes in ADH and in their urine.

Show answer

The water content of the blood rises, so the blood becomes too dilute.

The pituitary releases less ADH. The kidney tubules become less permeable to water, so less water is reabsorbed.

More water stays in the filtrate, producing a large volume of dilute, pale urine. Blood water content falls back to normal.

04Exam drill

D1

Define homeostasis. (1)

Show answer

The maintenance of a constant internal environment 1.

D2

Give one difference between excretion and egestion. (2)

Show answer

Excretion removes the waste products of metabolism, made by the body 1; egestion removes undigested food that was never absorbed 1.

D3

Name two substances filtered out of the blood at the glomerulus, and one that is not. (3)

Show answer

One of water, glucose, salts or urea 1; a second from that list 1; not filtered: proteins or blood cells, because they are too large 1.

D4

Explain what is meant by selective reabsorption, using glucose as an example. (3)

Show answer

Useful substances are taken back into the blood from the filtrate 1; all of the glucose is reabsorbed 1; by active transport, against the concentration gradient 1.

D5

Describe how the body responds when the water content of the blood falls too low. (4)

Show answer

The pituitary gland releases more ADH into the blood 1; ADH makes the kidney tubules more permeable to water 1; more water is reabsorbed into the blood by osmosis 1; a smaller volume of more concentrated urine is produced 1.

D6

Name the tubes carrying urine from the kidney to the bladder, and from the bladder out of the body. (2)

Show answer

Kidney to bladder: the ureter 1; bladder to outside: the urethra 1.

D7

Describe how urea is formed. (3)

Show answer

Excess amino acids are broken down in the liver 1; the amino group is removed — deamination — forming ammonia 1; the ammonia is converted into the less toxic urea 1.

D8

Name the hormones that lower and raise blood glucose, and state the organ that produces them. (3)

Show answer

Insulin lowers blood glucose 1; glucagon raises it 1; both are produced by the pancreas 1.
