02How it actually works

Homeostasis is the maintenance of a constant internal environment. The kidneys do two homeostatic jobs: they remove urea (excretion) and they control the water content of the blood (osmoregulation). Both depend on the same two steps — filter almost everything out, then take back what is needed.

Excretion is not egestion

Spec 2.71 · 2022 Y10 Q1(a)

Excretion is the removal of the waste products of metabolism — substances the body made. Egestion is the removal of undigested food as faeces, which was never absorbed and never part of a reaction.

OrganExcretes
LungsCarbon dioxide (and water vapour)
KidneysUrea, excess water and excess ions
SkinWater, salts and a little urea in sweat

The liver produces urea but does not excrete it, so it is not the excretory organ for urea — a distinction examiners like.

Where urea comes from

Spec 2.71

Urea is made in the liver from excess amino acids, and excreted by the kidneys.

  1. Excess amino acids cannot be stored.
  2. In the liver they are deaminated: the amino group is removed, forming ammonia, which is highly toxic.
  3. The ammonia is immediately converted to the less toxic urea.
  4. Urea travels in the blood to the kidneys and leaves in the urine.

The urinary system

Spec 2.73B · 2023 Y10 Q6(a) · 2022 Edexcel 2B Q4(a)

Two ureters (one per kidney), one urethra. The real mark scheme rejects "urethra" for the ureter, and rejects "gall bladder" for the bladder.

The nephron — the parts in order

Spec 2.74B · 2023 Edexcel 2B Q3(a) · 2024 Y10 Q6(a)

Each kidney contains about a million nephrons, and each nephron does filtration then reabsorption along its length.

  1. Glomerulus — a knot of capillaries, where blood is filtered under high pressure.
  2. Bowman's capsule — the cup around the glomerulus that collects the filtrate.
  3. Proximal convoluted tubule (first coiled part) — glucose is reabsorbed here.
  4. Loop of Henle — makes the tissue around it salty, so water can later leave the collecting duct.
  5. Distal convoluted tubule (second coiled part) — ions are adjusted.
  6. Collecting duct — water is reabsorbed here, controlled by ADH. What is left is urine.

"Proximal" means near the start; "distal" means further away. On a diagram, the proximal tubule is the coil joined straight onto the capsule.

Ultrafiltration — stage 1

Spec 2.75B · 2023 Y10 Q6(b)(i) · 2023 Edexcel 2B Q3(b)

High blood pressure in the glomerulus forces small molecules out of the blood into the Bowman's capsule.

The vessel bringing blood into the glomerulus is wider than the one taking it out. Blood enters faster than it can leave, so the pressure builds up. That high pressure drives the filtering.

SubstanceFiltered?Why
WaterYesSmall molecule
GlucoseYesSmall molecule
Ions (salts)YesSmall
UreaYesSmall molecule
ProteinsNoToo large to pass out of the glomerulus
Blood cellsNoToo large

So the glomerular filtrate is water, glucose, ions and urea — like plasma with the proteins taken out.

Selective reabsorption of glucose — stage 2

Spec 2.77B · 2023 Y11 mock Q9(a) · 2022 Edexcel Jan 2B Q5(a)

All the glucose is taken back into the blood in the proximal convoluted tubule, by active transport.

Glucose is useful, so the body cannot afford to lose it in urine. It is reabsorbed against the concentration gradient, which needs active transport and energy from respiration. That is why the tubule cells are packed with mitochondria.

The three-mark glucose answer

1. Selective reabsorption into the blood 1

2. In the proximal convoluted tubule 1

3. By active transport, using energy, against the concentration gradient 1

The word selective is the point: the kidney filters almost everything out, then chooses what to take back. Urea is not reabsorbed — it stays in the filtrate.

Water reabsorption in the collecting duct

Spec 2.76B · 2022 Edexcel Jan 2B Q5(b)

Water leaves the collecting duct by osmosis and goes back into the blood.

The loop of Henle makes the tissue around the collecting duct very salty. As filtrate flows down the collecting duct, water moves out by osmosis, from the dilute filtrate into the salty tissue, and then into the blood. How much water can leave depends on how permeable the collecting duct wall is — and that is what ADH controls.

Because water is taken back but urea is not, urea ends up far more concentrated in urine than in the filtrate.

ADH and water balance — the loop to get right

Spec 2.78B · 2023 Y10 Q6(c) · 2022 Edexcel 2B Q4(c) · 2024 Edexcel 2B Q3(b)

More ADH makes the collecting duct more permeable, so more water is reabsorbed and less urine is made.

When you are dehydrated — too little water, so blood is too concentrated:

  1. The hypothalamus in the brain detects the change.
  2. The pituitary gland releases more ADH into the blood.
  3. ADH makes the collecting duct more permeable to water.
  4. More water is reabsorbed into the blood by osmosis.
  5. A small volume of concentrated, dark urine is produced, and blood water content rises back to normal.

When you have drunk a lot — blood too dilute — every step reverses: less ADH, collecting duct less permeable, less water reabsorbed, and a large volume of dilute, pale urine.

✗ "ADH removes water." ✗ "More ADH makes you urinate more."
✓ "More ADH → collecting duct more permeable → more water reabsorbed into the blood → less, more concentrated urine."

Read the name as "anti-diuretic — anti-urine" and the direction is fixed for good.

Reading urine data

The 2024 real paper compared three athletes. No drink: small, very dark urine — blood concentrated, more ADH, more water reabsorbed. Pure water: large, pale urine — blood diluted, less ADH. Isotonic drink (salt and sugar at blood concentration): in between, because the salt and sugar are absorbed too, so the blood is not diluted as much.

Dialysis — when the kidneys fail

2022 Y10 Q7 · 2023 Edexcel Jan 2B Q1(c)

A dialysis machine removes urea by diffusion through a partially permeable membrane.

Blood flows on one side of the membrane and dialysis fluid on the other. The fluid is chosen carefully:

  • No urea in the fluid, so urea diffuses out of the blood down its concentration gradient.
  • Same glucose and ion concentration as normal blood, so there is no net loss of the useful ones.
  • Large proteins such as albumin are too big to pass through the pores, so they stay in the blood.

The dialyser speeds this up with long, coiled tubing (large surface area), a thin membrane (short diffusion distance), and fluid that keeps flowing so the concentration gradient is maintained.

Negative feedback, stated generally

Spec 2.72B

Every homeostatic mechanism detects a change and triggers a response that opposes it.

Water balance by ADH is the kidney example. Temperature control by the skin, and blood glucose by insulin and glucagon, work the same way — both are taught in module 7. Because the response opposes the change, the system is self-correcting.

04Understanding it

U1

A person runs a marathon on a hot day and drinks very little. Describe what happens to their urine and explain the hormonal control.

Show answer

They lose a lot of water in sweat, so the water content of the blood falls and the blood becomes more concentrated.

The hypothalamus detects this, and the pituitary gland releases more ADH into the blood. ADH makes the collecting duct more permeable to water, so more water is reabsorbed back into the blood by osmosis.

The result is a small volume of concentrated urine, darker in colour. Blood water content returns towards normal — negative feedback.

U2

Protein is found in a patient's urine. Explain what this suggests about their kidneys.

Show answer

Normally protein molecules are too large to be filtered at the glomerulus, so they stay in the blood and never enter the filtrate at all.

Protein in the urine therefore suggests the filter in the glomerulus is damaged, letting large molecules through. High blood pressure is one cause.

Note the reasoning: this cannot be a reabsorption problem, because protein is never in the filtrate to be reabsorbed. That distinction is what the question is testing.

U3

Why does an untreated diabetic have glucose in their urine, when a healthy person has none?

Show answer

In a healthy person, glucose is filtered out at the glomerulus but all of it is reabsorbed in the proximal convoluted tubule by active transport, so none reaches the urine.

In an untreated diabetic, blood glucose is very high because there is too little insulin to lower it. So much glucose is filtered that the active transport system cannot keep up — it cannot reabsorb it all.

The excess glucose stays in the filtrate and appears in the urine.

U4

Explain why the cells of the proximal convoluted tubule contain a large number of mitochondria.

Show answer

Glucose is reabsorbed there by active transport, which moves substances against the concentration gradient.

Active transport requires energy, which is released by aerobic respiration. That happens in the mitochondria, so cells doing a lot of active transport need many of them.

The same reasoning explains why root hair cells in plants are also packed with mitochondria.

U5

Explain why urea must be removed from the body, and where it is made.

Show answer

The body cannot store excess amino acids. In the liver they are deaminated — the amino group is removed — producing ammonia, which is highly toxic.

The liver immediately converts ammonia into the less toxic urea. Even so, urea is still a waste product and would be harmful if it built up, so it is carried in the blood to the kidneys and excreted in the urine.

A point worth keeping straight: urea is made in the liver but excreted by the kidneys.

U6

Someone drinks two litres of water quickly. Describe the changes in ADH and in their urine.

Show answer

The water content of the blood rises, so the blood becomes too dilute.

The pituitary releases less ADH. The collecting duct becomes less permeable to water, so less water is reabsorbed.

More water stays in the filtrate, producing a large volume of dilute, pale urine. Blood water content falls back to normal.

If they kept drinking and made very little urine, the blood would become too dilute and water would enter their cells by osmosis, making them swell — the 2022 Year 10 question.

U7

The blood vessel entering the glomerulus is wider than the one leaving it. Why does that matter?

Show answer

Blood can get in more easily than it can get out, so it backs up in the glomerulus and the pressure rises.

That high pressure is what forces water, glucose, ions and urea out of the capillaries into the Bowman's capsule — ultrafiltration. Without it, much less would be filtered and much less urea would be removed.

U8

Dialysis fluid contains glucose and salts at the normal blood concentration, but no urea. Why is it made that way?

Show answer

No urea: urea is more concentrated in the blood than in the fluid, so it diffuses out of the blood through the partially permeable membrane. That is the whole purpose.

Normal glucose and salts: the concentration is the same on both sides, so there is no net diffusion. The patient keeps the useful substances, and any excess salt still diffuses out.

The fluid is kept flowing so fresh, urea-free fluid is always next to the membrane, keeping the gradient steep.
