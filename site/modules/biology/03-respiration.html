Respiration

Four equations and one distinction that decides most of the marks: respiration releases energy, it does not make it. Every year someone writes "produces energy" and loses the mark for it.

01How it actually works

Respiration is the release of energy from glucose, in every living cell, all the time. The energy is transferred to ATP, which cells then use for movement, growth, keeping warm and building large molecules.

Two points the spec is fussy about. Energy is released, never created or produced — that is conservation of energy, and the mark schemes reject "produces energy" outright. And respiration is not breathing: breathing is ventilation, a separate thing covered in module 4.

Aerobic respiration

Spec 2.34, 2.35, 2.37

Uses oxygen, happens in the mitochondria, and breaks glucose down completely — which is why it releases far more energy per glucose molecule.

Aerobic respiration — word equationglucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + waterenergy released and transferred to ATP · in the mitochondria
Aerobic respiration — balanced symbol equationC₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂Othe reverse of photosynthesis · balancing numbers carry their own mark

Notice it is photosynthesis run backwards. That is worth knowing because it makes both equations one thing to remember rather than two.

Anaerobic respiration — and why it differs in plants and animals

Spec 2.36, 2.38

Without oxygen, glucose is broken down incompletely. Much less energy is released, and the products differ between organisms.

Anaerobic respiration in animalsglucose → lactic acidin muscle during hard exercise · no CO₂, no water
Anaerobic respiration in plants and yeastglucose → ethanol + carbon dioxidethis is fermentation · used in brewing and bread-making

The animal equation has one product only. Adding carbon dioxide to it is one of the most common errors in this topic — that belongs to the plant and yeast version.

Oxygen debt and why you keep panting

During hard exercise the muscles cannot get oxygen fast enough, so they respire anaerobically as well as aerobically. Lactic acid builds up, causing fatigue and cramp.

When you stop, you keep breathing hard to repay the oxygen debt — the extra oxygen needed to break down the lactic acid that accumulated. That is why breathing rate stays high after the exercise has finished, which is the point of the exercise practical in module 4.

The practical: respiring seeds

Spec 2.39

Germinating seeds in a vacuum flask release heat — the thermometer rises over a day or two. The control is a flask of dead (boiled) seeds, killed and rinsed in disinfectant, whose temperature does not rise.

The disinfectant matters: without it, microorganisms on the dead seeds would respire and warm the flask, which would wreck the comparison. Examiners ask why it is needed.

For carbon dioxide, air is drawn through limewater before and after the seeds. The limewater after the seeds turns milky; the limewater before them does not.

03Understanding it

U1

A student writes: "Respiration happens in the lungs and produces energy for the body." Give three corrections.

Show answer

1. Location. Respiration happens in every living cell (aerobic respiration in the mitochondria), not in the lungs. The lungs do gas exchange.

2. "Produces". Respiration releases energy that was already stored in glucose. Energy cannot be created.

3. What the energy does. It is transferred to ATP, which supplies energy for cell processes — movement, growth, keeping warm, building molecules.

U2

A sprinter's muscles ache after a 200 m race, and they keep breathing heavily for several minutes. Explain both, naming the substance responsible.

Show answer

During the race the muscles could not get oxygen fast enough, so they respired anaerobically as well as aerobically. This produced lactic acid, which builds up and causes the ache and fatigue.

Afterwards the sprinter continues to breathe heavily to take in extra oxygen — the oxygen debt — which is used to break down the lactic acid. Once it is cleared, breathing returns to normal.

U3

Why does anaerobic respiration release so much less energy than aerobic respiration from the same amount of glucose?

Show answer

Because the glucose is broken down incompletely. Without oxygen the molecule is only partly broken apart, so most of the chemical energy is still locked in the products — lactic acid in animals, ethanol in plants and yeast.

Aerobic respiration breaks glucose down completely to carbon dioxide and water, releasing far more of the stored energy.

Evidence for this: ethanol burns. If anaerobic respiration had extracted all the energy, there would be none left in the product to burn.

U4

In the respiring seeds practical, why must the dead seeds be rinsed in disinfectant, and why is a vacuum flask used?

Show answer

Disinfectant: it kills microorganisms on the dead seeds. Those microbes would respire and release heat, so the "dead" flask would warm up too and the control would prove nothing.

Vacuum flask: it insulates, so heat released by respiration is not lost to the surroundings. Without it the temperature rise would be too small to measure.

U5

Yeast is used to make both bread and beer. Explain, using one equation, why the same organism does two apparently different jobs.

Show answer

Both rely on anaerobic respiration in yeast: glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide.

In bread, the useful product is the carbon dioxide — the bubbles make the dough rise. The ethanol evaporates during baking.

In beer, the useful product is the ethanol, and the carbon dioxide provides the fizz.

One reaction, two different products being harvested.

04Exam drill

D1

State what is meant by respiration. (2)

Show answer

The release of energy from glucose 1; occurring in all living cells 1. "Produces energy" is not accepted.

D2

Write the balanced symbol equation for aerobic respiration. (2)

Show answer

Correct formulae C₆H₁₂O₆, O₂, CO₂, H₂O 1; balanced C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O 1.

D3

Give the word equation for anaerobic respiration in animals, and in plants. (2)

Show answer

Animals: glucose → lactic acid 1; plants/yeast: glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide 1.

D4

Give three differences between aerobic and anaerobic respiration. (3)

Show answer

Any three: aerobic uses oxygen, anaerobic does not 1; aerobic breaks glucose down completely, anaerobic incompletely 1; aerobic releases more energy 1; different products / aerobic in mitochondria and anaerobic in the cytoplasm.

D5

Explain why a runner continues to breathe deeply after finishing a race. (3)

Show answer

Lactic acid has built up in the muscles from anaerobic respiration 1; extra oxygen is needed to break it down — the oxygen debt 1; so deep breathing continues until the lactic acid has been removed 1.

D6

Describe how you would show that germinating seeds release carbon dioxide. (3)

Show answer

Draw air through the apparatus containing the germinating seeds 1; pass the air leaving the seeds through limewater 1; the limewater turns milky / cloudy, showing carbon dioxide is present 1. A second tube of limewater before the seeds acts as a control.

D7

Name the organelle in which aerobic respiration occurs, and state one type of cell you would expect to contain many of them. (2)

Show answer

Mitochondria 1; any cell needing a lot of energy — muscle cell, sperm cell, root hair cell 1.
