01How it actually works

Humans have a double circulatory system: blood passes through the heart twice for every complete circuit of the body. One loop goes to the lungs, the other to the rest of the body.

Why double beats single

Spec 2.52

The pulmonary circuit carries blood from the right side of the heart to the lungs and back. The systemic circuit carries it from the left side to the body and back.

The advantage, and the mark: blood is re-pressurised after the lungs. Lung capillaries are delicate, so blood must pass through them slowly at low pressure. Returning it to the heart lets it be pumped out again at high pressure, so it reaches the body quickly. A single circulation would deliver blood to the body slowly, limiting the rate of respiration.

The left ventricle therefore has a much thicker muscular wall than the right: it pumps to the whole body, while the right pumps only to the lungs.

The heart, chamber by chamber

Blood arrives in an atrium and leaves from a ventricle. Valves stop it going backwards.

The pulmonary vessels are the exception to the usual rule. Arteries carry blood away from the heart and veins carry it towards the heart — that is the definition, and it is about direction, not oxygen. The pulmonary artery is the one artery carrying deoxygenated blood.

The coronary arteries supply the heart muscle itself with oxygen and glucose. If they narrow with fatty deposits, the heart muscle is starved of oxygen — coronary heart disease, and a complete block causes a heart attack.

Each feature follows from the pressure. Arteries have thick elastic walls to withstand and smooth out high pressure from the heart. Veins have valves because pressure is too low to stop backflow on its own, and a wide lumen to reduce resistance. Capillaries are one cell thick to give a short diffusion distance for exchange with the tissues.

Red blood cells have no nucleus — that is the adaptation examiners most want, because it makes room for more haemoglobin. Saying they "have a nucleus to control the cell" is a guaranteed lost mark.

Clotting, and why it matters

Platelets trigger a series of reactions that convert soluble fibrinogen into insoluble fibrin, which forms a mesh trapping red blood cells to make a clot.

Two reasons it matters: it prevents blood loss, and it stops pathogens entering through the wound.

Immunity

Spec 2.66, 2.68, 2.69

Phagocytes engulf and digest pathogens — non-specific, they attack anything foreign.

Lymphocytes produce antibodies, which are specific: each antibody has a shape complementary to one antigen on the pathogen's surface. Antibodies bind to the antigens, clumping pathogens together so phagocytes destroy them.

Memory cells remain after an infection. On a second exposure they produce antibodies faster and in greater quantity, so the pathogen is destroyed before symptoms appear. That is immunity, and it is how vaccination works: a harmless form of the pathogen is introduced so lymphocytes make antibodies and memory cells without you getting ill.

The rule that survives the pulmonary exception

Arteries carry blood AWAY from the heart; veins carry it TOWARDS the heart. Define them by direction, never by oxygen — otherwise the pulmonary artery and vein look like mistakes.

03Understanding it

U1

Explain the advantage of a double circulatory system over a single one, as found in fish.

Show answer

In a single circulation, blood passes through the gill capillaries and then straight on to the body. Capillaries are narrow and offer high resistance, so blood leaves them at low pressure and travels slowly to the tissues.

In a double circulation the blood returns to the heart after the lungs and is pumped again at high pressure. It therefore reaches the body tissues much more quickly, so oxygen and glucose are delivered faster and a higher rate of respiration is possible.

That last clause is what makes it an advantage: mammals are warm-blooded and need a high metabolic rate.

U2

A baby is born with a hole between the left and right ventricles. Explain why they tire easily.

Show answer

The hole lets oxygenated and deoxygenated blood mix in the ventricles.

Blood leaving the left ventricle for the body therefore carries less oxygen than normal. Less oxygen reaches the respiring tissues, so less aerobic respiration occurs and less energy is released.

More anaerobic respiration takes place instead, producing lactic acid, so the baby tires quickly.

U3

Explain why veins need valves but arteries do not.

Show answer

Blood in arteries is at high pressure because it has just been pumped from the heart. That pressure alone keeps it moving forwards, so there is nothing to stop.

By the time blood reaches the veins it has passed through the capillaries, and the pressure is low. Without help it could flow backwards, particularly when travelling upwards from the legs against gravity. The valves close if blood starts to move the wrong way, so flow stays one-directional.

Veins also rely on the contraction of nearby skeletal muscles to squeeze blood along.

U4

Why does someone infected with measles a second time usually show no symptoms?

Show answer

After the first infection, memory cells specific to the measles antigen remain in the blood.

On the second exposure these memory cells recognise the antigen immediately and produce the specific antibodies much faster and in far greater quantity.

The pathogen is destroyed before it can multiply enough to cause symptoms. This is immunity, and vaccination produces the same state deliberately using a harmless form of the pathogen.

U5

A red blood cell has no nucleus, no mitochondria and a biconcave shape. Explain how each suits its function.

Show answer

No nucleus: leaves more room for haemoglobin, so each cell carries more oxygen.

No mitochondria: the cell cannot respire aerobically, so it does not use up any of the oxygen it is carrying — all of it is delivered to the tissues.

Biconcave disc: gives a large surface area to volume ratio for fast diffusion of oxygen in and out, and makes the cell flexible enough to squeeze through narrow capillaries.

The trade-off: with no nucleus the cell cannot repair itself or divide, which is why red blood cells only last about 120 days.

U6

Explain how a blood clot forms, and give two reasons clotting is important.

Show answer

Platelets arrive at the wound and trigger a series of reactions that convert soluble fibrinogen into insoluble fibrin. The fibrin forms a mesh of fibres across the wound, trapping red blood cells to form a clot, which dries into a scab.

Why it matters: it prevents further blood loss, and it seals the wound so pathogens cannot enter.

04Exam drill

D1

Explain what is meant by a double circulatory system. (2)

Show answer

Blood is pumped to the lungs by one / the right side of the heart 1; and to the body by the other / the left side 1. "Blood passes through the heart twice for every circuit of the body" also scores both.

D2

Explain why the left ventricle has a thicker wall than the right ventricle. (2)

Show answer

The left ventricle pumps blood to the whole body, the right only to the lungs 1; so it must generate a higher pressure, which needs more muscle 1.

D3

Give three structural differences between an artery and a vein. (3)

Show answer

Artery has a thicker / more muscular and elastic wall 1; artery has a narrower lumen 1; veins have valves and arteries do not 1.

D4

Describe two ways a red blood cell is adapted to carry oxygen. (2)

Show answer

It has no nucleus, so there is more room for haemoglobin 1; it is a biconcave disc, giving a large surface area for diffusion 1.

D5

Describe how lymphocytes defend the body against a pathogen. (3)

Show answer

They produce antibodies 1; the antibody is specific / complementary to the antigen on the pathogen's surface 1; the antibodies bind to the antigens, clumping the pathogens so phagocytes can destroy them 1.

D6

Name the blood vessel that carries deoxygenated blood away from the heart, and state where it goes. (2)

Show answer

The pulmonary artery 1; it carries blood from the right ventricle to the lungs 1.

D7

Explain why a capillary wall is only one cell thick. (2)

Show answer

It gives a short diffusion distance 1; so oxygen and glucose can diffuse into the tissues, and carbon dioxide out, quickly 1.

D8

Explain how vaccination gives long-term protection against a disease. (4)

Show answer

A harmless or inactive form of the pathogen, carrying its antigens, is introduced 1; lymphocytes produce antibodies specific to those antigens 1; memory cells are also produced and remain in the blood 1; on later infection antibodies are produced faster and in greater quantity, so symptoms do not develop 1.

D9

State the function of the coronary arteries, and explain the consequence of them becoming blocked. (3)

Show answer

They supply the heart muscle with oxygen and glucose 1; a blockage means the heart muscle receives less oxygen so cannot respire aerobically 1; the muscle cells are damaged or die, causing a heart attack 1.
