02How it actually works

You inherit two alleles of each gene — one from each parent. Which ones you have is your genotype; what shows is your phenotype. A genetic diagram is just a tidy way of listing every combination the parents could pass on, so you can read off the chances.

Genes, alleles and chromosome pairs

Spec 3.19 · 2023 Edexcel Jan 2B Q3(a)

Genes come in different versions called alleles, and those differences are why inherited characteristics differ.

Chromosomes come in pairs — one of each pair from your mother, one from your father. The same gene sits at the same place on both chromosomes of a pair, so you have two copies of every gene. The two copies may be the same allele or different alleles.

A gene is a section of DNA that codes for a protein (module 15). An allele is one version of that gene. The gene for fur colour is one gene; "black" and "agouti" are two alleles of it.

Gametes — sperm and eggs — are made by meiosis, so they carry only one chromosome from each pair, and so only one allele of each gene. At fertilisation, two gametes combine and the pair is restored.

Working out a genotype from a phenotype

Spec 3.20, 3.24 · 2022 Edexcel 1B Q8(a)(i)

A recessive phenotype tells you the genotype; a dominant phenotype does not.

You seePossible genotypesWhy
The recessive characteristicff onlyA single F would have been expressed
The dominant characteristicFF or FfBoth look the same — you need more evidence

The evidence usually comes from relatives:

  • A dominant-looking parent who has a recessive child must be Ff — the child's f came from somewhere.
  • A dominant-looking child of a recessive parent must be Ff — the recessive parent could only give f.

Which allele is dominant? In the 2022 rat question, black × agouti gave all agouti offspring. The offspring must carry both alleles, yet only agouti shows — so agouti is dominant. That was the two-mark answer: agouti, because all the offspring were agouti even though they carried the black allele.

The genetic diagram — four marks

Spec 3.23 · 2023 Y11 mock Q5(a)(iii) · 2022 Edexcel Jan 1B Q9(b)

Every genetic diagram has four marked lines: parent genotypes, gametes, offspring genotypes, and offspring phenotypes with the ratio.

Worked example — the mock's cross. A heterozygous animal with a condition caused by a dominant allele (Hh) is crossed with an unaffected animal (hh):

  1. Parent phenotypes: affected × unaffected
  2. Parent genotypes: Hh × hh 1
  3. Gametes: H and h from the first parent; h and h from the second 1
  4. Offspring genotypes from the Punnett square 1
  5. Offspring phenotypes and ratio: Hh affected, hh unaffected — 1 : 1 1
Gameteshh
HHh — affectedHh — affected
hhh — unaffectedhh — unaffected

Put each parent's gametes along one edge and fill each box with one gamete from the top and one from the side. The boxes are the four equally likely outcomes.

The four-line checklist

Parents: both genotypes written out, e.g. Hh × hh.

Gametes: shown separately — circled, or along the edges of the Punnett square. One letter each.

Offspring: every box filled.

Phenotypes: each genotype labelled with what it looks like, plus the ratio.

Two carriers — the 3 : 1 cross

Spec 3.23, 3.24 · 2024 Edexcel 1B Q8(b) · 2022 Edexcel Jan 1B Q9(b) · 2022 Edexcel 1B Q8(a)(ii)

Two heterozygous parents give a 3 : 1 ratio of dominant to recessive phenotype — a 1 in 4 chance of an affected child for a recessive condition.

Worked example — cystic fibrosis, caused by a recessive allele f. Two unaffected carrier parents:

  1. Parent phenotypes: unaffected × unaffected
  2. Parent genotypes: Ff × Ff
  3. Gametes: F and f from each parent
GametesFf
FFF — unaffectedFf — unaffected (carrier)
fFf — unaffected (carrier)ff — cystic fibrosis
  1. Offspring genotypes: 1 FF : 2 Ff : 1 ff
  2. Offspring phenotypes: 3 unaffected : 1 with cystic fibrosis

The same diagram, with different letters, answered the alkaptonuria question (Aa × Aa) and the second rat cross (Aa × Aa, 3 agouti : 1 black).

✗ Writing the ratio as "1 : 2 : 1" when the question asks for phenotypes.
✓ Genotype ratio 1 FF : 2 Ff : 1 ff; phenotype ratio 3 unaffected : 1 affected. Say which one you are giving.

Two pure-breeding parents

Spec 3.23 · 2023 Edexcel 1B Q6(a)

Crossing a homozygous dominant with a homozygous recessive gives offspring that are all heterozygous, all with the dominant phenotype.

Worked example — pea plants. Axial flowers (A) are dominant to terminal flowers (a).

  1. Parent genotypes: AA (axial) × aa (terminal)
  2. Gametes: all A from one parent; all a from the other
  3. Offspring: all Aa — 100% axial
Gametesaa
AAa — axialAa — axial
AAa — axialAa — axial

If those Aa offspring are then crossed with each other, you are back to the 3 : 1 cross: the recessive characteristic reappears in a quarter of the next generation. The allele was hidden, not lost.

Probability from a cross

Give the answer in the form the question asks for — a fraction, a decimal, a percentage or "1 in 4". A ratio like 1 : 3 is not a probability: 1 : 3 means a probability of 1/4, not 1/3.

Every fertilisation is independent. If a couple's first child has cystic fibrosis, the chance for their second child is still 0.25 — the gametes have no memory.

The "and" rule — boy or girl as well

Spec 3.25, 3.27 · 2022 Edexcel 1B Q8(a)(iii) · 2022 Edexcel Jan 1B Q9(b)(ii) · 2023 Edexcel Jan 2B Q3(b)(ii)

To find the chance of two separate things both happening, multiply their probabilities.

Sex is decided separately from the gene in the cross, and the chance of a boy is always 1/2. So:

Probability of two independent outcomesP(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)e.g. P(male and unaffected) = P(male) × P(unaffected)
Real questionWorkingAnswer
Rats, Aa × Aa: male and agouti1/2 × 3/43/8 = 0.375
Alkaptonuria, Aa × Aa: male and no condition1/2 × 3/43/8 = 0.375
Roan cattle, CRCW × CRCW: male and roan1/2 × 2/41/4 = 0.25

The same rule works for two children: the chance that two children of Ff × Ff parents both have cystic fibrosis is 1/4 × 1/4 = 1/16.

✗ "Male and agouti: 1/2 + 3/4" — adding gives more than 1, which is impossible.
✓ "1/2 × 3/4 = 0.375." "And" means multiply. The answer must be smaller than either chance on its own.

Why real ratios are never exact

Spec 3.25 · 2023 Edexcel 1B Q6(b)

Ratios from a cross are probabilities, so real numbers only come close — and the bigger the sample, the closer they get.

Worked, from the 2023 paper: 858 pea plants, 608 axial.

  1. Terminal = 858 − 608 = 250
  2. Ratio = 608 ÷ 250 = 2.43 : 1 — close to, but not, 3 : 1

Why not exactly 3 : 1? The scheme's points: the expected ratio is 3 : 1; which gametes fuse at fertilisation is random, so it is down to chance; and some plants of one type may not have survived or germinated. Four kittens from Hh × hh will not necessarily be two and two.

Finding an unknown genotype

Spec 3.23, 3.24

To find out whether a dominant-looking organism is homozygous or heterozygous, cross it with a homozygous recessive and look at the offspring.

If the unknown parent isCrossOffspring
Homozygous — BBBB × bbAll show the dominant characteristic (all Bb)
Heterozygous — BbBb × bbAbout half show the recessive characteristic (1 Bb : 1 bb)

A single recessive offspring proves the parent is Bb. But all-dominant offspring from a small litter only suggest BB — with a 1/2 chance each time, a heterozygous parent could produce a few dominant offspring in a row by chance.

Codominance — both alleles show

Spec 3.21B · 2023 Edexcel Jan 2B Q3(b) · 2022 Edexcel Jan 2B Q1(b)

With codominant alleles, both are expressed in the heterozygote, so it has a third phenotype of its own.

Codominant alleles are written as a capital letter for the gene with a superscript for each allele. Cattle hair: CR (red) and CW (white).

GenotypePhenotype

Ratio 1 red : 2 roan : 1 white. With codominance the genotype ratio and the phenotype ratio are the same, because every genotype looks different.

Blood groups use three alleles: IA and IB are codominant, and IO is recessive to both. IAIO (group A) × IBIO (group B):

GametesIBIO

All four blood groups are possible, each with a probability of 1/4 — the answer to the 2022 multiple-choice question.

Sex determination

Spec 3.26, 3.27 · 2023 Edexcel Jan 1B Q5

Sex is controlled by one pair of chromosomes: XX in a female and XY in a male.

Humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes; the 23rd pair are the sex chromosomes. In a karyotype — a photo of the chromosomes arranged in pairs — a male is recognised because his 23rd pair is not matched: one large X and one small Y.

  1. Parent phenotypes: female × male
  2. Parent genotypes: XX × XY
  3. Gametes: all eggs carry X; half the sperm carry X, half carry Y
GametesX (sperm)Y (sperm)
X (egg)XX — femaleXY — male
X (egg)XX — femaleXY — male
  1. Offspring: 1 female : 1 male — a probability of 0.5 for each, at every fertilisation

The sex of the child is decided by which sperm fertilises the egg, so it is the father's gamete that decides.

Family pedigrees

Spec 3.24 · 2024 Edexcel 1B Q8(b)(i) · 2023 Y11 mock Q5(b)

In a pedigree, two unaffected parents with an affected child prove the condition is recessive and both parents are carriers.

A pedigree is a family tree. Squares are males, circles are females, and shaded shapes are affected. A horizontal line joins parents; a vertical line drops to their children. Reading one for a recessive condition like cystic fibrosis:

Person in the pedigreeGenotypeHow you know
Anyone affectedffRecessive phenotype — only one possible genotype
Unaffected parent of an affected childFfMust have passed on an f, but shows the dominant phenotype
Unaffected child of an affected parentFfReceived f from the affected parent, but is unaffected
Unaffected, with no affected parents or childrenFF or FfNot enough information — say both

For a dominant condition the clue is the reverse: two affected parents with an unaffected child — both parents must be heterozygous.

This is also why breeders worry about recessive alleles, which the Year 11 mock rewarded. A dominant harmful allele always shows, so affected animals can be spotted and not bred from. A recessive allele hides in heterozygous carriers who look normal, so it is passed on unnoticed.

Polygenic inheritance

Spec 3.22 · 2023 Edexcel Jan 2B Q3(c)(i) · 2022 Edexcel 1B Q8(b)(i)

Most characteristics are controlled by many genes, not one — this is polygenic inheritance.

A single-gene characteristic, like roan hair or cystic fibrosis, falls into a few clear categories. A polygenic one, like height or body mass, has a smooth range of values, because each of several genes adds a little, and the environment — nutrition, for example — adds more on top.

Why height varies more than hair colour — two marks

1. Height is polygenic — controlled by many genes 1

2. The environment, such as nutrition, also affects height 1

The scheme ignored "multiple alleles". Polygenic means many genes, not many versions of one gene.

04Understanding it

U1

Two brown-eyed parents have a blue-eyed child. Brown is dominant (B). Explain, with genotypes, and give the chance their next child has blue eyes.

Show answer

A blue-eyed child must be bb, so each parent passed on a b. Both parents show brown, so each must also carry a B: both are Bb.

Bb × Bb gives BB, Bb, Bb, bb — so the chance of blue eyes is 1 in 4, 0.25, for every child.

U2

Why is it easier for breeders to remove a dominant harmful allele than a recessive one?

Show answer

A dominant allele is always expressed, so every animal carrying it shows the condition and can be stopped from breeding.

A recessive allele is hidden in heterozygous animals, which look normal. They are bred from without anyone knowing, so the allele keeps being passed on.

U3

Black rats are crossed with agouti rats. Every offspring is agouti. Which allele is dominant, and what are the genotypes of the offspring?

Show answer

Agouti is dominant. Every offspring received one allele from the black parent, so each one carries the black allele — yet none of them is black. The black allele is being hidden, so it is recessive.

The offspring are all heterozygous, Aa. (The parents were most likely AA × aa: if the agouti parent had been Aa, about half the offspring would have been black.)

U4

Two carriers of cystic fibrosis have three unaffected children. A friend says their fourth child is "due" to have it. Explain why the friend is wrong.

Show answer

Each fertilisation is independent. Which sperm meets which egg is random each time, and the earlier children do not change the gametes.

The chance for the fourth child is exactly what it was for the first: 1/4 = 0.25. The 3 : 1 ratio describes the probability for each child, not a pattern that must be completed.

U5

Two unaffected parents who are both carriers of alkaptonuria (Aa) are expecting a child. Why is the probability that the child is a boy without the condition 0.375, not 0.75?

Show answer

0.75 is the chance of being unaffected — three of the four boxes in Aa × Aa. But the question asks for two things at once: unaffected and male.

Sex is decided separately, with a chance of 1/2. Both must happen, so multiply: 3/4 × 1/2 = 3/8 = 0.375. Half of the unaffected children are expected to be girls, so the answer must be smaller than 0.75.

U6

A roan bull is crossed with a white cow. Why can you predict the calves' phenotypes directly from their genotypes, which you cannot do with a dominant and recessive allele?

Show answer

The alleles are codominant, so the heterozygote has its own phenotype. CRCW × CWCW gives CRCW (roan) and CWCW (white) in a 1 : 1 ratio.

Every genotype looks different, so the phenotype tells you the genotype. With a dominant allele, FF and Ff look the same, so you cannot tell them apart by looking.

U7

A student crosses two heterozygous pea plants and gets 608 tall and 250 short. She says her result disproves the 3 : 1 rule. Is she right?

Show answer

No. 608 ÷ 250 = 2.4 : 1, which is close to 3 : 1. The 3 : 1 ratio is a probability, not a guarantee.

Which gametes fuse at fertilisation is random, so real results vary around the expected ratio. Some seeds may also have failed to germinate. A bigger sample would be expected to come closer to 3 : 1.

U8

Hair colour in cattle comes in three clear types, but height varies smoothly from short to tall. Explain the difference.

Show answer

Hair colour is controlled by one gene with two alleles, so there are only three genotypes and three phenotypes.

Height is polygenic — controlled by many genes, each adding a little — so there are many combinations of alleles and a continuous range of heights. The environment also affects height, for example how well the animal is fed, which spreads the values even more.
