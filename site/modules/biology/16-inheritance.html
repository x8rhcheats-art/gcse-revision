01How it actually works

You inherit two alleles of each gene — one from each parent. Which ones you have is your genotype; what shows is your phenotype.

The genetic cross — four marks

Spec 3.23, 3.24

A heterozygous long-haired cat (Hh) crossed with a short-haired cat (hh), where long hair is dominant:

  1. Parent genotypes: Hh × hh
  2. Gametes: H and h from one; h and h from the other
  3. Offspring from a Punnett square: Hh, Hh, hh, hh
  4. Ratio and phenotypes: 1 : 1 — half long-haired, half short-haired

A Hh × Hh cross gives 3 : 1 dominant to recessive — HH, Hh, Hh, hh. The probability of a recessive offspring is 0.25, or 25%.

Ratios are probabilities, not guarantees. Four kittens from Hh × hh will not necessarily be two and two.

Sex determination

Spec 3.25

The 23rd pair are the sex chromosomes. Females are XX; males are XY. Eggs always carry X; sperm carry X or Y, so the father's sperm decides the sex. The chance of a boy is 50% every time.

Pedigree diagrams

Spec 3.26

A family tree shows who has a condition. The key clue for a recessive allele: two unaffected parents have an affected child — so both parents must be heterozygous carriers.

Why breeders worry about recessive alleles

A dominant harmful allele always shows, so affected animals can be spotted and not bred from. A recessive allele hides in heterozygous carriers who look normal, so it can spread through a population unnoticed — the reasoning the Year 11 mock rewarded.

03Understanding it

U1

Two brown-eyed parents have a blue-eyed child. Brown is dominant (B). Explain, with genotypes, and give the chance their next child has blue eyes.

Show answer

A blue-eyed child must be bb, so each parent passed on a b. Both parents show brown, so each must also carry a B: both are Bb.

Bb × Bb gives BB, Bb, Bb, bb — so the chance of blue eyes is 1 in 4, 0.25, for every child.

U2

Why is it easier for breeders to remove a dominant harmful allele than a recessive one?

Show answer

A dominant allele is always expressed, so every animal carrying it shows the condition and can be stopped from breeding.

A recessive allele is hidden in heterozygous animals, which look normal. They are bred from without anyone knowing, so the allele keeps being passed on.

Show answer

Females are XX so all eggs carry X 1; males are XY so half of sperm carry X and half carry Y 1; so half of fertilisations give XY, male 1.
