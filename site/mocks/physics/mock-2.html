Formulae provided

E = I × V × tE = energy transferred (J) · I = current (A) · V = voltage (V) · t = time (s — convert minutes first)
f = 1 ÷ Tf = frequency (Hz) · T = time period, the time for one complete wave (s)
P = W ÷ tP = power (W, watts) · W = work done, or energy transferred (J) · t = time taken (s) — one watt is one joule per second
v² = u² + (2 × a × s)v = final speed (m/s) · u = initial speed (m/s) · a = acceleration (m/s²) · s = distance moved (m)
p₁V₁ = p₂V₂p₁, V₁ = pressure and volume before · p₂, V₂ = after · temperature constant · any units, provided both sides match
p₁ ÷ T₁ = p₂ ÷ T₂p₁, T₁ = pressure and temperature before · p₂, T₂ = after · pressure in Pa or kPa · T must be in kelvin (K), never °C · volume must stay constant
ΔQ = m × c × ΔTΔQ = energy transferred (J) · m = mass (kg — convert grams first) · c = specific heat capacity (J/kg °C) · ΔT = temperature change (°C)
v = (2 × π × r) ÷ Tv = orbital speed (m/s) · r = orbital radius (m) · T = time for one full orbit (s)

Question 1Energy stores and transfers (7 marks)

A battery-powered drill is used to drill a hole in a wooden plank.

(a) State the energy store that decreases in the battery. (1)

(b) State the main method by which energy is transferred from the drill bit to the wood. (1)

(c) The drill is supplied with 4000 J of energy. 1200 J is transferred usefully.

(i) State the formula for efficiency. (1)

(ii) Calculate the efficiency of the drill as a percentage. (2)

(iii) Draw a labelled Sankey diagram for this energy transfer. Include a scale. (2)

Question 2Density and pressure (10 marks)

A student investigates a small irregular stone.

(a) Describe how the student could find the volume of the stone using a measuring cylinder. (3)

(b) The stone has a mass of 78 g and a volume of 30 cm³. Calculate its density in g/cm³, giving your answer to 2 significant figures. (3)

(c) The stone is dropped into a lake and sinks to a depth of 18 m. The density of the lake water is 1000 kg/m³.

Calculate the pressure due to the water at that depth. (3)

(d) State what happens to the pressure on the stone as it sinks deeper, and why. (1)

Question 3Circuits (12 marks)

A circuit contains a thermistor connected in series with a 220 Ω fixed resistor and a 6.0 V battery.

(a) Add to a circuit diagram an instrument to measure the current in the circuit, and state where it must be placed. (2)

(b) At room temperature the current in the circuit is 12 mA.

(i) State the formula linking voltage, current and resistance. (1)

(ii) Calculate the voltage across the 220 Ω resistor. (3)

(iii) Calculate the resistance of the thermistor. (3)

(c) The thermistor is warmed. Describe and explain what happens to the current in the circuit. (3)

Question 4Radioactivity (12 marks)

A student uses a Geiger–Müller detector to investigate a radioactive source.

(a) Before introducing the source, the detector records 24 counts per minute. Explain what this reading is, and give two sources of it. (3)

(b) With the source in place the detector reads 424 counts per minute. The reading is unchanged when a sheet of paper is placed between the source and the detector, but falls to 24 counts per minute when a 5 mm aluminium sheet is used.

Identify the type of radiation emitted and justify your answer. (3)

(c) State what happens to the atomic number and mass number of a nucleus when it emits this type of radiation. (2)

(d) The corrected count rate falls to 50 counts per minute after 96 minutes. Determine the half-life of the source. (3)

(e) Explain why radioactive decay is described as a random process. (1)

Question 6Waves (9 marks)

(a) Describe the difference between the motion of particles in a transverse wave and a longitudinal wave. (2)

(b) A police car with its siren on drives past a stationary pedestrian.

Explain what the pedestrian hears as the car approaches and then recedes, and why. (4)

(c) An oscilloscope shows one complete wave spanning 3 divisions. The time base is 2 ms per division.

(i) Determine the time period. (1)

(ii) Calculate the frequency. (2)

Question 7Specific heat capacity (11 marks)

A student determines the specific heat capacity of a metal block using an immersion heater of power 60 W.

(a) Describe the investigation the student should carry out. (5)

(b) The block has a mass of 1.2 kg. The heater is switched on for 5.0 minutes and the temperature rises by 22 °C.

Calculate the specific heat capacity of the metal. (4)

(c) The student's value is higher than the accepted value for this metal. Suggest why. (2)

Question 8Static electricity (8 marks)

A student rubs a polythene rod with a dry cloth. The rod becomes negatively charged.

(a) Explain, in terms of electrons, how the rod becomes negatively charged. (2)

(b) The charged rod is held near a thin stream of water flowing from a tap. The stream bends towards the rod.

Explain why, given that the water is uncharged. (3)

(c) The rod is touched against the cap of a gold-leaf electroscope. Describe and explain what happens to the gold leaf. (3)

Question 9Gases and change of state (8 marks)

A sealed syringe contains 60 cm³ of air at a pressure of 100 kPa. The plunger is pushed in until the volume is 25 cm³. The temperature does not change.

(a) Calculate the new pressure. (3)

(b) Explain, in terms of particles, why the pressure changed. (2)

(c) A substance is heated steadily from solid to liquid. Its temperature–time graph shows a horizontal section.

Explain what is happening to the particles during this horizontal section. (3)
