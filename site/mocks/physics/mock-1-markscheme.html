Q1Electromagnetic spectrum (6) → Module 4

(a)(i) Microwaves 1

(a)(ii) X-rays 1

(b)(i) Heaters, or night vision equipment 1

(b)(ii) Fluorescent lamps 1

(c) Any two 11: all transverse; all transfer energy; all travel at the same speed in a vacuum; all can be reflected and refracted.

Vague uses score zero. "Warming things up" is not "heaters". "Tanning" is not "fluorescent lamps". Use the specification wording — see the table in Module 4.

Q2Static electricity (9) → Module 5

(a) Friction between the fuel and the pipe 1; electrons are transferred 1; the fuel gains or loses electrons and so becomes charged, while the pipe gains the opposite charge 1

(b) Charge builds up until a spark jumps 1; the spark could ignite the fuel vapour, causing a fire or explosion 1; use an earthing or bonding wire so electrons flow to earth and charge cannot accumulate 1

(c)(i) Q = I × t 1

(c)(ii) I = (4.5 × 10⁻⁷) ÷ 90 1 = 5.0 × 10⁻⁹ A 1

For the last mark of (b), the electrons must be stated to flow to earth. "It's earthed" restates the situation and scores nothing.

Q3Moments and particles (8) → Module 1 and Module 2

(a)(i) moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot 1

(a)(ii) 24 cm = 0.24 m 1; 85 × 0.24 1 = 20.4 N m 1

(a)(iii) Use a longer spanner, increasing the perpendicular distance from the pivot 1

(b) Particles are close together or touching 1; randomly arranged, with no fixed pattern 1; they slide, flow or move past one another 1

Answer required in N m, distance given in cm. If the answer is 2040, code this as a unit-conversion error, not a physics error.

For (b), "the particles move" without "past each other" scores nothing for motion.

Q4Waves and sound (10) → Module 4

(a) 30 000 complete waves pass a point 1 each second 1

(b)(i) v = f × λ 1

(b)(ii) 30 kHz = 30 000 Hz 1; λ = 1500 ÷ 30 000 1 = 0.05 m 1

(c) Total distance = 1500 × 0.16 = 240 m 11; this is there and back, so depth = 240 ÷ 2 = 120 m 1

(d) Any one 1: sound is longitudinal, light is transverse; sound needs a medium, light does not; light is part of the EM spectrum, sound is not.

Part (c) is the halving question. Failing to divide by two is the single most common error in echo and sonar problems.

Q5Electrical circuits (13) → Module 3

(a) Cell, variable resistor, ammeter and lamp in series in one loop 1; voltmeter in parallel across the lamp only 1; all symbols correct 1; circuit fully closed with no gaps 1

(b) As current increases the filament heats up 1; the metal ions vibrate more so electrons collide with them more often 1; resistance therefore increases, so current rises less for each extra volt 1

(c)(i) V = I × R 1

(c)(ii) Each resistor has the full 3.0 V across it 1; I₁ = 3.0 ÷ 15 = 0.20 A and I₂ = 3.0 ÷ 10 = 0.30 A 1; total = 0.50 A 1

(c)(iii) Series total resistance = 25 Ω, so I = 3.0 ÷ 25 = 0.12 A 1; this is much smaller than 0.50 A, because series resistance is higher than parallel 1

Part (c)(iii) says comment. Calculations alone cap the score. A sentence comparing the two values is required — this is exactly where the 2025 cohort lost marks.

Q6Gases and pressure (8) → Module 2

(a) 17 + 273 = 290 K 1

(b) Particles move quickly and randomly in all directions 1; they collide with the walls of the cylinder 1; each collision exerts a force, and pressure = force ÷ area 1

(c) p₁ ÷ T₁ = p₂ ÷ T₂ 1; 120 ÷ 290 = p₂ ÷ 348 1; p₂ = 144 kPa 1

(d) Higher temperature means greater average kinetic energy, so particles hit the walls more often and harder 1

In (b), "particles" or "molecules" must be named. "They hit the walls" was refused across the whole 2025 year group.

Q7Thermal energy (11) → Module 2

(a) ΔT = 95 − 15 = 80 °C 1; ΔQ = 8.0 × 4200 × 80 1 = 2 688 000 J, or 2.688 × 10⁶ J 1

(b) P = I × V = 12 × 230 = 2760 W 11; t = E ÷ P = 2 688 000 ÷ 2760 1 = 974 s (about 16 minutes) 1

(c) Some energy is transferred to the thermal store of the surroundings and to the urn itself 1; so not all the electrical energy reaches the water, and more time is needed 1

(d) The oil reaches a higher temperature 1; because a lower specific heat capacity means less energy is needed per kilogram for each degree of temperature rise 1

Q8Practical investigation (11) → Module 7

(a)(i) Force applied, or weight added 1

(a)(ii) Extension of the spring 1

(a)(iii) Any one 1: the same spring used throughout; the same temperature; the ruler kept vertical.

(b) Repeat each measurement at least three times 1; calculate a mean extension for each force, discarding anomalies 1

(c)(i) Sensible scale using at least half the grid, axes labelled with quantity and unit 1; all points plotted correctly with clear crosses 1; a straight line of best fit through the origin, ignoring the anomaly 1

(c)(ii) The point (5.0, 11.5) 1; repeat the measurement at 5.0 N, and exclude it from the line of best fit if it cannot be reproduced 1

(c)(iii) Both variables are continuous, so a line graph shows the trend and allows intermediate values to be read 1

Expected extension at 5.0 N is about 10 cm, following the pattern of roughly 2 cm per newton. 11.5 breaks it. Note the point at 6.0 N returns to the trend, confirming 5.0 N is the outlier.

Use the word continuous in (c)(iii). It's what the mark scheme wants.

Q9Radioactivity (11) → Module 6

(a) Becquerel, Bq 1

(b) The time taken for the activity of a sample to halve 1, or for half the unstable nuclei in a sample to decay 1

(c) 24 ÷ 6 = 4 half-lives 1; 12 000 → 6000 → 3000 → 1500 → 750 1 = 750 Bq 1

(d) Gamma is highly penetrating so it can pass out of the body to be detected 1; alpha would be absorbed within a few centimetres of tissue and never reach the detector 1; alpha is strongly ionising so it would cause serious damage to cells inside the body 1

(e) Mass number 14 1; atomic number 7 1

In (b), "the time for a nucleus to halve" scores zero. It must be the sample, or the number of nuclei.

Q10Forces, motion and energy (13) → Module 1 and Module 8

(a) W = m × g = 75 × 10 1 = 750 N 1

(b)(i) 750 − 300 1 = 450 N downwards 1

(b)(ii) a = F ÷ m = 450 ÷ 75 1 = 6.0 m/s² 1

(c) Initially weight is greater than air resistance, so there is a resultant downward force and she accelerates 1; as her speed increases, air resistance increases 1; the resultant force therefore decreases, so her acceleration decreases 1; when air resistance equals weight the resultant force is zero and she falls at constant velocity 1

(d) Her gravitational potential energy decreases 1; her kinetic energy is unchanged because her speed is constant 1; so all of the GPE lost is dissipated to the thermal store of the surrounding air by air resistance 1

Part (c) says "in terms of forces". Any mention of GPE or KE here is ignored. Part (d) is the opposite — it's an energy question and forces won't earn marks. Read which one you're being asked for.

Reading the result

Any question scoring below 50% points at the linked module. Redo that module's cheat sheet and drill section before Mock 2.

Also count separately how many marks were lost to each of: unit conversion, significant figures or standard form, not stating a formula that was asked for, and answering with the wrong concept (energy when forces were asked for, or the reverse). Those four categories are usually worth ten to fifteen marks on a paper like this, and none of them require learning any new physics to fix.
