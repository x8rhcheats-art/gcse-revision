Q1Energy stores and transfers (7) → Module 8

(a) Chemical 1

(b) Mechanically, or by working 1

(c)(i) efficiency = (useful energy output ÷ total energy input) × 100 1

(c)(ii) 1200 ÷ 4000 1 = 0.30 = 30% 1

(c)(iii) Joined-up arrow diagram, input arrow labelled "chemical, 4000 J", useful output "kinetic, 1200 J" horizontal, waste "thermal, 2800 J" branching away 1; widths to a stated scale, with the waste arrow roughly 2⅓ times the width of the useful output 1

"Useful energy ÷ total energy" without the words output and input was explicitly refused by the department. Sankey: no input arrow means no mark, and "transferred to surroundings" must say thermal or heat.

Q2Density and pressure (10) → Module 2

(a) Part-fill the measuring cylinder with water and record the volume 1; lower the stone in fully, using a thread if needed 1; record the new volume and subtract to find the volume of the stone 1

(b) ρ = m ÷ V 1 = 78 ÷ 30 = 2.6 1 = 2.6 g/cm³ 1

(c) ΔP = h × g × ρ 1 = 18 × 10 × 1000 1 = 180 000 Pa, or 180 kPa 1

(d) Pressure increases, because there is a greater height of water above it 1

Q3Circuits (12) → Module 3

(a) An ammeter 1; connected in series, anywhere in the single loop 1

(b)(i) V = I × R 1

(b)(ii) 12 mA = 0.012 A 1; V = 0.012 × 220 1 = 2.64 V 1

(b)(iii) Voltage across thermistor = 6.0 − 2.64 = 3.36 V 1; R = V ÷ I = 3.36 ÷ 0.012 1 = 280 Ω 1

(c) The resistance of the thermistor decreases as temperature increases 1; so the total resistance of the circuit decreases 1; the current therefore increases 1

Milliamps. If (b)(ii) came out as 2640 V, that's the conversion, not the physics.

In (b)(iii) the series voltages must add to 6.0 V — that relationship is the whole method.

Q4Radioactivity (12) → Module 6

(a) Background radiation — ionising radiation present all the time, even with no source being used 1; any two named sources 11: rocks and soil, radon gas, cosmic rays from space, food and drink, medical procedures.

(b) Beta 1; paper does not reduce the count, so it is not alpha 1; 5 mm of aluminium reduces it to the background level, so it is not gamma, which would penetrate 1

(c) Mass number unchanged 1; atomic number increases by 1 1

(d) Corrected initial count = 424 − 24 = 400 1; 400 → 200 → 100 → 50 is 3 half-lives 1; 96 ÷ 3 = 32 minutes 1

(e) It is impossible to predict when any individual nucleus will decay 1

Part (d) requires subtracting background first. Working from 424 gives a wrong number of half-lives and loses all three marks.

Q6Waves (9) → Module 4

(a) Transverse: particles vibrate perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer 1; longitudinal: particles vibrate parallel to it 1

(b) Approaching, the waves are squashed together so the wavelength is shorter 1; the wave speed is unchanged, so from v = fλ the observed frequency is higher and the pitch sounds higher 1; receding, the waves are stretched so the wavelength is longer 1; the observed frequency is lower and the pitch drops 1

(c)(i) 3 × 2 ms = 6 ms, or 0.006 s 1

(c)(ii) f = 1 ÷ 0.006 1 = 167 Hz 1

In (b), do not write that the sound travels faster or slower. The speed is fixed by the air.

Q7Specific heat capacity (11) → Module 2 and Module 7

(a) Any five 1 each: measure the mass of the block on a balance; insert the heater and thermometer into the holes; record the starting temperature; switch on the heater and start the stopwatch at the same moment; after a measured time record the highest temperature; calculate energy from power × time; insulate the block with lagging to reduce energy transfer to the surroundings.

(b) E = P × t = 60 × 300 = 18 000 J 11

c = E ÷ (m × ΔT) = 18 000 ÷ (1.2 × 22) 1 = 682 J/kg °C 1

(c) Some energy is transferred to the surroundings rather than to the block 1; so the energy actually used to raise the block's temperature was less than the value used in the calculation, making the calculated c too large 1

5.0 minutes is 300 seconds. Using 5 gives an answer 60 times too small.

Q8Static electricity (8) → Module 5

(a) Friction transfers electrons 1; electrons move from the cloth onto the rod, so the rod gains electrons 1

(b) The negative rod repels electrons in the water, pushing them to the far side of the stream 1; the near side of the stream is left with an induced positive charge 1; unlike charges attract, so the stream is attracted and bends towards the rod 1

(c) The leaf rises away from the metal rod 1; charge from the rod spreads through the metal cap and stem to the leaf 1; the stem and leaf now carry like charges, which repel 1

In (b), any suggestion that the water is charged, or that it is repelled, loses marks. The department was emphatic about this.

Q9Gases and change of state (8) → Module 2

(a) p₁V₁ = p₂V₂ 1; 100 × 60 = p₂ × 25 1; p₂ = 240 kPa 1

(b) The same number of particles now occupy a smaller volume 1; so they collide with the walls more frequently, increasing the total force per unit area and therefore the pressure 1

(c) The energy supplied is used to break the bonds or overcome the forces of attraction between particles 1; rather than increasing their kinetic energy 1; so the temperature stays constant while the substance changes state 1

In (b), the particles do not speed up — the temperature is constant. Only the frequency of collisions changes. Saying they move faster is a common and costly error.

What to do with this on 30 August

Do not start new topics. Take every mark lost, write it as a single line on one side of A4, and read that sheet on the 1st and 2nd. Most of what's left at this stage will be wording and unit errors, and those respond to rereading in a way that new content does not.

If a whole question collapsed, reread only that module's cheat sheet section — not the drills.
