Formulae provided

E = I × V × tE = energy transferred (J) · I = current (A) · V = voltage (V) · t = time (s — convert minutes first)
f = 1 ÷ Tf = frequency (Hz) · T = time period, the time for one complete wave (s)
P = W ÷ tP = power (W, watts) · W = work done, or energy transferred (J) · t = time taken (s) — one watt is one joule per second
v² = u² + (2 × a × s)v = final speed (m/s) · u = initial speed (m/s) · a = acceleration (m/s²) · s = distance moved (m)
p₁V₁ = p₂V₂p₁, V₁ = pressure and volume before · p₂, V₂ = after · temperature constant · any units, provided both sides match
p₁ ÷ T₁ = p₂ ÷ T₂p₁, T₁ = pressure and temperature before · p₂, T₂ = after · pressure in Pa or kPa · T must be in kelvin (K), never °C · volume must stay constant
ΔQ = m × c × ΔTΔQ = energy transferred (J) · m = mass (kg — convert grams first) · c = specific heat capacity (J/kg °C) · ΔT = temperature change (°C)
v = (2 × π × r) ÷ Tv = orbital speed (m/s) · r = orbital radius (m) · T = time for one full orbit (s)

Question 1Electromagnetic spectrum (6 marks)

The electromagnetic spectrum is arranged here in order of increasing frequency:

radio · __A__ · infrared · visible light · ultraviolet · __B__ · gamma

(a) (i) Name the part labelled A. (1)

(a) (ii) Name the part labelled B. (1)

(b) (i) Give one use of infrared radiation. (1)

(b) (ii) Give one use of ultraviolet radiation. (1)

(c) State two properties that all electromagnetic waves have in common. (2)

Question 2Static electricity (9 marks)

A worker fills a plastic container with fuel. The fuel flows through a plastic pipe.

(a) Explain how the fuel becomes electrostatically charged as it flows through the pipe. (3)

(b) Explain why this is dangerous, and describe one way the danger can be reduced. (3)

(c) A charge of 4.5 × 10⁻⁷ C builds up over a period of 90 seconds.

(i) State the formula linking charge, current and time. (1)

(ii) Calculate the mean charging current. Give your answer in standard form. (2)

Question 3Moments and particles (8 marks)

A student uses a spanner to loosen a nut. They apply a force of 85 N at a perpendicular distance of 24 cm from the centre of the nut.

(a) (i) State the formula linking moment, force and perpendicular distance from the pivot. (1)

(a) (ii) Calculate the moment of the force. Give your answer in N m. (3)

(a) (iii) The student cannot loosen the nut. Suggest one way they could increase the moment without applying a larger force. (1)

(b) The nut is lubricated with oil, which is a liquid.

Describe the arrangement and the motion of the particles in a liquid. (3)

Question 4Waves and sound (10 marks)

A ship uses sonar to measure the depth of the sea. It emits a pulse of sound at a frequency of 30 kHz. Sound travels at 1500 m/s in sea water.

(a) Explain what is meant by a frequency of 30 kHz. (2)

(b) (i) State the formula linking wave speed, frequency and wavelength. (1)

(b) (ii) Calculate the wavelength of the sound in sea water. (3)

(c) The pulse returns to the ship 0.16 s after it is emitted. Calculate the depth of the sea beneath the ship. (3)

(d) State one difference between a sound wave and a light wave, other than their speed. (1)

Question 5Electrical circuits (13 marks)

A student investigates a filament lamp.

(a) The student has a filament lamp, a cell, a variable resistor, an ammeter, a voltmeter and connecting wires.

Draw a circuit diagram the student could use to investigate how the current in the lamp varies with the voltage across it. (4)

(b) The graph of the student's results is a curve, becoming less steep as voltage increases.

Explain the shape of this graph. (3)

(c) In a second circuit, a 15 Ω resistor and a 10 Ω resistor are connected in parallel across a 3.0 V battery.

(i) State the formula linking voltage, current and resistance. (1)

(ii) Calculate the current supplied by the battery. (3)

(iii) The two resistors are then reconnected in series across the same battery. Comment on how the current supplied by the battery changes. Use calculations to support your answer. (2)

Question 6Gases and pressure (8 marks)

A cylinder of gas is used in a laboratory.

(a) The gas is at a temperature of 17 °C. Convert this to kelvin. (1)

(b) Explain, in terms of the particles, how the gas exerts a pressure on the walls of the cylinder. (3)

(c) The gas in the sealed cylinder has a pressure of 120 kPa at 290 K. The cylinder is left in the sun and warms to 348 K. The volume of the cylinder does not change.

Calculate the new pressure of the gas. (3)

(d) Explain, in terms of particles, why the pressure changed. (1)

Question 7Thermal energy (11 marks)

A technician heats water in an electric urn.

(a) The urn heats 8.0 kg of water from 15 °C to 95 °C.

Calculate the energy transferred to the water. [specific heat capacity of water = 4200 J/kg °C] (3)

(b) The heating element operates at 230 V and draws a current of 12 A. Calculate the time taken to supply this energy, assuming all of it reaches the water. (4)

(c) In practice the urn takes longer than this. Explain why. (2)

(d) The same energy is supplied to 8.0 kg of cooking oil, which has a lower specific heat capacity than water. State and explain what happens to the temperature of the oil compared with the water. (2)
